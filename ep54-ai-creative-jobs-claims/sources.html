Verification note: each source is marked by what it is: [primary] (the original data, filing, ruling, law, report or study), [secondary] (reporting on it, or a relay), [company claim] / [company rule] (a company speaking about itself), [union], [trade body survey] / [advocacy survey] / [advocacy] (a body with a stake), [commissioned report], or [our arithmetic]. Prefer primary for anything quoted on screen. Every source relied on was downloaded and saved with its text extracted, in the show's evidence archive (not part of the published bundle). Where the saved copy did not record the page's own address, the entry says "link not recorded in the saved copy" and gives the publisher, title and identifier instead: find it by title, and never cite a guessed link. Links built only from a published identifier (an arXiv number, a DOI, a PubMed or FRED series id, an SSRN abstract number) are the identifier's own address. Several sites refused automated fetching (bls.gov, sec.gov, sagaftra.org, some state legislatures, congress.gov); those pages were read from web.archive.org copies, through a text-reader proxy that returns the site's own text, or in the desktop app's built-in browser. The engine's machine dossier (deep-research-v3, run wf_e2ba4017-c41), the peg hunt's draft and the earlier episode's files (ep49) are not sources: they were read as lists of claims to test.
