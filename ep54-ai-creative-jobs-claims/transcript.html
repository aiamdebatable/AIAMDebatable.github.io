Cold open

NarratorYou've probably seen the joke. Why is AI doing the art, while I'm still doing the dishes?

NarratorUnder it is a real worry, for anyone who makes things for a living.

NarratorAnd a pile of claims about it. Some are true. Some are a forecast, a survey, or a company's own story, dressed up as a fact.

NarratorIn October twenty twenty-six, Getty Images, the biggest stock-photo company in the world, went into bankruptcy court in Delaware. Online, people said AI did it.

NarratorSo we're taking the claims people repeat most, one at a time, and stamping each one. True. Partly true. Not measured. Or false.

Claim 1: did AI bankrupt Getty Images?

ErinOkay, Raj. Did AI bankrupt Getty?

RajGetty's own filing says no. That one's false.

RajAnd false has a strict meaning today. It's only for a claim the record contradicts. Not one we just couldn't prove.

ErinThen what does the filing say did it?

RajThree things it calls non-operational. Court judgments in lawsuits over old warrants. A merger with Shutterstock that Getty abandoned, after Britain's competition regulator required Shutterstock's whole editorial business to be sold. And the cost of carrying its debt.

RajIts debt was about one and a half years of revenue. Just the payments on it took about a quarter of a year's sales.

RajAnd it says the pressure on its budget brand, iStock, and on its agency customers would not, alone, have forced it into court.

ErinSo where does AI come in at all?

RajIn that same filing. Under broader industry headwinds, it names generative AI as one of several shifts, with changes in how people consume media and how companies market. Felt principally, it says, in iStock and among its agency customers.

RajIts paying customers did fall, by about a quarter since twenty twenty-two. That's the count for the whole company, and Getty says it's driven mostly by iStock and agency buyers.

RajBut its annual reports for twenty twenty-three to twenty twenty-five explained that fall without mentioning AI. A shift to subscriptions, shrinking agency customers, and the end of a free-trial offer. AI shows up as a cause only in the bankruptcy filing.

RajAnd its revenue grew in every one of those years, to just under a billion dollars in twenty twenty-five.

ErinAnd if I sell my photos through Getty?

RajGetty says it expects no impact on its contributors. That's a stated intention, not a finding. Their royalties have been flat, at about two hundred and twenty million dollars a year in total, and Getty doesn't say how much of that comes from AI training deals.

ErinSo we just take Getty's word for it?

RajNo. It's Getty's own account, to its creditors and the court. And Getty is on both sides. It licenses its pictures to AI companies for training, and it sells its own AI image tools.

RajThe one outside voice is Britain's regulator. It expects big AI firms to compete with stock photos more and more. That's a forecast, not a measured loss.

RajSo, false as the cause. AI is one named pressure, on one part of the business. Hold that gap, between the headline and the filing. You'll see it again.

Claim 2: was the strike really about dues?

ErinHere's one from the video-game actors' strike. It stopped being about AI. It was really about union dues, and who controls the hiring.

RajThe union struck ten big game companies and their production arms in July twenty twenty-four. When it called the strike, its own release said: AI protections remain the sticking point.

RajIn March twenty twenty-five, its message to members said the two sides were still far apart on AI. Using past performances. Consent. Pay. And a replica of you working during a future strike, whether you like it or not.

RajWhen it ended in twenty twenty-five, the AP reported a strike that had halted over AI protections. And the companies' own spokesperson described the deal as wage rises, AI protections and safety.

ErinAnd the dues? The hiring?

RajNowhere in what we read. Not at the start, not in the middle, not at the end. So that claim is false.

RajOne honest limit. That's the public record. It isn't proof that nobody ever argued about dues. But no record we found shows that fight.

ErinDid they get the AI protections they struck for?

RajSome. A performer's written consent, and disclosure, before a digital replica is used, and pay floors for it. But no term on training AI with their performances. And smaller games on the union's tiered-budget indie deals weren't covered.

Claim 3: do Glaze and Nightshade protect your art?

ErinThis one's for anyone who posts their art. Run it through Glaze or Nightshade first, and it's protected from AI.

RajBoth come from a University of Chicago lab. Glaze adds tiny changes to an image, meant to stop a model copying your style. Nightshade is meant to poison what a model learns from it.

RajIn its makers' own tests, Glaze disrupted style copying more than nine times in ten.

ErinThen it works.

RajIn their tests. Then outside researchers tried off-the-shelf tricks. Adding noise and then upscaling the picture still got almost perfect copies of the style, from art protected with Glaze's second version.

RajGlaze shipped a patch against that attack. And the project itself says Glaze is not a permanent solution, and it works less well, or not at all, on a style a model already knows.

RajNightshade's evidence comes from its makers' own lab. Its own guide says it only affects one kind of model, and only when a lot of shaded images get trained on.

RajSo, partly true. It raises the cost of copying you. It doesn't make your work safe.

ErinAnd if the tools don't hold? Can't the courts stop it?

RajNot yet. In twenty twenty-five, two federal judges in the same California court found that training AI on books was fair use.

RajAnd they split on pirated copies. One said building a library from pirated books was not fair use. The other said downloading them could be. Neither ruling binds any other court.

RajThe first case, against Anthropic, settled for one and a half billion dollars. That's about three thousand dollars per book, not per author, shared with the publisher if there is one, before costs and fees. And it pays for the pirated copies. It isn't a licence to train.

RajA note about us, said once. This show is made with AI. Anthropic, the company behind that AI, is a party in two of the cases in our research: this one, and a music publishers' suit over song lyrics.

RajHold that. The tools help at the edges, and the court cases haven't settled the rest.

Claim 4: are AI placeholders a non-issue?

ErinThen there's the game fight. AI placeholders are a non-issue.

RajThat's the defence of Expedition Thirty-Three, the role-playing game that had two Indie Game Awards taken back in December twenty twenty-five.

RajA placeholder is a stand-in. Studios build with rough art first and swap in the real thing before release, so nobody wastes time on art that gets cut.

RajDays after the game came out in April twenty twenty-five, fans spotted textures that looked AI-made. Five days after launch, a patch replaced a placeholder texture. The patch note doesn't say AI. The link comes from the press, and from the director's own words.

RajHe says the team tried AI in twenty twenty-two, used it as placeholders for textures they missed, and took it out as soon as they found it. Everything in the game, he says, is human made.

ErinSo it really was a non-issue.

RajNot quite. Those textures shipped. Anyone who played in the first five days could have them on screen.

RajAnd the award had a hard rule against generative AI. When the game was entered, a studio representative agreed no gen AI was used in making it. Once the studio confirmed it had been, the award was pulled.

RajSo, partly true. Small, yes. Fixed within a week, yes. But it cost the award, because of what the studio signed when it entered.

ErinAnd the other version I keep seeing? The game was made with AI?

RajPartly true as well. AI touched the making, for a few textures, for a few days. Nothing on the record shows AI in the game you'd play today.

Claim 5: is Pinterest flooded with AI images?

ErinPinterest is flooded with AI images. I hear that one all the time.

RajWhere people look most, it holds up. In April twenty twenty-five, a spot-check went through sixteen of the trends in Pinterest's own trend report.

RajTwelve of the sixteen showed several AI images in the top five search results. All sixteen had them in the top twenty. None carried a label.

RajAnother check that year, of healthy recipe ideas, found at least six of the first twelve pins were AI. One was labelled.

ErinSo the whole site is mostly AI now.

RajNobody knows that. These are hand checks of popular searches, not a count of the site. Pinterest's only figure is a small percentage of impressions, with no number attached.

RajAnd the seventy-nine percent going around, for Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest? It traces to one blog, citing a detector company's analysis. No sample, no tool, no time period. We couldn't find the report.

RajSo, partly true. Flooded where it's been checked, and that's where people look. How much of the whole site is AI, nobody has counted.

RajMusic has the same shape. One streaming service says AI tracks are more than half its uploads on peak days, but only one to three percent of what people actually play.

Claim 6: does Spotify pay pennies?

ErinSpotify pays artists pennies, and the labels take the rest.

RajStart with pennies. Spotify doesn't set a price per stream. It pays about two-thirds of its music revenue into a pool, split by each track's share of the listening.

RajSo any per-stream number you've seen is someone dividing total payouts by total streams, after the fact. It moves with how much people listen.

ErinAnd the labels take the rest?

RajNot all of it. In the UK regulator's figures for twenty twenty-one, the streaming services kept about a third of the money. Record labels took over half, and songwriters and publishers about fifteen percent.

RajAnd UK artists on the three major labels got, on average, about a quarter of the labels' streaming money. That's before their advances were paid back.

RajAnd the money piles up at the top. In UK research the regulator cited, the top zero point four percent of artists took nearly two-thirds of all streams.

RajOne more change. Since April twenty twenty-four, a track needs a thousand streams a year to earn anything. Spotify says that money, about forty million dollars a year, goes to the tracks above the line.

RajSo, partly true. Nobody sets a price per stream, and the service keeps about a third. But on a major-label deal, the label keeps most of the recording money.

ErinAnd the AI acts? The singer on the charts, the AI actress?

RajQuickly. An AI act did hit number one, on Billboard's country download chart, with about two thousand sales, and no AI act shows on the Hot 100 in anything we read. The AI actress's maker said an agency would sign her. No agency has been named.

Claim 7: did Miyazaki call AI an insult to life itself?

ErinMiyazaki, the animation director, said AI is an insult to life itself.

RajHe did say it. In a twenty sixteen documentary on Japan's NHK.

RajA tech company's team showed him a figure whose movement had been learned by AI. It moved by using its head like a leg. They suggested it for zombie games.

RajHe answered with a friend who has a physical disability, for whom even a high five is hard. Then he called it an insult to life itself, and said he wanted nothing to do with it in his work.

ErinSo that's his verdict on AI art.

RajThat's the stretch. It was twenty sixteen, years before public image generators. It was one demo of AI-learned movement. And we found no statement from him on today's image tools.

RajThe man who showed him that demo has since said he wishes the clip would stop going around.

RajSo this one is true, as a twenty sixteen remark. Share it with its date and its demo, or it says something he never said.

Claim 8: is AI wiping out creative jobs?

ErinLast one, and it's the big one. AI is wiping out creative jobs.

RajStart with the scariest number you've seen. A hundred thousand film and TV jobs, already gone to AI.

RajIt traces to a twenty twenty-four report. About a hundred and eighteen thousand jobs likely to be consolidated, replaced or eliminated by AI by twenty twenty-six. It's a forecast, built on executives' expectations.

RajThree hundred executives were surveyed. It counts no jobs AI might create. And it was commissioned by the Animation Guild and three other groups with a stake in the answer.

ErinBut film jobs really did fall, didn't they?

RajThey did. Payrolls in film and sound recording are down about a hundred and twenty-eight thousand since late twenty twenty-two.

RajBut late twenty twenty-two was a peak, the rebound after the pandemic. The fall runs through the twenty twenty-three strikes and a production slump. And the government's statisticians give no cause.

ErinAnd the surveys? A quarter of illustrators say they've lost work to AI.

RajThat's from a survey by Britain's Society of Authors, in early twenty twenty-four. A quarter of the illustrators who chose to answer. It never published how many illustrators that was.

RajAnd the wording moves the answer. An American authors' survey offered don't know. Six percent said they were aware of losing work to AI, and half said they didn't know.

RajA voice actors' survey asked, have you ever knowingly lost a job to a synthetic voice? Twenty-one percent said yes. That's ever, not this year.

RajSix, twenty-one, twenty-six. Three surveys, three wordings, three answers. And nobody has a baseline for how often freelancers lose clients anyway.

ErinSo what do the government's own counts show, across creative work?

RajAcross arts, design and media, payroll jobs were about flat from twenty twenty-two to twenty twenty-five. Over the same years, all jobs grew about five percent.

RajGraphic designers really did fall, by about one in thirteen. But all of it came in one year, twenty twenty-four to twenty-five, two years after the image tools launched.

RajPayroll writers are down about a tenth. By our arithmetic, that's within the survey's sampling error, and they're still above twenty nineteen. Musicians and photographers went up.

RajTwo warnings come with these counts. The government's own statisticians don't encourage reading them year to year. And they leave out the self-employed, which means a lot of the people who make things for a living.

RajAnd they don't put any of these changes down to AI.

ErinSo nothing's happening to creative jobs?

RajSomething is. It's just narrower than the slogan. The clearest evidence is on freelance platforms, where researchers can compare work AI can do against work it can't.

RajAfter ChatGPT arrived, four separate studies found that freelance writing work fell, against comparable work.

RajOn one platform, postings for the kinds of work AI can do fell seven percent, while postings for work it can't do rose seventeen.

RajThe sharpest falls were short jobs, one to three weeks long. Postings to write a company's About Us page fell by more than half, against the trend.

RajSo freelance writing is the one that's true. The work fell, and the studies tie it to ChatGPT.

RajBut AI is wiping out creative jobs, across the board? Not measured. Not because nothing is happening, but because nobody has counted it. A forecast is not a count, and a fall is not a cause.

The verdict: eight claims, four stamps

ErinSo, Raj. Eight claims. Which ones can I stop repeating?

RajTwo. AI didn't bankrupt Getty, by Getty's own account. And the game actors' strike never stopped being about AI.

ErinAnd which ones hold up?

RajMiyazaki's line, with its date and its demo. And freelance writing work really did fall, after ChatGPT arrived.

ErinAnd everything in between?

RajGlaze, the placeholders, Pinterest and Spotify each have a real core, and a piece people add. And whether AI is wiping out creative jobs, nobody has measured yet.

RajWhere the record leans: the flood of AI work is real where it's been counted. The cheap, short, commissioned jobs are the ones going. The courts haven't decided. And no tool keeps your work safe.

ErinAnd the next time one of these lands in my feed?

RajAsk three things before you share it. Whose account is this? Is it a forecast, or a count of what happened? And did anyone actually tie it to AI?

ErinAnd the joke? AI doing the art while I do the dishes?

RajThe worry under it is fair. For some freelance writers, it's already true. For most of the rest, it's still an open question. Check which one you're sharing.
