Inside an AI Data Center: What's Actually in There?

What is in a data center, how does it work, and how much power and water does it really use?

Last changed 2026-11-07 · See every change

An explainer: the racks, the power, the heat, the water, and which numbers hold up

Settled on the record

Why it was in the news

When this episode came out in September 2026, data centers were at the centre of arguments over power bills, water, and towns voting on whether to let one in. Their electricity use had roughly tripled in seven years and was still climbing.

Settled

What the record has settled

A data center is a building full of computers that never turn off, and nearly all the power it draws ends up as heat. US data centers used about 176 terawatt-hours in 2023, around four and a half percent of the country's electricity, and the national lab expects 7 to 12% by 2028. Nationally, the power plants behind them use more than ten times the water the buildings use on site.

What people get wrong

The "bottle of water per AI prompt" comes from a study that said a bottle for every 10 to 50 responses, for an older model, counting the power plant's water too. Five-gigawatt headlines are usually a plan: Meta's Louisiana figure is a goal, not what the site drew. Each question is small, the total is big and growing, and whether the water matters depends on where the building is.

This one's an explainer: no sides were argued, so there's no dial.
