  the direction is sound but a number would imply more precision than the source supports.

## Fairness audit
- **The record is lopsided, and the brief says so.** On "is AI causing mass unemployment now?" every
  aggregate series and every null study points one way, and so do the alarm side's own best sources
  (Amodei, Goldman, the Canaries authors). The brief does not manufacture a 50/50 there. It places the
  genuine split where the evidence puts it: whether the entry-level gap is AI, and how fast change comes.
- **The engine's imbalance was fixed before the steelman.** The engine argued the alarm side mostly from
  relays (Amodei via a Yahoo copy of Axios, Goldman via Fortune). Hand pass D opened Amodei's own essay,
  the Axios article itself, and Goldman's own June 2026 transcript and Top of Mind issue, so Side A is now
  argued from primaries (D-A1, D-A9–A13, D-A19). Goldman's October note is client research and stays
  unread; only what Fortune quotes from it is used, attributed to Fortune (D-A3, D-A4).
- **Acemoglu was moved, not dropped.** The engine filed him with the "slow, nothing much happens" side;
  his own words put him at "small net negative for labor" (D-A19). The brief uses him that way.
- **The label runs both ways, and both directions are in.** Company claims that credit AI (Barclays,
  Klarna, Benioff) and that deny it (Jassy, TCS, Salesforce's spokesperson) are all management
  statements, marked as stated views; neither direction is measured (B-A10, B-A11, B-B33, B-B41).
- **Kroger is not used as an AI example** in either direction: its quoted words say "technology", and
  whether the full memo names AI is unresolved (B-A9, B-A15).
- **Interested parties are named as such.** The WGA 2023 summary is the union's own campaign site; Littler
  and Hunton are management-side law firms; "first-in-the-nation" is a union leader's description of a
  package; company headcount claims are management self-reports.
- **The show's own model maker appears in the record.** Dario Amodei is Anthropic's CEO and one exposure
  index is Anthropic's. Both are used at equal weight with every other source, with no on-air callout, per
  the show's rule. So are three more: one of the two exposure measures in California's tracker is built
  from Claude usage (T1-7); the best direct measure of non-engineers building software is Anthropic's own
  study of its Claude Code users, which publishes no share and is named as Anthropic's data wherever it is
  used (T3-1 to T3-7); and Anthropic's study of its own staff is labelled as self-report at one company
  (T4-30, T4-31). Amodei's "90% of code" is quoted from the transcript, with the fact that Anthropic sells
  coding AI, and set against the one independent measurement found (T4-24, T4-32).
- **Barclays and Claude, as top-up T5 verified it.** The peg hunt's lead that Barclays' AI "runs on
  Claude" was CORRECTED (T5-12). What is verified at a primary: on 2026-10-01 Anthropic announced, quoting
  two Barclays co-COOs, that Barclays is expanding its use of Claude and expects half its developers to be
  using Claude Code by the end of 2026, a target (T5-1, T5-3). Barclays also announced Microsoft 365
  Copilot for 100,000 colleagues in 2025 (T5-11). The two air together, at equal weight, as plain facts.
  No primary ties either tool to the 5,000 cuts or the "fewer developers": the results speech names no
  vendor and the Claude announcement never mentions jobs (T5-8, T5-9), so the brief forbids any line or
  picture that sets them side by side as cause.

  mix? The authors call their results descriptive; the critiques that could be reached are their own list
  of rivals (C-C8–C-C17).

  AI record what the employer chose to say (B-B14, A-C3); California's tracker bypasses the employer but,
