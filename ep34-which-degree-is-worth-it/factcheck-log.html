Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both designs got the same treatment: every claim re-opened at its primary. The record is not symmetric. "Pick the field, at the price you'll actually pay" has ED's own field medians, College Board's and NCES's net prices, and a causal literature that says a degree pays for students at the margin. "Bet on finishing, cheapest path first" has strong descriptive records on completion and on what non-completion costs, but its routes (community college then transfer, apprenticeship) have thin, older or single-state outcome evidence, and no study compares an apprenticeship head to head with a bachelor's net of cost, time and completion. The brief says so.
  • No false balance on "is college worth it". On the average, the record leans one way: a bachelor's pays, raw and in the best causal studies. The episode does not stage "yes vs no"; it asks which degree, at what price, for whom, and says where the average stops being informative.
  • Opinion is labelled as opinion. Gallup's 31% is what Americans think, not evidence about outcomes.
  • Advocacy is attributed. FREOPP, Employ America, EIG, Brookings, New America and the Burning Glass Institute appear as named analyses, never as the record itself.
  • No politics. The 2025 law and the 2027 rule are described by what their text does. No line frames the episode for or against an administration or party.
  • No field is mocked. Low-paying fields (drama, teacher education, psychology) are shown as numbers with their spreads, and the brief notes that early medians understate later gaps and miss non-money value. The verdict never tells a viewer what to love.
  • No field is singled out by politics (top-ups, 2026-10-07). The graduates-vs-jobs table applies one rule to all 73 NY Fed majors and reports where every field lands. The worst on underemployment are criminal justice and the performing arts, then fine arts; ethnic studies, sociology, psychology, English, history and philosophy sit mid-pack among the fields that fall short, beside business management, communications, biology and political science. Gender and ethnic studies together are about 2,500 degrees a year. Price is set by the school, not the field (section 17).
  • Both sides of "make colleges carry the risk" are steelmanned from named sources (section 18): its advocates (Looney and Yannelis, Cooper) and its critics, including its own designers' warning about access, and the needed low-pay fields ED's commenters raised.
  • Associations are not sold as causes (section 22): each non-money row says whether it is causal, and for college itself the causal record is thin.
  • Who the averages leave out is said. Scorecard earnings cover federal-aid recipients only; the 2027 test sees only graduates; Clearinghouse income quintiles are by neighbourhood.
