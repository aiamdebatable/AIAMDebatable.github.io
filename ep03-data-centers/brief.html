Research brief — Episode 3 · The AI Data-Center Boom

The full research behind the episode. This is the "full research" we link below the video.

The question

Should we slam the brakes on the AI data-center boom — or build fast and aim the demand? It's a live fight online ("AI is boiling the planet" vs. "AI will save the climate"), but most of the shouting is about facts that aren't actually in dispute. The real disagreement is narrower.

Settled (not the debate)

  1. Demand is large, growing fast. Data centers used ~415 TWh of electricity in 2024 (~1.5% of global power); the IEA projects that roughly doubles to ~945–950 TWh by 2030 — about Japan's total consumption today. [IEA, Energy and AI, 2025]
  2. It's concentrated. In the US, data centers were ~4% of national electricity in 2023; LBNL projects 6.7–12% by 2028. Ireland: 21% of all national electricity went to data centers in 2023, more than every urban home combined. Loudoun County, VA = "Data Center Alley." [LBNL 2024; CSO Ireland 2024]
  3. Driven by AI. Per-query energy estimates are disputed and falling (from ~2.9 Wh in 2023 toward ~0.3 Wh in 2025 — roughly a web search); the real driver is training frontier models and the race to build campus-scale data centers. [Epoch AI 2025; IEA] — ⚠️ corrected: an earlier "~10× a search" claim was outdated (see factcheck-log #8).
  4. Few permanent jobs. A hyperscale site employs thousands during construction but typically only ~50–150 people once running.
  5. Water is real but local. Google reported drawing >6 billion gallons in 2023 (+~17% YoY). Impact depends entirely on siting — negligible in a wet region, contested in a desert. [Google 2024 Env. Report]
  6. Right now the gap is partly fossil-bridged. Demand is arriving faster than clean supply can be built, so utilities are leaning on new gas (e.g. Georgia) and delaying coal retirements — even though Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta are the four largest corporate clean-energy buyers on Earth. [BloombergNEF] Both are true at once because clean and dirty megawatts show up in different places and times.
  7. Two words carry hidden weight: efficiency (data centers got ~550% more compute for ~flat energy 2010–2018 — yet efficiency has never capped total demand: Jevons paradox) and additionality (is the clean power genuinely new, or just claimed?). [Masanet et al., Science 2020]

Contested (the real fight)

Green — "Build Fast" (net accelerant): The boom is the strongest force for new firm clean generation we have. Hyperscalers' 24/7 demand and deep pockets are financing the largest clean-energy deals in history and reviving firm nuclear (Microsoft–Three Mile Island restart; Amazon–Susquehanna; Google–Kairos; Meta's nuclear RFP — all 2024). Slowing it doesn't cool the planet; it exports the data centers and the leverage to places that set no clean terms. Fix: aim the demand — tie the buildout to new clean power.

Gold — "Pump the Brakes" (net drag): The load arrives years before the clean power does (wind/solar connect in 1–4 years; new nuclear takes 10+). In the meantime the fossil bridge burns, and the public often pays for the wires while one company keeps the profit. Efficiency won't save us (Jevons). Braking isn't banning — it's pacing: site where the grid and water allow, require bring-your-own new clean power, and make large loads pay their own interconnection.

The reframe

Not whether the load is real and growing (it is) or whether efficiency alone caps it (it doesn't) — those are settled. The real fight is timing and terms: does the private buildout drag clean firm power onto the grid faster than it drags fossil bridges on, and on whose terms?

Both sides quietly agree

The binding constraint isn't the data centers — it's how fast we can build clean generation and the transmission to carry it. When both opponents reach for the same fact, it's worth noticing.

Verdict framing

No winner. The settled facts are settled; whether the boom accelerates the clean grid or just strains it is handed to the viewer. Each side's weakest link is flagged: Green's claim that today's contracts offset today's gas is more hope than accounting; Gold's "pace by permit" risks exporting the buildout to somewhere with no rules at all.
