The verdict

What the record supports

What didn't hold up
Social Security isn't going broke; it will run short, and it wasn't stolen without being owed. The law doesn't cut checks automatically. Read the verdict as it aired

Raj This one doesn't resolve into a winning lever, and neither team wins it. Three things from me: what's settled, what survived each side, and the dial that's yours.

Raj The record both teams accepted: the money was spent, and it is owed. From late twenty thirty-two, taxes coming in cover about seventy-eight cents on the dollar on every retirement check, and the law doesn't say how. Fixing it takes a third more payroll tax or a quarter off every check, and waiting raises both.

Raj What survived from green. The cap eroded, and the Chief Actuary names that, not longer lives, as a main reason. The payroll rate is the only single lever that closes the whole gap. And a higher age does nothing for twenty thirty-two, and the longer lives it counts on went mostly to the top.

Raj What survived from gold. People live longer at sixty-five than in nineteen eighty-three. A later age tied to longevity closes about a third with no new tax. And the cap alone closes fifty-eight percent at most, so money in has to mean the rate as well, and that lands on every worker's pay.

Raj What fell. Social Security isn't going broke; it will run short, and it wasn't stolen without being owed. The law doesn't cut checks automatically. People don't just work longer, a later age doesn't fix twenty thirty-two, the cap alone doesn't fix it, neither lever touches only the rich, and Congress hasn't moved the retirement age.

Raj So here's what the record supports. Doing nothing is the harshest option, because the default reaches today's retirees too. Every serious source points to a mix, or one very large single move. And no package has been scored on this year's numbers.

Raj A single dial is left: how much comes from money in, how much from money out, and on whom. All money in, like the rate alone, costs a third more tax or more. Both is what nineteen eighty-three did. Mostly money out means a later age and a slower formula, cutting future checks.

Raj If nobody turns it, every retirement check comes up about a fifth short from late twenty thirty-two. Your full retirement age is sixty-seven if you were born in nineteen sixty or later, and claiming at sixty-two costs thirty percent. How much in, how much out, and on whom is a dial only you can set.

The judge named no sides on this one, so there's no dial: we don't invent positions.
