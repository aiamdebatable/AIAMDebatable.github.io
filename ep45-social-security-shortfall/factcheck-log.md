## Fairness audit
- **Neither lever's case is written beyond its evidence.** The age side's best figures (life
  expectancy at 65 up about 3 years since 1983, against a 2-year age rise; the actuaries' 32% for age 69
  and indexing) are on the record at the primary (log 154–158, 114). So are its costs: a cut at every
  claiming age, nothing done for 2032, and longevity gains that went mostly to the top (log 105, 120,
  159–166). The cap side's best figures (the taxed share down from 90% to about 83%, named by the Chief
  Actuary as one of two reasons the 1983 fix fell short) are primary (log 201, 205, 101). So are its
  limits: 58% at most for eliminating it, less with a benefit credit, and revenue that depends on how
  people respond (log 217, 218, 234–242).
- **Corrections cut both ways and every one is kept.** The CBO lifetime measure (the bottom fifth loses
  the smallest share from an age rise) cuts against the "hits the poor hardest" line; the NAS correction
  (the largest proportional cut at the bottom within Social Security) cuts the other way (log 125, 174,
  175). The "costs a medium earner nothing" correction cuts against the cap side; the "only 6% ever pay"
  correction cuts against its critics (log 231, 203).
- **Advocacy is marked where it speaks:** CRFB (a deficit-reduction group), CEPR and SCEPA, AARP, a
  sponsor's press release and a blog; each is used only for its own figure, attributed, and several of
  their headline figures were corrected (log 41, 46, 74).
- **Our arithmetic is labelled every time** (log 63, 105, 108, 110, 155, 285, 286, 349, 359, 362, 363, 392), and the per-person
  dollar figures are never attributed to SSA.
- **Election season:** the episode is about the levers, not candidates. Campaign positions on the cap
  were rejected as a peg (Peg hunt, Phase 3).
- **Identity↔stance rotation:** to be checked at seating (the build step), not here.
