Is it good for us?

RyanOkay, Mara. Give me the short version before the long one.

MaraGood for you? It's lean, and it's a real iron win. It is not a superfood.

MaraSafe? Safe enough, if you do a few things. And one of the risks is one almost nobody mentions.

MaraEvery fall, states ask hunters to get their deer tested for chronic wasting disease. This year Wisconsin found its first case in Marinette County, and Michigan wants deer heads from Kent, Montcalm and three neighbouring counties.

MaraWe mean wild white-tailed deer, the kind that families in the upper Midwest hunt, butcher, share and eat. Not farmed venison from a store.

RyanThen start with what's on the label. How lean is it, really?

MaraEvery number here is one serving, three ounces cooked. That's one cooked quarter-pound patty.

MaraA venison loin steak: about a hundred and thirty calories and two grams of fat.

MaraA skinless chicken breast: about a hundred and forty calories and three grams. Pork tenderloin is right there with them.

MaraLean beef sirloin: about a hundred and sixty calories, and five grams of fat.

RyanSo it beats the steak. But it doesn't beat the chicken.

MaraIt's in the chicken-breast club. Not above it.

MaraThe real win is iron. About three and a half milligrams a serving. Roughly a fifth of what you need in a day. Chicken breast has less than one.

RyanPeople say it's lower in cholesterol, too.

MaraThat one doesn't hold. Cooked lean venison is about the same as chicken and beef. A few entries run higher.

RyanAnd the neighbour's package is ground.

MaraThen it's a different food. Plain ground venison has about seven grams of fat a serving. An eighty-five fifteen beef patty has about twelve.

MaraBut one hunters' guide to grinding your own says to add pork fat. Ten to twenty percent for burger, and more for sausage.

MaraWith a fifth of it pork fat, the burger lands about where eighty-twenty beef does. And USDA's venison sausage recipe has more fat than raw eighty-twenty hamburger.

RyanAnd from the white paper, you can't tell which one you've got.

MaraOne more thing. Nobody has measured the payoff. No study has followed venison eaters to see whether their hearts or cancer rates do any better.

MaraSo healthier than beef is a reading of the label. Not a result. Hold that.

Can it make us sick?

RyanSo, chronic wasting disease. What actually is it?

MaraA prion disease of deer, elk and moose. A prion is a protein folded the wrong way.

MaraFor decades it was a Colorado and Wyoming problem. Then it showed up in South Dakota's whitetails in two thousand one, and in Wisconsin a year later.

MaraToday it's been found in thirty-seven states and five Canadian provinces. That's the US Geological Survey's count.

MaraWhere it settles in, it's common. In Wyoming last year, about one in three adult male whitetails tested positive. In Richland County, Wisconsin, about one in three deer that hunters brought in.

RyanCan you tell by looking at the deer?

MaraUsually not. Minnesota's DNR says an infected deer looks healthy throughout most of the infection. Only a lab test can tell.

MaraThe Marinette buck that made the news this fall is the exception. It was put down because it looked sick.

RyanSo can people catch it?

MaraHere's where the alarm started. In twenty seventeen, an internal Health Canada note described unpublished results as CWD passing to monkeys, including by feeding them the meat.

MaraThe peer-reviewed study came out this year. Its title starts with two words. Limited transmission.

MaraThe standard test found no prions in the monkeys. A far more sensitive lab test found faint traces in six of the seven brains it checked.

MaraAnd tissue from one monkey that had the disease put directly into its brain, passed through mice and then voles, gave those rodents full prion disease.

MaraThe authors say their results do not imply that it passes efficiently to primates. And they say the jump cannot be excluded.

RyanDoes everyone read it that way?

MaraNo. An NIH lab watched monkeys for eleven to thirteen years and found nothing, by any test. Outside experts split, too. One says leftover material from the dose is a serious possibility. Another says the prions look newly made.

MaraWhat nobody has found is a human case. The CDC says CWD infections in people have never been reported, and it's not known if people could get infected.

MaraEven the hunters' own National Deer Association writes that the risk is low, but it's not zero.

RyanWhat about the hunters who died? I saw that story.

MaraThat was a twenty twenty-four conference poster. The one diagnosis was ordinary, sporadic CJD, and the authors say causation is unproven. Its Methods section reads, in full, not applicable.

RyanSo, decades of no human case. That sounds good.

MaraIt is. But the watch is short. A human infection could take ten years or more to show.

MaraAnd the group being watched is small. In preliminary CDC numbers, reported by the National Deer Association, more than six hundred Wisconsin hunters said they ate, or meant to eat, venison from a positive deer.

MaraWith that few people, zero cases is what you'd expect either way.

They said mad cow couldn't cross

RyanHere's what a lot of people say, though. They told us mad cow couldn't reach people, either.

MaraIt's a fair thing to raise. For ten years, British officials told the public there was no evidence it could, that it was most unlikely, and that beef was safe to eat.

MaraIn nineteen ninety, the chief medical officer said on TV there was no risk.

MaraIn March nineteen ninety-six, ten young people turned out to have a new variant of CJD, probably from mad cow. It has killed more than two hundred and thirty people worldwide. Most of them in Britain.

RyanSo why trust anyone this time?

MaraBritain's official inquiry found the government did not lie. But its campaign of reassurance was a mistake. Its confidence was borrowed from scrapie, a sheep disease that hadn't infected people in over two hundred years.

MaraToday's reassurance rests on tests with CWD itself. Mad cow, fed to monkeys in one study, crossed easily.

MaraCWD, fed to monkeys at a similar dose in another lab, showed no classic disease in eleven to thirteen years.

MaraBut here's where the warning is sharpest. In cattle, the infection sat in the brain and spinal cord, so pulling those out protected beef eaters. In deer, the prions are in the muscle. The venison itself.

MaraCWD also comes in several strains, so the past doesn't necessarily predict the future. And a first human case might be filed as ordinary CJD.

MaraSo the honest verdict isn't, it's safe. And it isn't, it's coming. It's not proven yet.

RyanOkay. Then I'll just cook it really well.

MaraThat doesn't help. In one infected elk, medium-well grilling made the prions easier for the lab to find. Even the boiling water tested positive.

RyanSo medium-well is not a disinfection setting.

MaraIt is not. And dish soap didn't reliably clean the grinders. Bleach did. Hold that.

What the test tells you

RyanSo the hunter gets it tested. What does the test actually look at?

MaraNot the meat. Two lymph nodes from the throat. In Wisconsin deer tested both ways, about one positive in five showed it only in the lymph nodes. None showed it only in the brainstem.

MaraAnd it comes back one of two ways. Detected, or not detected. There's no result called CWD-free.

MaraOn thirteen hundred Michigan deer, three different lab tests disagreed on just seven. All seven were false alarms. Not misses.

RyanThen what does not detected tell me?

MaraThat it's less likely. Not that it's certified. The test is weaker early in an infection, when there's little to find.

MaraIn one lab study, it showed up in fawns' throat tissue as early as six weeks after exposure. So a deer infected shortly before the hunt could test clean. That's an inference. Nobody has counted that miss rate in wild deer.

MaraMinnesota's DNR calls it not a food safety test. Virginia's wildlife agency says a negative makes infection considerably less likely, without guaranteeing it.

MaraAnd that matters, because infectious prions have been found in the muscle of infected deer, including ones that looked healthy.

RyanAnd if it comes back positive?

MaraDon't eat it. Don't give it away. Don't feed it to the dog. Wisconsin says landfill the meat and soak your knives in half bleach, half water. Wisconsin and Michigan will replace the tag.

RyanTesting's free in a lot of places. So why doesn't everyone do it?

MaraMost don't. In Wisconsin, about one deer in nineteen got tested last season. In Minnesota, about one in thirteen.

MaraWhen North Carolina's wildlife agency asked hunters why not, the top answers were: it's not in my area, I didn't know how, my deer looked healthy, and I'm not worried about eating it.

MaraOver ninety-five percent of North Carolina hunters also said they'd test their deer, or dispose of it safely, if it were free.

RyanIn Wisconsin, it is free. One in nineteen.

MaraDifferent states. But still.

MaraAnd in Virginia, more than nine in ten hunters told surveyors the disease hasn't changed how much deer meat they eat.

RyanIs it just, it's never been a problem, so it won't be now?

MaraMaybe. Nobody has measured that. Here's the nearest thing. In an Illinois survey, hunters who lived in CWD counties saw less risk than hunters elsewhere. The researchers had expected the opposite.

MaraTheir suggested explanation: more than ninety-eight percent of tested deer there had come back negative, and that can feel like proof. They couldn't test it, and no study has.

MaraAnd it's the kind of reasoning Britain leaned on with that sheep disease. Fifty years without a human case is real. But with so few people watched, zero is what you'd see either way.

Eat it before the result?

RyanSo the hunter's waiting on a result. Can they eat it before it comes back?

MaraTwo state agencies answer that differently, in writing. Here are both, with the wait. We're not picking.

MaraMinnesota's DNR: it's the hunter's choice to eat venison before the result. Keep it separate and identifiable until then. Results there take about one to two weeks, sometimes longer.

MaraWisconsin's health department: in CWD areas, don't eat it or give it away until the result says not detected. Wisconsin's wait averaged about nine days last season.

MaraAnd in Michigan, the DNR says results may take six to eight weeks.

RyanWeeks. That's a lot of dinners.

RyanNow the neighbour's package. Whose deer is in it?

MaraThat's the question for the person given it. Wisconsin warns that sausage and jerky may contain trim from other deer, which may or may not have been tested. And it tells hunters to ask.

MaraMinnesota's agriculture department asks processors to hold the meat until results come in. That's guidance, not law.

MaraSo even a hunter who waited could be eating someone else's untested deer. Hold that.

The risk nobody talks about: lead

RyanYou said one risk almost nobody mentions.

MaraLead. From the bullet. In one Wyoming study, thirty hunter-shot deer went to thirty different processors. Every carcass had metal fragments.

MaraThe ground meat from twenty-four of the thirty deer had at least one. About one package in three. And more than nine in ten of the fragments they analysed were lead.

RyanCan't you just cut around the wound?

MaraNot reliably. In a Minnesota study using sheep, only about three in ten fragments sat within two inches of the exit wound. Lead turned up eighteen inches away, the farthest point they checked.

MaraAnd most fragments are too small to see, or to feel when you chew.

MaraGround meat is where it collects. Minnesota found lead in about a quarter of ground venison packages, against two percent of whole cuts.

MaraMinnesota X-rays donated venison, and throws out about one pound in ten.

RyanHow much does that matter in a person?

MaraIn a North Dakota study with the CDC, people who ate wild game had slightly higher blood lead. The gap was about a tenth of the level the CDC uses to flag a child. Small, but real.

MaraNobody has linked venison lead to an illness. But nobody has measured health outcomes in the people who eat it, either. And the CDC says no safe blood lead level in children has been identified.

RyanSo who should skip it?

MaraHealth departments in North Dakota, Minnesota and Michigan say young children and pregnant women shouldn't eat venison shot with lead. New York said much the same in twenty twenty-five.

RyanIs there a fix?

MaraThe bullet. In Minnesota's tests, copper bullets left about nine fragments. Lead types left about eighty to a hundred and forty. And studies on deer, bear and boar find copper kills about as well.

The everyday ones

RyanOkay. What about the everyday stuff? Undercooking?

MaraThe federal chart says cook venison to a hundred and sixty degrees. The CDC says the lower whole-cut rule doesn't cover wild game.

MaraAt a twenty seventeen retreat in Wisconsin, nine of the eleven people who ate deliberately undercooked venison got a parasite infection, toxoplasmosis.

RyanAnd the forever chemicals? PFAS?

MaraThat's about place, and mostly the liver. In a twenty twenty Marinette County study, one PFAS chemical turned up in the livers of all twenty deer tested, and in the muscle of one.

MaraMichigan says skip deer liver and kidneys, statewide. Maine has four areas where it says don't eat any part.

MaraOn EHD, North Dakota says there's no risk from handling or eating the meat. On screwworm, USDA's not a food safety issue is about inspected commercial meat, and Texas says don't eat an infested animal.

Is it really free meat?

RyanAt least it's free meat, right?

MaraOne Maine hunter worked it out in his local paper. His buck's processing came to roughly three dollars a pound. Ground beef is about six eighty.

MaraNow our math. It's rough. The average big-game hunter spent about thirteen hundred and fifty dollars on trips and gear in twenty twenty-two.

MaraSpread a year of that over one average deer, and it's about twenty-six dollars a pound. Nearly twice the price of a sirloin steak.

RyanSo the free meat costs more than steak.

MaraIf that's your one deer. It's per hunter, not per deer. Some hunters get several deer, and many get none. And big game includes elk and bear.

MaraThe honest version: it's cheap meat if you'd hunt anyway. It isn't free. That package was a real gift.

Four claims, quickly

RyanQuick ones. Things people say every season. Venison is the healthiest red meat on the planet.

MaraNot supported. One hunting magazine wrote that, and said venison was lower in cholesterol than the other meats. The chicken in its own table was lower. And lean bison and grass-fed beef are just as lean.

RyanIt's full of omega-3.

MaraMostly false. One study of wild elk, mule deer and antelope found a good balance of omega fats, like grass-fed beef. But the amounts are tiny. Next to salmon, a trace.

RyanIt's organic, so it's cleaner.

MaraWe found no source on calling wild venison organic. And cleaner fails in places. The lead fragments. The PFAS hot spots.

RyanZombie deer disease is going to jump to people.

MaraThere are no zombies. It's a nickname. One viral headline said a new study shows a risk to humans. The same article admitted the study fails to provide conclusive proof.

The verdict: three people, one deer

RyanSo, Mara. Back to the package in the freezer. Is it good for you?

MaraYes, with limits. As lean as chicken breast, with a lot more iron. Not lower in cholesterol. Not an omega-3 food. And the burger and the sausage are a different food.

RyanAnd is it safe?

MaraThat depends on which of the three you are.

MaraIf you shot it: in a CWD area, get it tested. It's free in all three states. Keep the meat separate and identifiable until the result. Never eat or share a positive deer. Eat before the result? The states differ, and whoever you share it with is relying on your choice.

MaraIf you were given it: ask two questions. Was it tested, and what came back? And could the sausage hold trim from other deer? Not detected means less likely, not certified. And cook it to a hundred and sixty.

MaraAnd if you've already eaten from a deer that later tests positive? No agency we read says what to do. Their advice covers only the before.

MaraIf there's a child under six at the table, or someone pregnant: everything above, plus lead. Three state health departments say don't serve them lead-shot venison. Whole cuts, or meat from a deer shot with copper, is the lower-lead plate.

RyanSo is it worth it?

MaraThe upside is real, but modest. Lean meat, and a lot of iron. And the steps that make it safer cost little. A free test, a thermometer, and whole cuts or copper for the smallest plates.

RyanSo when the neighbour knocks with that white paper package?

MaraSay thank you. Then ask whose deer is in it, and what the test said.
