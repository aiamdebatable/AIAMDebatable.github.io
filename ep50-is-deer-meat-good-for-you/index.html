Is Deer Meat Good for You?

Is venison really healthier than beef, and is it safe to eat?

Last changed 2026-11-04 · See every change

One deer, three people: the hunter, the person given the meat, and a parent. What's good in venison, what can make you sick, and what each should do before dinner.

The verdict

▶ Rewatch the verdict at 15:29 on YouTube
Read the verdict as it aired

Ryan So, Mara. Back to the package in the freezer. Is it good for you?

Mara Yes, with limits. As lean as chicken breast, with a lot more iron. Not lower in cholesterol. Not an omega-3 food. And the burger and the sausage are a different food.

Ryan And is it safe?

Mara That depends on which of the three you are.

Mara If you shot it: in a CWD area, get it tested. It's free in all three states. Keep the meat separate and identifiable until the result. Never eat or share a positive deer. Eat before the result? The states differ, and whoever you share it with is relying on your choice.

Mara If you were given it: ask two questions. Was it tested, and what came back? And could the sausage hold trim from other deer? Not detected means less likely, not certified. And cook it to a hundred and sixty.

Mara And if you've already eaten from a deer that later tests positive? No agency we read says what to do. Their advice covers only the before.

Mara If there's a child under six at the table, or someone pregnant: everything above, plus lead. Three state health departments say don't serve them lead-shot venison. Whole cuts, or meat from a deer shot with copper, is the lower-lead plate.

Ryan So is it worth it?

Mara The upside is real, but modest. Lean meat, and a lot of iron. And the steps that make it safer cost little. A free test, a thermometer, and whole cuts or copper for the smallest plates.

Ryan So when the neighbour knocks with that white paper package?

Mara Say thank you. Then ask whose deer is in it, and what the test said.

The judge named no sides on this one, so there's no dial: we don't invent positions.
