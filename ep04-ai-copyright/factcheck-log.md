## Provisional / "moves fast" caveats (stated in the episode)
- **All three fair-use rulings (Bartz, Kadrey, Thomson Reuters) are trial-court level; Thomson Reuters
  is on appeal to the Third Circuit. No binding appellate precedent yet.** The close explicitly tells
  the viewer to "treat the direction as real, and the specifics as provisional," and the verdict board
  carries the caveat "leans on district-court rulings still under appeal."
- **The decisive question — market dilution under factor four — is unlitigated on a full record.** This
  is exactly why the episode declares no winner; the gold-side caveat on the board reads "the market
  dilution harm it rests on hasn't yet won in court."
- Licensing-deal dollar figures are press-reported, not filed/audited — hedged as "reported."
- (Not used) The Perlmutter firing is politically charged; left out to avoid asserting motive.

## Fairness audit
- **Topic-shape:** honestly two-sided but NOT 50/50 on every sub-question. The episode says so out
  loud — the piracy and the "training can be transformative" holdings are treated as settled, and only
  the market-harm question is staged as the live debate. No fake balance.
- **Steelman parity:** each side gets a full opening (8 beats) + a rebuttal (4 beats); each side's
  strongest real authority is used (Green: Bartz/Kadrey/Getty/§107 idea-expression; Gold:
  Thomson Reuters/Copyright Office/dilution). Each side's board entry carries its own honest caveat.
- **Identity ↔ stance rotation held:** vs Ep-3 the two debate genders were flipped (Green now male
  Raj, Gold now female Kate), the researcher lead rotated to female (Lena), and the judge rotated to
  male (Walt). No demographic is locked to the fair-use side or the creators' side across the series.
- **No winner:** the judge hands the market-harm question to the viewer.
