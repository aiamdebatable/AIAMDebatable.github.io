Episode 6 · Fact-check log

Fact-check log — "AI emotion-recognition in schools: ban it, or bound it?"

Independent verification pass, run 2026-07-16 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). A dedicated research pass fetched primary/high-quality sources (Barrett et al. 2019 in Psychological Science in the Public Interest; the EU AI Act Article 5; Human Rights Watch's 2022 ed-tech review; the Lauren Rhue racial-bias study; the UK ICO warning; deployment reporting) and produced a claim-by-claim table. fact_check.status: web-verified.

Verdict summary

  • CONFIRMED: every number and named source used on air.
  • CORRECTED: none — the draft used only confirmed claims.
  • APPROXIMATE (flagged, used carefully): the Barrett effect sizes (r≈.32, configs ~15–25%) are summarized from meta-analyses within the review — the episode does not cite those specific numbers, only the review's headline conclusion ("you cannot reliably infer emotion from a facial expression"), which is the robust claim. The market figure ($2.7B→$9B) is a vendor/analyst projection and is framed as "roughly" / "projected."

Provisional / "moves fast" caveats (stated in the episode)

  • The close explicitly warns: "Europe's ban is on the books, but enforcement is young and untested, and the technology keeps rebranding — this one will keep moving." The EU prohibition is legally in force (Feb 2025) but enforcement mechanics and case law are still maturing; the episode presents it as in-force-but-untested, not as a fully litigated regime.

Fairness audit

  • Topic-shape (special): on the SCIENCE this is NOT 50/50 — the strongest expert review says emotion-from-face inference isn't reliable, and the episode says so plainly and refuses to stage a fake "does it work" debate. The genuine two-sided fight is the POLICY question (prohibit vs. bounded rules), and both steelmans there are real. This honesty is itself the fairness safeguard.
  • Steelman parity: each side gets a full opening (8) + rebuttal (4). Green's real points (the EU's own safety/expression carve-outs; accessibility research; the line-drawing problem; consent/audit as the tool for immature tech) and Gold's (invalid science + captive minors + demographic bias + chilling effect + the EU's outright ban) are all sourced. Each board entry carries an honest caveat.
  • Identity ↔ stance rotation held: vs Ep-5, debate genders flipped (Green now male Cole, Gold now female Lena); the researcher lead rotated to female (Kate); the judge rotated to male (Ryan). And the ban/prohibit stance has now been argued by a woman (Ep-4 Kate), a man (Ep-5 Andre), and a woman (Ep-6 Lena) — no demographic tracks a stance across the series.
  • No winner: the judge hands the ban-vs-boundary question to the viewer.
