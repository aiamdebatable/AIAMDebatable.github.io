Fact-check log — Can You Trust the Polls This November?

Independent verification pass, run 2026-10-03 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). Each claim gets a verdict: CONFIRMED, CORRECTED, UNRESOLVED or APPROXIMATE. This is a REQUIRED gate, set out under "Verification gate" in the episodes README. The show's differentiator is that this file is real.

  • CONFIRMED — the source says what the episode says.
  • CORRECTED — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
  • UNRESOLVED — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.
  • APPROXIMATE — checked, roughly right, and deliberately not cited on air as a precise figure.

Verification statusadversarial-pass _(a deep-research fan-out with single-vote primary verification,

then a hand pass that opened each primary: certified canvasses, AAPOR's task-force reports, the papers)_

⛔ No live 2026 horse-race number appears in this log or the episode. Candidates are "the polled leader" and "the winner".

1. "At least three" fake polls by Median Strategies, in a big-city mayoral race and two governor's races

CONFIRMED. AP: "at least three 'polls' across three states"; a mayoral race, a state's Democratic primary for governor, and another state's governor's race. Shared by a campaign, a news outlet and aggregator accounts on social media.

  • https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-know-about-the-fake-election-poll-numbers-shared-and-retracted-by-a-mysterious-company

2. The firm admitted it on Monday 17 August

CONFIRMED (secondary). AP and AAPOR both say "Monday"; 17 August 2026 was a Monday. The Los Angeles Times original could not be opened.

  • https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-know-about-the-fake-election-poll-numbers-shared-and-retracted-by-a-mysterious-company

3. AAPOR: "the responses were simply made up. No individuals responded to survey questions."

CORRECTED (date). The quote is exact. The statement was first published 19 August 2026; the same text was re-posted on AAPOR's news-releases page on 25 August. Our earlier notes said 25 August; the episode uses 19 August.

  • https://aapor.org/announcements/aapor-responds-to-fabricated-polling-data/
  • https://aapor.org/news-releases/aapor-responds-to-fabricated-polling-data/

4. The New York Times, FiftyPlusOne and RealClearPolling kept the fakes out of their averages

CORRECTED. An earlier scouting note named only two of these and was unsure of the third. AP: they "were not … included in the databases of major independent aggregators, including The New York Times, Real Clear Politics or FiftyPlusOne." The Times and FiftyPlusOne said why (missing method details); RealClearPolling's reason was not found.

  • https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-know-about-the-fake-election-poll-numbers-shared-and-retracted-by-a-mysterious-company

5. Michigan: a 12.9-point lead in the final average; a 0.90-point certified margin (13,792 votes)

CONFIRMED. Michigan Secretary of State, official results (updated 24 Aug 2026, 83/83 counties): winner 744,952, runner-up 731,160, of 1,537,628 party votes. FiftyPlusOne's final average: 51.8 vs 38.9. The miss on the margin is about 12 points; part of it is how undecided voters broke, so the episode says "the margin was off", not "the leader's share was off".

  • https://mvic.sos.state.mi.us/votehistory/Index?type=C&electionDate=8-4-2026
  • https://fiftyplusone.news/polls/senate/democratic-primary/michigan

6. Wisconsin: the last big public poll had the polled leader up about 20; the other candidate won by 3,673 votes

CORRECTED. The certified margin is confirmed (315,413 vs 311,740; 0.46 points). An earlier reading of the poll used its early-voter box (48 vs 14); the whole-sample figures are 44 vs 22, 44 vs 24 and 42 vs 21 (+20 to +22). The episode uses "about 20".

  • https://elections.wi.gov/sites/default/files/documents/August%202026%20Partisan%20Primary_Summary%20Results.pdf
  • https://statenavigate.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/WIDEMPoll2.pdf

7. Wisconsin's late shifts: dropouts, a re-entry and a late endorsement

CONFIRMED. Marquette's poll director: three candidates dropped out and one re-entered in the final month; a late endorsement by the governor; "the vast majority of late-deciding voters went to" the winner. Marquette's final poll (22–27 July) had 34% undecided.

  • https://law.marquette.edu/facultyblog/2026/08/a-look-at-2026-primary-polling-in-wisconsin/

8. Minnesota, the other direction: the final poll had the winner two points behind; she won by 19.6

CONFIRMED. SurveyUSA (29 Jul–4 Aug, 578 likely primary voters, ±5.3): 40 vs 42. Minnesota Secretary of State: 411,853 (59.01%) vs 274,920 (39.39%). A miss of about 22 points, with the wrong leader.

  • https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ElectionAugust.pdf
  • https://www.sos.mn.gov/elections-voting/election-results/2026/2026-primary-election-results/

9. Pew: AI stand-ins differed from real answers by 12 points on average across nearly 300 questions

CONFIRMED. "Across nearly 300 individual survey questions, the estimates produced using our AI respondents differed from their human counterparts by an average of 12 percentage points." More than 15 points on about 28% of questions. Main model: Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6. The three surveys tested held no vote-choice question (from Pew's published questionnaires; Pew does not say this in a sentence).

  • https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really/
  • https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/methodology-silicon-samples/

10. "About 15,000 panelists" in the Pew study

UNRESOLVED. Not found on Pew's pages (one wave had 8,512). Not used on air.

11. AAPOR's AI task force: AI-made data should not be called a "poll" or "survey"

CONFIRMED. "These terms should not be used to describe data created through artificial intelligence." (Responsible AI Integration in Survey Research, May 2026, p.51.)

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Responsible-AI-Integration-In-Survey-Research.pdf

12. Nexstar's debates use a poll test: 10% in a general-election poll, plus $100,000 raised

CORRECTED. An earlier note called the poll "one alternative route" onto the stage. That comes from a 2022 version of Nexstar's rules still online. The current policy: a candidate "must comply with all five sections"; the poll is one of the five, and "if there is no poll available, the rest of the document will govern." WOOD-TV reported the Michigan governor's candidates met "at least 10% on a poll and … at least $100,000". No candidate was found to have been kept off a stage by the poll test.

  • https://www.kron4.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2026/03/Nexstar-Debate-Criteria-2025-002.pdf
  • https://www.nexstar.tv/nexstars-michigan-television-stations-to-air-exclusive-live-debates-between-candidates-for-governor-and-u-s-senate/

13. Real error is about twice the printed margin; 95% intervals covered only 73–88% of results

CONFIRMED. 4,221 polls, 608 state races, 1998–2014: RMSE "approximately 3.5 percentage points, about twice as large as that implied by most reported margins of error"; coverage 73% (Senate), 74% (governor), 88% (presidential). Polls are "not systematically biased toward either party" over that period; errors within a race are correlated.

  • https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/polling-errors.pdf

14. "Democrats and Republicans have each been underestimated about equally often since the 1930s"

CONFIRMED. AAPOR 2024 task-force report, executive summary. The same report says the last three presidential cycles all missed in the same direction and that the cause is unclear; the episode says both.

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AAPOR-Task-Force-on-2024-Pre-Election-Polling_Report.pdf

15. 2024: state presidential polls the most accurate since 1944 (3.0 on the margin); a 2.7-point lean across offices; House ~7

CONFIRMED. AAPOR 2024 report, executive summary and Table 2.1.1.

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AAPOR-Task-Force-on-2024-Pre-Election-Polling_Report.pdf

16. 2020: the largest national miss in 40 years

CONFIRMED, with scope. National only ("highest in 40 years"); 1980 was larger (6.0). State tracking began in 2000. National 4.5 on the margin; state 5.1.

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/AAPOR-Task-Force-on-2020-Pre-Election-Polling_Report-FNL.pdf

17. 2020: errors "did not shrink" in the final three days

CORRECTED. State polls' lean shrank slightly (+4.3 → +4.1 → +3.9); national did not. Not used on air.

18. 2016: national polls missed by 2.2; state polls by 5.1, with the wrong winner in three states

CONFIRMED. AAPOR 2016 report.

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/AAPOR-2016-Election-Polling-Report.pdf

19. Midterms: 2018 overstated Democrats by 0.1; 2022 overstated Republicans by 0.6; races still missed by ~4.6–4.8

CONFIRMED. AAPOR 2024 report (signed errors); AAPOR 2020 report Table 4 (2018 Senate and governor 4.6); AAPOR 2024 Appendix I.3 (2022 Senate 4.8). A single all-office 2022 figure is printed only as a chart; not used.

20. Primary polls: state presidential primaries missed by 7.0–9.3 points per cycle, 2000–2016

CONFIRMED. AAPOR 2016 report, Table 1.

  • https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/AAPOR-2016-Election-Polling-Report.pdf

21. Senate and governor primaries miss by about 13 points

APPROXIMATE. Only one analyst database (FiftyPlusOne) supports it; named on screen if used, never as a settled figure.

  • https://www.gelliottmorris.com/p/2026-08-12-primary-polls

22. Response rates: 36% (1997) to 6% (2018) at Pew; 0.4% for one major poll in 2022

CONFIRMED. Pew 2012 and 2019 pages (Pew's own phone surveys); Pew 2026-09-16 for the 0.4% and "No one approach is clearly superior in all contexts".

  • https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/02/27/response-rates-in-telephone-surveys-have-resumed-their-decline/
  • https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2026/09/16/3-ways-to-correct-a-polls-political-balance-party-affiliation-past-vote-and-voter-file-information/

23. Showing a ±2% margin of error had no effect; an 87% win chance was read as about 70%

CONFIRMED. Westwood, Messing & Lelkes, Journal of Politics 2020 (4,151 respondents); read from the authors' preprint.

  • https://solomonmg.github.io/pdf/aggregator.pdf

24. Plain poll numbers did not move vote intentions in a 23,421-person Dutch experiment

CONFIRMED (abstract). Stressing a party's growth produced a small bandwagon effect. Full text paywalled. No comparable test in a real US election was found; a Swiss referendum working paper finds close polls raised turnout (abstract only; not used as settled).

  • https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/off-the-fence-onto-the-bandwagon-a-large-scale-survey-experiment-/

25. In 2016 policy was 10% of general-election coverage, "less than a fourth the space given to the horserace"

CONFIRMED. Shorenstein Center (Patterson).

  • https://shorensteincenter.org/news-coverage-2016-general-election/

26. Trained newsrooms cut horse-race stories from 27% to 13%

UNRESOLVED. Only a university press release was readable; the paper carries an August 2026 corrigendum we could not read, and another summary reports a different size of change. Not used on air.

27. Transparent pollsters were about 2 points more accurate (538)

APPROXIMATE. 538's own scale (weighted, not raw error), membership classified in 2023. Used only as "tended to be more accurate".

  • https://abcnews.com/538/best-pollsters-america/story?id=105563951

28. France bans publishing election polls from the Saturday before a vote until polls close

CORRECTED (wording). The law starts the ban at 00:00 on the Saturday (midnight Friday night) and ends it when the last mainland polling station closes. In 2017 Belgian and Swiss outlets published election-day exit polls, not pre-election polls.

  • https://www.commission-des-sondages.fr/pdf/Loi77.pdf

29. An Iowa federal judge dismissed a suit calling a pre-election poll "fake news"; the appeal is pending

CORRECTED (grounds). Dismissed 6 Nov 2025 with prejudice for failure to state a claim — no false statement of fact, no deception, no duty — applying the actual-malice standard; the court did not grant absolute immunity. AAPOR's amicus was filed 28 April 2026. Argued 22 Sept 2026; no decision as of 3 Oct.

  • https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69940186/
  • https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72034895/

30. FiftyPlusOne banned one pollster and suspended another on 20 August

CONFIRMED. For altering respondents' answers and for undisclosed campaign work — a different breach from Median's (no respondents at all).

  • https://blog.fiftyplusone.news/p/suspending-two-pollsters-data-falsification-undisclosed-partisan-work

Corrections applied to source

  • AAPOR statement date 25 Aug → 19 Aug (first publication) → the peg and the brief.
  • Nexstar's poll test: "one alternative route" → one of five required sections → the brief.
  • Wisconsin's final poll: early-voter box (48–14) → whole sample (+20 to +22) → the brief.
  • RealClearPolling: "unclear" → excluded, per AP → the brief.
  • The Donnelly ruling's grounds: "First Amendment immunity" → failure to state a claim → the brief.

Fairness audit

  • Misses shown in both directions (2000/2012 vs 2016/2020/2024; 2018 vs 2022; Michigan and Wisconsin vs Minnesota). AAPOR's own caveat about the recent one-way run is stated, not hidden behind "equally often".
  • No live 2026 horse-race number; no candidate as subject; the person behind the fakes is not named.
  • Pew's study uses Claude; it airs as a plain fact at equal weight.
  • Cast rotation: everyone from the previous debate rests; no one holds the seat they held two debates ago. The restrictive rail (gold, "stop calling it news") is argued by a man this episode, after a woman last time; the other rail by a woman. The judge and the desk lead are each in that seat for the first time in this run of episodes.
  • Spoken figures are rounded ("about twelve points", "about one in sixteen"); the exact figures are on screen. No year ranges are spoken.
