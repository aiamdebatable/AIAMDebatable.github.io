  • Corrected

    The New York Times, FiftyPlusOne and RealClearPolling kept the fakes out of their averages

  • Corrected

  • Corrected

The other 23 claims checked
  • Confirmed

    "At least three" fake polls by Median Strategies, in a big-city mayoral race and two governor's races

  • Confirmed

    The firm admitted it on Monday 17 August

  • Confirmed

    Michigan: a 12.9-point lead in the final average; a 0.90-point certified margin (13,792 votes)

  • Confirmed

    Wisconsin's late shifts: dropouts, a re-entry and a late endorsement

  • Confirmed

    Minnesota, the other direction: the final poll had the winner two points behind; she won by 19.6

  • Confirmed

    Pew: AI stand-ins differed from real answers by 12 points on average across nearly 300 questions

  • Unresolved

    "About 15,000 panelists" in the Pew study

  • Confirmed

    AAPOR's AI task force: AI-made data should not be called a "poll" or "survey"

  • Confirmed

    Real error is about twice the printed margin; 95% intervals covered only 73–88% of results

  • Confirmed

    "Democrats and Republicans have each been underestimated about equally often since the 1930s"

  • Confirmed

    2024: state presidential polls the most accurate since 1944 (3.0 on the margin); a 2.7-point lean across offices; House ~7

  • Confirmed

    2020: the largest national miss in 40 years

  • Confirmed

    2016: national polls missed by 2.2; state polls by 5.1, with the wrong winner in three states

  • Confirmed

    Midterms: 2018 overstated Democrats by 0.1; 2022 overstated Republicans by 0.6; races still missed by ~4.6–4.8

  • Confirmed

    Primary polls: state presidential primaries missed by 7.0–9.3 points per cycle, 2000–2016

  • Approximate

    Senate and governor primaries miss by about 13 points

  • Confirmed

    Response rates: 36% (1997) to 6% (2018) at Pew; 0.4% for one major poll in 2022

  • Confirmed

    Showing a ±2% margin of error had no effect; an 87% win chance was read as about 70%

  • Confirmed

    Plain poll numbers did not move vote intentions in a 23,421-person Dutch experiment

  • Confirmed

    In 2016 policy was 10% of general-election coverage, "less than a fourth the space given to the horserace"

  • Unresolved

    Trained newsrooms cut horse-race stories from 27% to 13%

  • Approximate

    Transparent pollsters were about 2 points more accurate (538)

  • Confirmed

    FiftyPlusOne banned one pollster and suspended another on 20 August
