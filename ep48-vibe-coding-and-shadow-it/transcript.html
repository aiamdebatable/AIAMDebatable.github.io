The verdict

KateThere's no winner today. Let me set out what's settled, what each team proved, and then hand you a dial for your own tool.

KateBoth teams accepted this: if a business-built tool leaks, the duty sits with the company that holds the data. The one filing we found involved a bank's customer data in an unapproved AI tool. How many of these apps exist, and whether they're less safe than what they replace, we found no measure.

KateWhat held up for green. Working isn't safe: about one in eight secure in the agent test. Wrong data can hide inside a tool that works, as England's lost Covid cases showed. And when a builder leaves, the platforms' own pages show the company can be left unable to fix it.

KateWhat held up for gold. People build because it's faster and easier, and rarely out of malice. The official advice is tiered, not a ban, and fine for low-risk internal tools. And the alarming numbers measure lab models and staff using unapproved AI, not apps like hers.

KateWhat didn't hold up. That people build mainly because IT is too slow; one survey, mostly of IT managers, ranked that last. That the alarming numbers are about business-built apps. That an app always dies with its builder; in a company workspace it can pass on. And that US bank rules require checking AI-built tools.

KateWhere the record leans: the risk rises with what a tool touches. The UK's cyber agency, the five countries' guidance on agents, Gartner and Microsoft's own rules all draw the line there, and the companies that govern this give every tool that matters an owner who stays.

KateThe dial: how much does your tool touch? Just you, nothing sensitive: the UK agency says that can be fine, though public is still public. Your team and company data: someone else should know it exists. Customers' data, money or logins: a second pair of eyes first. That's where the bank's filing came from.

KateThen the second dial: who looks after it when you leave? So before you build, three checks. Does anyone else know it exists? What does it touch? Who looks after it when I leave? Where you set both dials is your call.
