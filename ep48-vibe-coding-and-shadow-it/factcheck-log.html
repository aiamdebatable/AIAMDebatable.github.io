Fairness audit

  • The record is lopsided in places, and the brief says so. On "who carries the legal duty?" every regime read points one way (the company that holds the data, whoever built the tool), so the brief does not stage a 50/50 there (A5–A7, A17, A27). On "are business-built apps less safe than what they replace?" and "do guardrails work?" nothing is measured, so neither team may claim the answer (B pass, C26–C30, C24). The fight is placed where the evidence leaves it open: how much review a tool needs, given what it touches, and how fast to let building spread.
  • Both sides are argued from the same checked rows. "Let the business build" gets the counter-study (Sandoval, B30), the raw-model limits of the lab tests (B18–B24), the NCSC's "can often be perfectly fine" for limited-exposure internal tools (C31), the platforms' own fixes (B48, B51) and the long-standing audit category for business-built tools (C12). Open questions this episode could not settle

  • Has an app or agent that business staff built (not a chat tool they used) caused a breach, a fine or a filing? The EDGAR full-text search over 8-Ks from 2025 to 2026-10-27 found one AI-linked Item 1.05 filing, CB Financial's, and it is about an employee using an unapproved AI application (A8, A14); no employee was found sued or fired over an app they built (A53). AG actions in Texas, Colorado and Illinois were not searched.
  • Are business-built tools less safe than the spreadsheets, Access databases and off-the-shelf apps they replace? No source compares them. The lab tests measure raw models; the deployed-app evidence is a preprint, vendor scans and a competitor's scan, and none shows the builders were business staff (B18 to B43, B63).
  • Do IT's guardrails reduce incidents? No measured outcome exists for any of them (C26–C30); the "89% drop" when sanctioned tools are provided traces to one vendor report with no stated method (C24).
  • How many people outside engineering build software with AI? No independent count (D14). Census is firm-level and cannot separate engineers (D10); Anthropic's study ranks groups but gives no share and cannot see whether anything built was kept (D12, D15).
  • What does an individual worker risk? Federal hacking law is a poor fit (A34, A36), but Van Buren left open whether policies can draw the line (A35); state computer-crime laws, trade-secret law, duty of loyalty and employment contracts were not researched.
  • Does either IBM year separate pasting data into a chatbot from tools staff built? No (B1, B6).
  • Does the S-1 quantify non-technical adoption anywhere? Not in any passage the full-text search reached (P4), but the filing has not been read cover to cover (A54).
  • New York's SHIELD penalty amount (A21, GBL 350-d not read), and whether Item 1.05 filings say how attackers got in (A16).
  • Did Gartner's 2009 "a quarter of new business apps by 2014" come true? Never measured (C38).
