## Fairness audit
- **The record is lopsided in places, and the brief says so.** On "who carries the legal duty?" every
  regime read points one way (the company that holds the data, whoever built the tool), so the brief
  does not stage a 50/50 there (A5–A7, A17, A27). On "are business-built apps less safe than what they
  replace?" and "do guardrails work?" nothing is measured, so neither team may claim the answer (B
  pass, C26–C30, C24). The fight is placed where the evidence leaves it open: how much review a tool
  needs, given what it touches, and how fast to let building spread.

## Open questions this episode could not settle
- Has an app or agent that business staff **built** (not a chat tool they used) caused a breach, a fine
  or a filing? The EDGAR full-text search over 8-Ks from 2025 to 2026-10-27 found one AI-linked Item
  1.05 filing, CB Financial's, and it is about an employee **using** an unapproved AI application (A8,
  A14); no employee was found sued or fired over an app they built (A53). AG actions in Texas, Colorado
  and Illinois were not searched.
- Are business-built tools less safe than the spreadsheets, Access databases and off-the-shelf apps they
  replace? No source compares them. The lab tests measure raw models; the deployed-app evidence is a
  preprint, vendor scans and a competitor's scan, and none shows the builders were business staff (B18
  to B43, B63).

  drop" when sanctioned tools are provided traces to one vendor report with no stated method (C24).

  cannot see whether anything built was kept (D12, D15).
