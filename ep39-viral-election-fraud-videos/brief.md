# Research brief — Viral Election-Fraud Videos: What Did They Really Show?

_The full research behind the episode. This is the "full research" we link below the video. It is an
EXPLAINER built as case files: one case per widely shared clip or story, each set against what the
investigation, audit or court found, then the skill a viewer can use on the next one. Every figure here is
in the fact-check log._

## The question

Viral election-fraud videos: what did they really show, and how can you tell?

Most people never watch a ballot get counted. What they see is a clip: boxes coming out from under a
table, papered-over windows, a truck, a person at a drop box at night. The process, followed as written, is
checked over and over. The hard part is the "evidence" people are handed to look at and argue about. This
episode takes the most-shared clips and stories from 2020 and 2024, from every side, and opens the record
on each one. It also says plainly where something real did happen, and where real fraud was proven.

This is the follow-up to "How Your Ballot Gets Counted" (ep30). That episode showed the process. This one
tests the evidence.

## Why now

**Georgia's early voting opens on October 13, 2026, and the most famous clip of 2020 is back in a
courtroom.** In January 2026 the FBI seized Fulton County's 2020 ballots, the county where the
"ballots from under the table" footage was filmed. The warrant was signed and executed on
**January 28, 2026**, and the FBI's own inventory lists **653 boxes**. The unsealed affidavit says, in its own
words, that "some of those allegations have been disproven while some of those allegations have been
substantiated, including through admissions by Fulton County." The affidavit swears there is probable
cause that "unknown persons" violated two federal laws, a records-retention misdemeanor (52 U.S.C. § 20701)
and a felony against tabulating ballots "known by the person to be materially false, fictitious, or
fraudulent" (§ 20511). It describes the investigation as into "whether any of the improprieties were
intentional acts," and its own conclusion is conditional: "If these deficiencies were the result of
intentional action…" That is the agent's sworn claim; the only finding by a judge is probable cause to search.
(Corrected 2026-10-19, re-check #19: the earlier wording fused the two statements.)
([affidavit for the executed warrant, N.D. Ga., Doc. 22-2](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.gand.355087/gov.uscourts.gand.355087.22.2_2.pdf);
[CBS News, 2026-02-10](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fulton-county-fbi-search-court-records-2020-election/))

On **May 6, 2026** a federal judge refused the county's request to get the records back. The test was a
"callous disregard" bar, which is very high. The judge called the affidavit "defective in some respects" and
said one figure in it was presented in a misleading way. He also wrote that it was not a case where an
officer "intentionally lied." No charges had been reported when this research was done.

Then, on **September 30, 2026**, the State Election Board's executive director sent letters asking the

the board never authorised what the letters asked for. The letters demand that a QR-code ban "effective July 1,

counting is lawful this November. On **October 8** the county election officials' association asked the board
to withdraw the request. Its letter warned: "Repeated calls telling Georgians their voting system is
compromised, days before advance voting, tells them that their vote may not count."
([Votebeat, 2026-10-04](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/10/04/georgia-election-board-mills-federal-letters-dhs-doj-board-approval/);

**Updated 2026-10-13 (top-up pass):** there are still no charges in the Fulton investigation. A federal
grand-jury subpoena for the details of every 2020 Fulton count worker was quashed on July 7, 2026, and the

**Split:** the description carries the hot peg: early voting opening while federal agents hold the 2020
ballots and the board's letters circulate. The script carries the structural fact. Six years on, the

separates what was disproven from what was real. The script names the county, the dates and the documents.
It does not make any person or party the subject.

## How the cases were chosen, and where the list leans

Cases were chosen by how widely they spread and how often they still come up. They come from every side:
2020 claims from the losing side, 2020 and 2024 claims from the other side, and fabricated footage made
by a foreign operation. ⚠ Reach is mostly **unmeasured**. Only two clips have a view count from a solid
outlet: Bucks County ("hundreds of thousands of views" on X) and the Georgia "Haitian voters" video ("over
half a million views"). For the rest, the selection rests on the record of who pushed the claim and where
(a hearing, a press conference, a film, a court case), not on numbers.

**The list leans where the record leans, and that is hard one way.** Almost every viral clip shows
ordinary procedure that looks sinister without the rulebook, or footage that was faked. But "it was all
debunked" is also wrong. Underneath the clips there are three different kinds of real thing:

1. **Real sloppiness that was not theft.** This is Fulton 2020: repeated scans, missing images, a monitor
   out of the room. It also covers Wisconsin's guidance that broke statute, and Arizona's
   chain-of-custody paperwork.
2. **Real but small fraud, almost always one person acting alone.** Four dead-voter ballots in Georgia,
   all cast by relatives. In Utah, 13 noncitizens who had voted, out of about 2.07 million registration
   records checked.
3. **Proven organised fraud, twice.** North Carolina's 9th District in 2018 was overturned by a 5–0
   bipartisan board. Bridgeport, Connecticut's 2023 mayoral primary was voided by a judge. Neither was
   exposed by a viral clip. Both were caught by records, investigators and, in Bridgeport, by
   continuous camera footage that someone subpoenaed.

## The case files

Trays (ruled 2026-10-13): **EXPLAINED** = the record breaks the claim as shared. **PROVEN** = real fraud or a
real error, with its scale. **OPEN** = a named gap in the record, with the evidence that would settle it and
who holds it. **NOT CHECKABLE** = no record can test it. "Partly" marks a case where something real happened,
but not what the clip said. On screen the town's grammar holds: EXPLAINED and PROVEN are SOLID (settled);
OPEN is GLASS (still argued); NOT CHECKABLE gets its own look. The full sort is in § The four trays, below.

### 1. "Suitcases of ballots pulled from under a table" — State Farm Arena, Fulton County, Georgia, election night 2020 — **EXPLAINED**
- **As shared:** workers sent observers home, then pulled hidden "suitcases" of ballots out from under a
  table and counted them in secret. (Many people, including our host, remember it as Wisconsin. The
  clip most people remember was filmed in Georgia.)
- **What the footage shows:** at about 10 p.m., with the room full of people including official monitors
  and the media, workers put ballots that had already been opened but not yet counted into ballot
  carriers. They sealed the carriers and stored them under the table, because they thought they were done
  for the night. Video from that morning shows the table coming into the room with nothing under it.
  Counting resumed at 11:03 p.m.
- **What the record found:** the Secretary of State's investigators, the GBI and the FBI reviewed the
  full footage and found no hidden or outside ballots. The State Election Board dismissed the "poll
  worker fraud" case in June 2023. The statewide hand count of about 5 million paper ballots matched the
  machines to within 0.1053% of votes. That is about ten times closer than the 1–1.5% error the state
  expects from people counting by hand. The two election workers seen in the clip won about $148 million
  in a defamation verdict, and a court declared the statements about them false.
- ⚠ Never say "suitcases": the record says ballot carriers or bins. Nobody on this research team has
  watched the footage itself, so the script must not describe colours or shapes.

### 2. "Observers were thrown out" — the same night — **EXPLAINED, partly** (the stop was real; no order to leave is shown; the instruction itself is NOT CHECKABLE)
- **Real:** a stop was announced to the room. The observers and press left around 10:46 p.m. believing
  counting was over. It resumed 17 minutes later without them. The State Election Board's own monitor was
  out of the room from about 8:15 to about 11:52 p.m. (a fact-checker's interview with the monitor;
  attribute it).
- **Not real:** an order to leave. In their January 2021 interviews, both party watchers said no one told
  them to leave. Their earlier affidavits differ, and the brief says so. Nothing was counted in secret
  that the later hand count did not see.

### 3. "The same stack scanned over and over" — **EXPLAINED as fraud; PROVEN as sloppiness**
- The clip shows a standard rescan after a scanner jam. As fraud, that is EXPLAINED.
- **But real double-scanning happened.** The state's review board found that about 200 ballots in two
  batches were double-scanned in Fulton's initial count. The 2020 recount also left 3,075 repeated ballot
  **images**, found by the State Election Board's 2024 investigation. The state could not confirm that any
  of them were counted, and 1,806 were machine-printed ballots that cannot be told apart from identical
  votes. The board reprimanded the county.

### 4. Fulton was badly run, not shown to be rigged — **PROVEN sloppiness; OPEN on intent** (and the peg)
- The court-ordered monitor, the state's performance review board, the 2024 investigation, Fulton's own
  admissions and the 2026 affidavit all describe disorganisation and record-keeping failures. In 2020,
  Georgia did not require counties to keep ballot images; a 2021 law changed that.
- The 2026 affidavit is built on these real failures. It does not say fraud was proven. Its "40%" figure
  is about the repeated scans: 40% of the duplicates went to the losing candidate, against a share the
  analyst put at about 30% (the state's hand count gives 26.2%). Most of the duplicates still went to the
  winner, and the analyst himself called the pattern "not partisan."
- The judge's May 2026 order is about returning property, not a finding of fraud, and not a finding that
  the agent lied. Both camps' victory laps go further than the documents.

### 5. "Thousands of dead people voted" — Georgia 2020 — **EXPLAINED as "thousands"; PROVEN as four**
- The claims ran from 5,000 to 10,315. The Secretary of State's sworn testimony in June 2022 put the
  confirmed count at **four**, all cast by relatives. That is about 1 in 1.25 million ballots. The 10,315
  came from matching on birth year only.
- ⚠ A new, unpublished federal claim of "more than 42,000 deceased registrants" on Georgia's 2026 rolls is
  likely to circulate. It is about names on rolls, not ballots, and its method is not published.

### 6. "The rolls are full of dead and moved people, so the election is fake" — **PROVEN on the rolls, EXPLAINED on the leap**
- Stale entries are real and lawful by design. Federal law makes states wait two federal elections before
  removing someone who may have moved. In 2024 the rolls held 234.5 million registrations against 158.2
  million ballots counted. States removed 21.3 million records between 2022 and 2024, about 4.6 million of
  them because the voter had died.
- A roll entry is not a ballot. The gap is mostly people who did not vote.
- The Justice Department's 2025–26 suits for states' unredacted voter rolls: 31 filed, 26 dismissed, none
  won. Those rulings are about the department's legal power to demand the data. They are not about whether
  the rolls are clean.
- **"States are hiding their rolls" — EXPLAINED as hiding; PROVEN that three private identifiers were withheld (top-up T2).** In most states the voter roll itself is public
  or for sale, with names, addresses and voting history. Maryland quoted $125 for it and West Virginia $500,
  and Homeland Security bought public files itself. What the refusing states withheld were three private
  identifiers their own laws protect: full date of birth, driver's licence number, and the last four digits
  of the Social Security number. The department's letters said the purpose was checking list maintenance;
  a department email to one state described "a review for noncitizens and dead voters via DHS." About 16 to
  18 states handed over the full file. Refusals crossed party lines: states led by both parties refused,
  citing the same privacy laws. Where federal checks were run, the numbers shrank on review. In Nevada,
  15,903 flagged became 185 confirmed, by Homeland Security's own account. The department has published no
  findings of its own. On October 9, 2026 a federal court vacated the program nationwide.

### 7. "Noncitizens are voting" — **PROVEN in small numbers; EXPLAINED as "millions"**
- Utah checked about 2.07 million records: 27 confirmed noncitizens registered, 13 of whom had voted. Utah
  itself warns its data was "not reliable in indicating an individual was not a citizen." Georgia's 2024
  audit found 20 registered out of 8.2 million; how many had voted is disputed between secondary sources.
  Georgia (September 2026) lists 432 *potential* noncitizens who voted at some point since 1996. In Texas,
  2,724 were flagged, 578 of them later shown to be citizens, and 117 referred.
- Always say registered vs voted, and potential vs confirmed.
- **Top-up T5 (the full record):**
  - Every state that checked found noncitizens on the rolls, in dozens to low hundreds. Most were lawful
    residents, often put there by government error, not people without legal status.
  - Who they were: in Reuters' federal cases, 49 of the 62 defendants whose status was known held green
    cards. All 41 North Carolina 2016 cases were lawfully present. In Utah, 19 of 27 were lawful residents.
  - How they got on the rolls: motor-voter systems registered them in Pennsylvania and Oregon. Arizona had
    218,000 voters with no proof of citizenship on file because of a driver's-licence coding error; only 2
    known noncitizens had turned up among them as of October 2024. In New Jersey, a software error registered
    about 6,600, fewer than 400 of whom voted, and the state found and disclosed it itself.
  - Rates: Iowa 2024 is the honest high end, at 35 ballots, about 21 per million. North Carolina 2016 was
    about 8.5 per million, Michigan 2024 about 2.8.
  - Flag lists shrink on review. Iowa flagged 2,176 and confirmed 277. Texas in 2019 flagged 95,000; about
    25,000 were already naturalized citizens, and the list was rescinded. Alabama in 2024 flagged 3,251, at
    least 2,074 of them eligible voters.
  - Kansas measured both sides of proof-of-citizenship rules: they blocked 31,089 applicants and stopped 28
    noncitizen attempts.
  - The 2014 survey estimate that 6.4% of noncitizens voted was rebutted as survey error by the survey's own
    principal investigators.
  - Some clips of "noncitizens voting" may show lawful local voting: D.C., 17 Maryland towns, San Francisco
    and Oakland school boards, and three Vermont cities allow it.
  - Trays: "millions of illegals voting": EXPLAINED. "Noncitizens are on the rolls": PROVEN, in small numbers.
    "Noncitizens are voting": PROVEN, rarely. "Cleaning the rolls disenfranchises citizens": PROVEN in part, because it
    depends on the method. Blanket rules and purges from stale data burden hundreds of citizens per
    noncitizen found; checks that review each person do not.
  - Topic #107 (proof of citizenship to register) owns the policy fight. This episode uses the record.

### 8. Detroit, TCF Center 2020: "observers locked out, windows covered, a 4 a.m. ballot dump" — **EXPLAINED as fraud; PROVEN as rule-bending at the doors**
- **Real, and shown first:** windows were papered over and challengers from both parties were held at the
  doors. A Republican-led state Senate committee said those acts were "not always lawful or wise."
- **EXPLAINED as fraud:** the "dump" was about 16,000 Election Day absentee ballots delivered by van. The
  committee, the state audit and the court found no fraud. Philadelphia had the same observer fight, and the
  state Supreme Court held that the election code sets no minimum distance.
- **"Detroit had more votes than voters" (top-up T3):** after the canvass, 95 of Detroit's 134 absentee
  counting boards were out of balance, meaning the poll book and the ballot count did not match. That is
  real, and it is why the county canvassers first deadlocked. The state audit got it down to 14 boards,
  each off by 5 or fewer. The net was **21 more names than ballots out of about 174,000**, which is the
  opposite direction from "more votes than voters." Tray: EXPLAINED on "more votes than voters," PROVEN on "the
  books didn't balance." Under Michigan's rules, an out-of-balance precinct *can be barred* from a recount.

### 9. "A truck of filled-in ballots driven from New York to Pennsylvania" — October 2020 — **EXPLAINED**
- A joint investigation by the Postal Service Inspector General, the Postal Inspection Service and the FBI
  traced the trailer. It held Priority Mail parcels, its seal matched, and every trailer named was accounted
  for. The only big ballot flow from New York to Pennsylvania was BLANK ballots, printed in Rochester for two
  Pennsylvania counties. The viral 250,000–300,000 is about 2,000 times the roughly 150 ballot images in the
  facility's log for the whole season.

### 10. "2000 Mules": drop-box video plus phone tracking — **EXPLAINED**
- The best-known clips show family members returning their own household's ballots, which is legal in
  Georgia. Georgia's investigators said the phone data did not reach probable cause. In the voter's
  defamation case, the film-maker's own December 2024 filing said the drop-box videos were never linked to
  phone location data. The publisher apologised and pulled the film in May 2024. The case settled in July 2026.

### 11. Bridgeport, Connecticut, 2023 mayoral primary — **PROVEN** (what real stuffing looks like on camera)
- Police drop-box video, entered in court without objection, showed party workers making multiple drops of
  multiple ballots. A judge voided the primary, which had been decided by 251 votes. The state's
  election-enforcement commission referred cases, and prosecutors charged nine people over the 2023 cycle.
  The trials are pending.
- **What was proven** is illegal handling of real voters' absentee ballots by people not allowed to touch
  them. That voids the ballots under Connecticut law, whether or not any vote was faked. It is not a finding
  of fake ballots.
- **The lesson:** a camera that recorded all the time, footage obtained by subpoena, and numbers that did
  not reconcile. The absentee pool was nearly ten times the margin.

### 12. North Carolina's 9th District, 2018 — **PROVEN** (organised fraud)
- A paid absentee-ballot operation in Bladen and Robeson counties. The bipartisan State Board voted 5–0 for a
  new election. In the two counties' parts of the district, 1,364 mail-in absentee votes were cast, against
  a 905-vote margin. It was caught through absentee request and return data, interviews and subpoenas.
  The operative died before trial, others pleaded to misdemeanours, and the candidate was not charged.

### 13. Paterson, New Jersey, 2020 all-mail council election — **PROVEN as a broken election; criminal fraud not proven**
- A judge voided the race as "rife with mail in vote procedural violations." About 19% of mail ballots were
  rejected and 347 bundled ballots were disqualified. But the criminal cases collapsed (one dismissal is
  under appeal), and no conviction was found. "19% rejected" is not "19% fraud."

### 14. Bucks County, Pennsylvania "ripped-up mail ballots" video — October 24, 2024 — **EXPLAINED (fabricated)**
- Staged with props, not AI. The county said within hours that the envelope and materials were not its own.
  The district attorney and police called it fabricated the same day. The next day, US agencies said Russian
  actors made and spread it. The agencies disclosed no method; what a viewer can check is the envelope and
  the county's own statement.

### 15. Georgia "Haitian voters" video — October 31, 2024 — **EXPLAINED (fabricated)**
- The names in it returned no Georgia registration. Four "licences" showed the same photo and lacked the
  marks real Georgia licences carry. Georgia's Secretary of State called it false and probably foreign, and
  US agencies said it was Russian-made. It had over half a million views. An Arizona "overseas ballots" video
  days later was a staged interview.

### 16. "The machines flipped votes" — **EXPLAINED on flipping; PROVEN on vulnerabilities and breaches**
- In Antrim County, Michigan, the wrong election-night numbers came from a human error in loading the cards.
  The scanners' own paper tapes were right, and the hand count moved 12 votes out of about 16,000.
- Wisconsin's 2024 hand-count audit found a 0.0% machine error rate across 327,230 ballots. Georgia's 2024
  audit found zero QR-versus-text differences across 5,025,863 machine-printed ballots.
- A Delaware judge ruled a network's machine claims false, and the network paid $787.5 million.
- **The real "partly":** the federal cyber agency listed nine vulnerabilities in the model Georgia uses,
  and said there was "no evidence" any were exploited. A federal judge called the security concerns
  "substantial" before dismissing the case on standing; it is on appeal. The same judge called the
  unauthorised access to election equipment in Coffee County, Georgia, after the 2020 election a "serious
  security breach." "Vulnerable, not shown exploited, and the
  paper matched" is the call. "All debunked" is not.

### 17. Arizona 2020: "Sharpiegate" and the Senate's contractor review — **EXPLAINED, with PROVEN paperwork gaps**
- Sharpies were officially issued and could not cancel votes. The contractor's own hand count largely
  matched the official result. From all its lists, the Attorney General's office sent one accidental
  deceased-voter case and two double-voting cases for prosecution. Real but separate: missing
  chain-of-custody paperwork for large batches.

### 18. From the other side, 2020: "the Postal Service pulled sorting machines to stop mail ballots" — **PROVEN removals and slowdown; EXPLAINED as targeting ballots**
- Removals really did spike: 712 machines, against about 375 a year. Service fell about 10 points in July.
  The Inspector General tied both to cost-cutting, not to the election. Election mail then ran better than
  other mail (93.8% on time vs 88.1%), and all 760 delayed election pieces found in election week reached
  offices by Election Day.

### 19. From the other side, 2024: "20 million missing votes", "Starlink flipped votes" — **EXPLAINED**
- The certified gap between the two elections was 6.25 million votes, not 20 million. The 20 million came
  from early, partial counts, and both camps used it. The counting machines were not on the internet. A
  federal court dismissed every request to recount or audit the 2024 result, on standing. Ten of ten experts
  Votebeat consulted doubted the "anomalies" model, which found "fraud" in simulated data built to have none.
  Keep this short: padding it to match 2020 would be false balance.

### 20. Wisconsin, the real viral items — mixed
- The clip people remember as Wisconsin was filmed in Georgia. No Wisconsin "ballots under the table" clip
  was found, but absence of search results is not proof, so the script says "the clip most people remember
  was filmed in Georgia," not "there was none."
- Wisconsin's own items: the 2020 "found votes at 4 a.m." jump (EXPLAINED: Milwaukee reported its absentee count
  in one batch). The 2024 trucks at Milwaukee's count (EXPLAINED: legal absentee ballots). Unlocked tabulator
  doors in 2024, after which 31,000 ballots were recounted (PROVEN as an error, no tampering). And a 2022
  Madison clip of a poll worker initialling ballots, which was relabelled as "cheating in Philadelphia."
  That last one is the best "check the place" teaching case in the research. The state's audit bureau found
  real non-compliance in 2020: guidance that broke statute, 3 unsigned certificates out of 14,710 sampled,
  and 11 ballots from voters who died after their ballot was mailed. That is non-compliance, not theft.

### 21. "Bags of ballots show up at 2–4 a.m." — **EXPLAINED (the timing is real and comes from the rules)** (top-up T3)
- Pennsylvania and Wisconsin still bar opening mail ballots before Election Day, so large cities count them
  late and report them in big batches.
- Mail and in-person ballots lean differently. In Philadelphia 2020, the same candidate took 92.1% of the
  mail vote and 71.4% of the Election Day vote. A late mail batch leans one way, and where mail is counted
  first, the early count leans the other way.
- Milwaukee's famous 2020 batch landed at 3:26–3:44 a.m. and split 82.5% to 17.5%, not 100 to 0.

### 22. "10,000+ votes for one candidate and zero for the other" — **EXPLAINED** (top-up T3)
- Michigan's "138,339 votes, zero for the other side" was a county clerk's typo: an extra zero (153,710
  instead of 15,371). The bad figure was 2.76 times the county's registered voters, and it was fixed in
  about 40 minutes.
- A sworn affidavit's "spikes cast solely for one candidate," totalling 384,733 ballots, gives the runner-up
  33.5% in its own chart.
- One-sided precincts are real, and they happen on both sides. Philadelphia 2012 had 50 divisions with zero
  votes for one candidate (59 was the election-night figure). A Rockland County, NY district went 552–0 the
  other way in 2024, and that was cited as proof of hacking.
- The fraud signature in published research is turnout near 100% *together with* near-100% for one side,
  across many units at once. The real U.S. red flag was NC-9's unreturned mail ballots: 45% in Bladen and
  66% in Robeson, against about a quarter across the district. Benford's-law tests, used widely in 2020, do
  no better than a coin toss.
- **Over 100% turnout:** the Michigan affidavit's list was Minnesota townships. Its revised list put Detroit
  at 139.29%; the real figure was 50.88%. Wisconsin's claim used a 2018 registration count. Pennsylvania's
  "202,377 more votes than voters" compared certified ballots with a voter database counties were still
  filling in; whether a remaining gap ever closed is unresolved.
- A real counting error, in the other direction: Prince William County, Virginia certified a 2020 count
  that was off by 3,975 on the margin, with the errors going toward the runner-up. It was caught when two
  precincts showed identical totals.

### 23. Pennsylvania's undated mail ballots — **EXPLAINED as fraud; PROVEN as a real fight over a paperwork rule** (top-up T4)
- The law says the voter must "fill out, date and sign" the envelope. Counties never use that date to judge
  timeliness: ballots are stamped on arrival, and the deadline is 8 p.m. on Election Day. The one fraud case
  on record (Lancaster, 2022) was a ballot that *was* dated, twelve days after the voter died, and the voter
  database caught it.
- About 10,657 ballots were set aside over the date in 2022, out of over 1 million mail ballots.
- In the 2024 Senate recount, three county boards voted to count 1,513 undated ballots despite the state
  Supreme Court's order, against a margin of about 15,115. The court ordered them not to, they complied, and
  none were counted. The viral "precedent doesn't matter" clip of a commissioner was about provisional
  ballots, at a different meeting, and she apologised.
- **For November 2026 the rule is reversed.** A federal court struck down the date requirement and the Third
  Circuit affirmed. State guidance tells counties to keep these ballots separate and count them. An appeal
  to the U.S. Supreme Court is pending, so a 2024 clip watched now can make lawful counting look like
  defiance.
- Trays: "officials voted to count ballots the court excluded": PROVEN. "They counted illegal ballots":
  PROVEN in part (invalid under a paperwork rule, not fake, and in the end not counted). "Undated ballots are
  fraud" or "it swung the race": EXPLAINED. "Pennsylvania tossed legal votes" in 2022–24: PROVEN in part.

### 24. "Proven fraud gets swept under the rug" — **PROVEN in part (follow-through under-covered); EXPLAINED as suppression** (top-up T1)
- **Real:** proven fraud happens regularly. Heritage logs 55 cases for 2024–25. Organised schemes were proven
  or charged in Atlantic City, Millbourne (PA), Frisco City (AL), Hamtramck (MI) and Frio County (TX). Three
  local elections were voided or rerun. None came near a statewide or federal result.
- **Real:** the follow-through is under-covered. Of 12 cases tested, all had local coverage and 9 reached
  AP, but pleas and sentences got far less national attention than arrests, and that cuts both ways.
  Walk-backs are much quieter than the original claims (Texas's 95,000 in 2019; Alabama's 3,251 in 2024).
- **Not shown:** that true fraud stories are suppressed. Articles do disappear: about 70 about the Fulton
  election workers came down after a defamation settlement, and there were network retractions and a pulled
  film. In every case found, the story that vanished had turned out to be false.
- **2026 so far:** six Justice Department releases since September 4 name 57 people, mostly for noncitizen
  voting. All are charged and none convicted. Even if every charge stands, that is about 1 in 2.8 million
  ballots. In Maricopa, 52 of 60 flagged voters were citizens.
- **Unknowable:** undetected fraud. "Rare" describes what has been found. Mesa County, Colorado shows the
  system catching most but not all of a real case: signature checks stopped 12 of 16 forged ballots, and 3
  were counted.

### 25. Wisconsin 2020: "we went to bed with one side ahead and woke up to the other" — **EXPLAINED as fraud; PROVEN rule breaks** (top-up T7)
- **The night:** one side led by more than 100,000 for most of it. After 3:30 a.m. the city of Milwaukee
  reported 169,541 absentee ballots at once, 84% to the eventual winner, then Green Bay and Kenosha. It
  wasn't only Milwaukee: the suburbs moved too. Ozaukee's margin for the losing side fell from 19 points to
  about 11, and Waukesha's from 27 to 21.
- **The checks:** the losing campaign's paid recount of Milwaukee and Dane counties (about $2.4 million in
  actual cost) moved the margin by 87 votes, to 20,682. The state Supreme Court ruled 4–3; the dissent was
  about rules, not fraud. The conservative Wisconsin Institute for Law & Liberty found "no evidence of
  widespread voter fraud" and "no unexplained ballot dump", along with "nearly 300" ineligible voters, and
  found that votes not meeting the legal requirements exceeded the margin.
- **Rules bent (PROVEN):** care-facility guidance that broke the statute (the commission voted for it
  unanimously, Republican appointees included); drop boxes, ruled illegal in 2022 and legal again in 2024;
  clerks completing witness addresses. **OPEN:** the statewide count of care-facility ballots.
- The special counsel hired to investigate said decertifying would change nothing. He was later held in
  contempt and fired by the Speaker who hired him.

### 26. "81 million in 2020, far fewer in 2024: where did the votes come from, and where did they go?" — **EXPLAINED** (top-up T8)
- **Both sides set records in 2020:** 81,283,501 and 74,223,975, against the old record of 69,498,516 in 2008.
  Turnout was 66.4% of eligible voters, the highest since 1900. Mail ballots went from 33.1 million to
  69.3 million.
- **About 89% of the larger 2020 gain** came from things that lifted both sides: population growth, the
  turnout surge and third-party voters coming back. About 1.6 million was a swing between the two sides.
  Causal studies found mail voting raised turnout modestly and favoured neither party.
- **The host's hypothesis, tested by a method fixed in writing first:** PARTLY supported. The 2020 winner's
  side's gain is a record, and by raw votes the only outlier since 1980. Measured as a share of eligible
  voters, it edged the old record by 0.06 points, inside the normal statistical range. The other side's
  totals swing about twice as much historically, so "the other side is steady" does not hold.
- **The 2024 drop (6.27 million) is ordinary in size.** It came almost entirely from states where nobody
  alleged fraud: in the seven battlegrounds the side's total moved −0.3%; elsewhere −9.4%. Studies matching
  surveys to voter records find 15% of that side's 2020 voters stayed home in 2024, against 11% for the
  other side, and new voters leaned to the 2024 winner.

### 27. Nevada 2020 — **EXPLAINED as claimed; PROVEN small; five narrow OPEN items** (top-up T10)
- Margin 33,596. The court found "no credible or reliable evidence"; the state Supreme Court affirmed
  unanimously. The Republican Secretary of State's 2021 review found the state party's 122,918 "records"
  were 3,963 unique reports, and 3,950 of those were one complaint printed 3,950 times. Of 1,506 "dead
  voters", 10 were dead and were referred.
- PROVEN: a man who said publicly that someone had stolen his dead wife's vote had cast it himself, and
  pleaded guilty; a voter who tried to vote in two states was convicted.
- OPEN: the signature-checking machine's false-accept rate (about 30% of envelopes approved with no human
  look); 4,057 voters once flagged at the DMV for immigration papers; whether out-of-state-address voters
  voted twice; and the outcomes of the remaining referrals.

### 28. Pennsylvania 2020 — **EXPLAINED as claimed; PROVEN small** (top-up T11)
- Margin 80,555. The losing campaign's lawyer told the federal court "this is not a fraud case." The appeals
  court: "Charges require specific allegations and then proof. We have neither here."
- Claimed vs found: "8,021 dead voters" in Philadelphia, against testimony from the city's only Republican
  commissioner that there was not "evidence of 8"; "1.8 million sent, 2.5 million counted" was a primary
  figure (3.08 million were sent); the Erie backdating claim found 0 backdated ballots; Delaware County's
  "47 USB drives" were programming drives, all accounted for.
- PROVEN: 3 illegal 2020 ballots convicted (a dead mother's, a son's, one person voting in two states); 9
  military ballots discarded by mistake in Luzerne; 9,428 late ballots set aside under a real legal fight,
  never needed.

### 29. Arizona 2022 governor's race — **EXPLAINED as stolen or sabotage; PROVEN flawed Election Day** (top-up T6)
- PROVEN: about 70 of 223 Maricopa vote centers needed troubleshooting when printers couldn't fuse toner onto
  long ballots; 16,724 ballots went to secure "Door 3" boxes and all were counted. An independent
  investigation led by a former state chief justice found "the primary cause of the election day failures
  was equipment failure."
- EXPLAINED: sabotage and "stolen" claims lost two trials and the appeals; a "35,563 ballots added" claim drew
  a sanction as "unequivocally false." Margin: 17,117.
- PROVEN, official misconduct: Cochise County supervisors refused to certify until a court ordered it; one
  pleaded guilty. For contrast, the Attorney General race recount moved 511 to 280, from human counting error.

### 30. "Stolen" claims from the other side — **EXPLAINED** (top-up T6)
- Ohio 2004 (machines and exit polls): rejected by the losing party's own report and by the pollsters; a
  real recount-rigging crime in Cuyahoga County (negligent misconduct, convictions in 2007) changed nothing.
  2016 "hacked tallies": the Senate Intelligence Committee "has seen no evidence that any votes were
  changed"; Russian scanning of registration systems is a separate, proven thread. Georgia 2018: the court
  found two real defects and ruled for the state. Florida 2000: who "really" won depends on a counting
  standard nobody adopted (NOT CHECKABLE).
- Belief cuts both ways: in December 2016, half of one side's voters believed Russia changed the vote
  tallies, and six in ten of the other side's believed millions voted illegally.

### 31. "The Postal Service won't deliver ballots to states that don't hand over their rolls" — **EXPLAINED as shared; PROVEN rule, blocked** (top-up T6)
- A real rule, but not as shared. The Postmaster General testified to a proposed rule in June 2026, and the
  final rule (August 26, 2026) refuses outgoing ballot mailings without a list of the people a state mails
  ballots to. That is a mailing list, not the voter roll; it applies to every state, not only the ones that
  refused; and it does not cover returned ballots, military and overseas ballots, or primaries.
- Blocked for November 3, 2026: two district courts enjoined it, an appeals court refused to lift that, and
  the Supreme Court denied a stay. The question for future elections is a legal one, still in court.

### 32. The company formerly called Dominion, in 2026 — **EXPLAINED as rigging; PROVEN vulnerabilities and errors** (top-up T9)
- Sold in October 2025 and renamed Liberty Vote; the same machines and certifications. No order ending
  voting machines was issued. DHS made grants conditional on a plan to move off QR-code ballots plus 5% hand
  audits.
- The July 2026 declassification's own intelligence memo says vote-counting systems "would be difficult to
  manipulate" at scale and that audits and paper trails "very likely would uncover" an attempt.
- PROVEN: a lab teardown of Puerto Rico's machines found real, unpatched flaws (no sign any were used); a
  ballot-programming error on Puerto Rico's 2024 status ballot; a vendor-reported error in Newton County,
  Texas, followed by a recount; defamation payouts ($787.5 million; $67 million). OPEN, narrowly: whether
  anyone intruded on the Puerto Rico machines at all (the report is unpublished).

### 33. "Could one side's officials change a presidential result?" — **EXPLAINED for 2020** (top-up T7)
- About 93% of 2020 votes had a paper record; 31 states plus DC audit before certifying; boards and poll
  workers are bipartisan in the decisive states; about 10,000 jurisdictions count votes. Flipping Georgia,
  Arizona and Wisconsin together would have produced a 269–269 tie, not a win.
- Insiders have changed counts (Clay County, KY; a Philadelphia election judge; Ohio's 2004 recount; NC-9).
  Every case was local and was caught. In 2024, the same systems and mostly the same officials certified the
  other side's wins.

### 34. "So many articles can't all be fake" — **EXPLAINED** (top-up T7)
- Three different things get called "evidence": an article reporting a claim, a sworn affidavit (signed under
  oath, never tested), and a finding by an investigator or court. Courts weighing the 2020 affidavits found
  them "only impressive for their volume"; of 64 cases, about 30 had a hearing on the evidence and one minor
  case was won.
- The people who checked were often from the side making the claim: in the record held here, every reviewer
  with a party label was Republican or Republican-led (the Attorney General at the time; officials or reviews
  in Georgia, Michigan, Arizona, Nevada and Wisconsin).
- Repetition makes claims feel true: one earlier exposure roughly doubled the share of people who rated a
  fake headline as accurate (38 to 73 of 949).

## The four trays

**OPEN (15 narrow items; each has a named gap, the evidence that would settle it, and who holds it).** The
two biggest: Fulton 2020's repeated scans and missing images, accident or intent (the FBI holds the boxes;
even if every repeated image counted, a few hundred net votes against about 12,000); and whether
Pennsylvania's remaining gap between certified ballots and its voter database ever closed. The rest: how
many of Virginia's ~1,600 removed voters were citizens; the method behind DHS's "42,776 deceased" Georgia
registrants; the federal citizenship check's error rate; the count of noncitizens among Arizona's 218,000
voters without proof on file; whether Maricopa charges were filed; Nevada's signature machine, its 4,057
DMV flags, its possible two-state voters and its open referrals; Wisconsin's statewide care-facility ballots;
any intrusion on Puerto Rico's machines; a Delaware County, PA records review; and why Texas took down its
"155 prosecuted" figure. None, alone or together, is shown to approach a statewide margin.

**PROVEN.** Six races voided or rerun, none statewide (NC-9, Bridgeport, Harris County, Paterson, two Alabama
towns); insiders who changed counts, all local and caught; small cases, mostly one person each, including
small numbers of noncitizen votes; real sloppiness (Fulton, Detroit's books, Maricopa's printers, a Virginia
county off by 3,975); rules broken (Wisconsin's care facilities and drop boxes, Cochise County, the Coffee
County breach); wrong flag lists (Texas 2019, Alabama 2024).

**NOT CHECKABLE.** Undetected fraud anywhere; whether State Farm observers were told to leave; who "really"
won Florida 2000; Wisconsin's indefinitely-confined voters one by one; the exact effect of the 2020 grants.

**EXPLAINED.** Almost every widely shared clip and claim, from every side, 2000 to 2026.

## Settled (not the debate)

- The most-shared 2020 clips show ordinary procedure, and the paper backs the count. Georgia's hand count of
  about 5 million ballots matched the machines to within 0.1053%.
- Two 2024 clips were fabricated (Bucks County; Georgia "Haitian voters"). Local officials showed the props
  were wrong, and US agencies attributed both to Russian actors.
- Real fraud exists and has been proven: NC-9 in 2018 and Bridgeport in 2023. Both were caught by records,
  not by a viral clip.
- Proven cases nationally are rare and mostly lone individuals. AP's review of six disputed 2020 states found
  fewer than 475 *potential* cases against a combined margin of 311,257 votes. Heritage's own database lists
  1,619 proven cases from 1982 to 2025 and says it is a sample, not a total.
- At the national close-out in 2020, the Attorney General at the time said no fraud had been found "on a
  scale that could have affected a different outcome." Of 64 post-election cases, 30 had a merits hearing
  and one minor one was won.
- Fulton County ran 2020 badly. Every body that looked, including the 2026 affidavit, says so. None of the
  state bodies found fraud or a changed outcome.

## Contested (what is genuinely open)

- **Were Fulton's 2020 repeated scans intentional?** This is a live federal criminal investigation. The case
  for intent: the duplicates leaned somewhat toward the losing candidate, images are missing, and the state
  monitor was out of the room. The case against: three counts agreed, and the analyst and the state
  investigator both read the pattern as not partisan. The 17,434 "shortfall" was a progress report the judge
  said was presented in a misleading way. And the affidavit reuses allegations the state already
  investigated. No charges have been reported. The episode does not prejudge it.
- **Is fraud rare, or rarely caught?** Hand counts check the machines, not who was allowed to vote. A properly
  filled-out fraudulent absentee ballot passes a recount. Against that: every big audit that went looking
  found single digits to hundreds of cases, and organised schemes (NC-9, Bridgeport) left paper trails that
  were caught. The best estimate of undetected double voting in 2012 is at most 1 in 4,000 votes, possibly
  all clerical error.
- **Georgia's machines:** real vulnerabilities on the record, no evidence they were exploited, audits that
  match the paper. Whether to move to hand-marked ballots is a policy fight, and the state has set a
  committee to report by January 2027.

## The reframe

The shouting treats every clip as either "fraud caught on tape" or "totally debunked." The record has three
different things in it, and the clips almost always show the wrong one. The famous clips show ordinary
procedure that looks sinister without the rulebook: boxes stored, a jam cleared, families returning ballots,
a van at 4 a.m. The real problems are the dull ones no clip shows: missing images, repeated scans, missing
custody forms, a monitor out of the room. The proven schemes were found in records and in footage nobody
went viral with. So the useful question is not "can I trust what I saw?" It is "what should be on screen if
this were normal, and who has already checked?"

## Both sides quietly agree

Fulton County kept poor records in 2020. The monitor, the state board, the county itself and the FBI
affidavit all say so, and nobody in the record claims it changed the result. Underneath the fight, both camps
want the same things: footage that is actually kept, ballot images that are retained, and custody paperwork
that follows every box. That is the direction Georgia's 2021 law and later rules moved.

## Verdict framing (handed to the viewer)

No winner is declared. The episode hands the viewer a checklist they can use on the next clip. It is framed
as what the investigators in these cases actually did, not as a proven cure:
1. **Find the first upload.** The upload date is not the film date.
2. **Grab a still and reverse-search it.** Free keyframe tools exist.
3. **Read sideways before you read down.** Search what the local election office says about this clip.
   Students taught this roughly doubled their scores. A one-line reminder alone did not help adults.
4. **Ask what the rulebook says should be on screen, under the rule in force on that date.** Two officials,
   a sealed container and a form is the process working.
5. **Ask what paper trail would exist if it were true, and who has already followed it.**
6. **Go to your state or county office, or CanIVote.org.** The federal "Rumor vs. Reality" page is gone.
7. **Aim to tell true from false, not to doubt everything.**

## Script traps

- **Process only.** No candidate or party is the subject. Write "the losing candidate", "the side that lost"
  and "a sitting official", and give officials by role. A party is named only where it is the actor (a party
  primary in Bridgeport).
- ⛔ **Private people are never named or shown:** the two Fulton election workers, the poll watchers, the
  voter in 2000 Mules, the Postal Service contractor, relatives in dead-voter cases, the Bridgeport and
  Paterson defendants, the wrongly flagged Texas voter. No document close-ups with names visible.
- **Live case:** re-check FBI charges and filings in Fulton on the day of scripting.
- **Numbers:** 653 boxes, January 28, 2026. Give "40%" with its base. Four dead-voter ballots. Use the EAVS
  errata figures (4.6 million removed for death). About $148 million in defamation damages. The 2024 vote gap
  was 6.25 million.
- **Attributions of fakes:** "US agencies said", never "proved". The props and the county statements are what
  a viewer can check.
- **The machine case keeps its "partly".** Curling ended on standing and is on appeal. It was not a ruling that
  the system is safe, and not a ruling that it is hackable in elections.
- **The 2020 close-out statements are narrower than they sound** ("on a scale that could have affected a
  different outcome"; CISA's councils spoke about systems; the Supreme Court ruled on standing).
- **The footage itself was never viewed by this research.** Describe what the investigators' reports say it
  shows. Before any clip or still is used on screen, it needs a frame check and a rights check.
- **Never borrow** the line "no election outcome in the U.S. has ever been altered by ballot fraud." NC-9 was
  rerun.
