Wolf Hunting in Michigan: Who Should Manage the Wolves, and by What Rule?

The question: Who should manage Michigan's Upper Peninsula wolves, and by what rule: delist and hunt to a state target, or keep protection and kill only for confirmed conflict?

▶ Watch on YouTubeThe full record ↓

Two designs for a recovered predator — and Washington just put a 90-day clock on one of them.

Why anyone is asking now

On September 4, 2026, the President signed an order giving the Interior Secretary 90 days, to December 3, to decide whether the gray wolf should come off the endangered list. A delisting bill had passed the House and was stuck in the Senate, and a Michigan bill would require a hunting season if the wolf were delisted.

The fight

Green arguesManage them like a game species

The wolf recovered: Michigan passed the federal floor of 200 wolves in 2000, and the 2026 winter count was at least 792. Delist it, let the state set a number and the commission set a season, and let a farmer shoot the wolf in his calf pen. A plan with no number has decided by default.

Gold arguesManage the conflict, not the number

Attacks follow a few packs, not the size of the population. Remove the wolf that kills, pay full value for losses including dogs, and put guard dogs and other tools on the hundred farms that carry the losses. Minnesota's three hunting seasons moved neither the wolf count nor the losses.

What both sides quietly agree on

Both teams accepted that there are at least 792 wolves, and 500 to 1,200 across a year as pups are born and winter takes them. There are about 13 verified livestock attacks a year, on one farm in ten, no wolf has ever attacked a person in Michigan, and winter runs the deer herd more than wolves do. Both also want a farmer able to act on the wolf in front of him.

What the shouting gets wrong

Since February 2022, nobody in Michigan may kill a wolf except to protect a human life, so both teams were arguing about a tool the state doesn't have. The real dial is who may kill a wolf that has killed nothing: a licensed hunter, nobody, or someone in between. And who decides that: a court, a secretary on a clock, a legislature, a commission, or the tribes whose treaties cover the ground.

The verdict

Walt This one doesn't resolve, and no design wins it. Three things from me. What survived. What fell. And what's left for you to set.

Walt Both teams accepted the same settled ground. At least seven ninety-two wolves, five hundred to twelve hundred across a year. About thirteen verified attacks a year, on one farm in ten, and nobody, ever. Winter runs the herd. And a state that today may kill a wolf for nothing but a human life.

Walt What survived from green. The wolf is the only recovered species Michigan is forbidden to manage, and a plan with no number has decided by default. And the pen. Both teams want the farmer able to act on the wolf in front of him.

Walt What survived from gold. The conflict follows packs, not the count, so the remedy is the pack. Pay for the dogs. And the one record we have of a designed season, Minnesota, moved neither the population nor the losses.

Walt What fell. Green's own engine: a recreational season as a tool against attacks. No study anywhere has measured it, and she said so. And gold's tools on the farm: fladry and guard dogs, unmeasured in Michigan, and he said so.

Walt So here's the design that survives both teams. The wolf that kills is removable, by the farmer in the act and by the state on confirmation. Losses paid at full value, dogs included. The count published as a floor and a range, never as one number. And a plan that says what the number is for, even if the answer is a band and not a line.

Walt That leaves two dials neither team could settle for you. Who may kill a wolf that has killed nothing: a licensed hunter, nobody, or someone in between. And who decides that: a court, a secretary on a clock, a legislature with one word, a commission, or the tribes whose treaties cover the ground.

Walt So the question we're handing you isn't wolves or no wolves. Both teams just built you a state that can act on the pen. It's this. What counts as a conflict worth killing a wolf over? A calf, a hound, a deer, or the feeling of being watched. That one's yours.
