## 1. "Executive Order 14425, signed 4 September 2026, gives the Interior Secretary 90 days to determine whether the gray wolf has met the recovery criteria for delisting or downlisting, and to begin that process if so."
**CONFIRMED.** Section 3(a), read in the Federal Register text (91 FR 57765, published 2026-09-10) and
the White House PDF: "Within 90 days of the date of this order … The Secretary of the Interior shall
make a determination as to whether the gray wolf and the Mexican wolf have met the recovery criteria
for delisting or downlisting under the Endangered Species Act (ESA) and, if he determines that the
recovery criteria has been met, shall begin the process of delisting or downlisting them." Ninety days
from 4 September is 3 December 2026. Script trap: the order directs a *determination* and a
*legislative recommendation*; it does not delist anything and does not order a hunt.
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/10/2026-18571/supporting-americas-ranchers
- https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/eo-14425.pdf

## 2. "The same order tells Interior to revise its criteria for authorizing lethal wolf removal, to rework depredation-compensation rules, and to press states to loosen their own take standards."
**CONFIRMED, with the verb kept.** §3(e): the Secretary "shall consider amending regulations or providing
additional guidance … to revise the Department's criteria for authorizing lethal gray wolf and Mexican
wolf removal." §3(d): both Secretaries "shall consider developing or amending … producer compensation
regulations … to ensure that all depredation claims … are consistently, accurately, and expeditiously
assessed." §3(c): on a recovery finding, the White House "shall engage with States to encourage them
to delist … and to revise their standards for lethal takings." The operative verbs are *consider* and
*encourage*; the script must not say the order changes any rule.
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/10/2026-18571/supporting-americas-ranchers

## 17. "The Michigan-specific study of lethal removal found its local benefit statistically insignificant and offset by increased recurrence nearby."

distant." The correction notice of 19 Dec 2018 restates the same hazard ratios and stresses that none
are significant. The two fetches of the abstract returned different percentages for the neighbouring
increase (22% and 78%), so **the percentages are APPROXIMATE and stay off air**; the finding used on air

## Corrections applied to source

1. **The GOLD design as first framed was wrong about the present.** The topic note and the first draft
   of the question described GOLD as "lethal removal only for confirmed depredation — Michigan's regime
   since 2022." The Fish and Wildlife Service's 2025 assessment shows that, because Michigan's wolves are
   listed *endangered* rather than *threatened*, no lethal depredation control has been authorized in
   Michigan since February 2022 — GOLD-as-described is the 2003–2022 regime. The brief now states the
   current regime as "no lethal removal at all" and carries the asymmetry with Minnesota as a settled
   fact (claim 11).
2. **"Under current rules it is illegal to kill a wolf unless caught in the act of killing livestock or
   dogs"** (from a 2023 Bridge Michigan summary, repeated in the first scouting note) is a description of
   Michigan's *state* statutes (2008 PA 290 and PA 318), which are inoperative while the wolf is federally
   endangered. Removed.
3. **The 2016 Ontonagon account** is stated as Bridge Michigan's reporting from FOIA'd emails, not as
   adjudicated fact.
