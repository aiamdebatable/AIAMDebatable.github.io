Cold open

NarratorWhat is all this new power for, and who pays while we find out? Smaller nuclear reactors are being offered as an answer to data centers' hunger for electricity. In September, the US regulator approved construction of what it called the first commercial one, at Clinch River, Tennessee.

NarratorThe permit sets its earliest completion date: October first, twenty thirty-one, about five years off. And the owner gave the regulator its cost estimate, with the figure kept confidential.

NarratorSo is this the start of a fleet, or a permit with its price blacked out? From the permit to the price to your bill, you're the jury.

NarratorOne rule for this town. What the record has settled, we build solid. What people still argue about, we build in glass.

The research · What a small modular reactor is

KateBefore anyone argues: what makes a reactor small, and what's new about it?

RyanEvery reactor running in America is big: the newest, at Plant Vogtle in Georgia, is about eleven hundred megawatts. By the international definition, a small modular reactor is three hundred or less. One Vogtle reactor is nearly four of the one approved in Tennessee.

KateSo it's a whole new kind of reactor?

RyanThat one isn't. It's a smaller boiling-water reactor, the water-cooled kind America has run since nineteen sixty, on the same fuel. What's simplified: no pumps to push the water round, and safety systems built to cool the core for about three days with no power or operators.

KateBuilt in a factory?

RyanIn big prefabricated pieces, then assembled on site. The genuinely new designs are others: one cooled by liquid sodium in Wyoming, one by helium gas planned for Texas and Indiana, and a molten-salt test reactor in Tennessee.

KateSo this isn't the usual fight over whether nuclear is safe?

RyanNot really. It's whether smaller, simpler and repeated can finally make nuclear cheap and fast to build.

The research · What it's for, and what got approved

RyanStart with what it's for. The grid's reliability watchdog expects summer peak demand to grow by more than two hundred gigawatts in ten years, mostly from data centers. And tech companies are lining up nuclear deals: Amazon, Meta, and in October a study of a twelve-reactor plant in Indiana.

KateWait. Are those orders?

RyanMostly options. Amazon's deal with the reactor maker says it's not obligated to buy. Meta's six point six gigawatts is an up-to figure, and about two and a half of it is plants already running. Indiana is a feasibility study, with Amazon funding the development work.

KateAnd the demand itself? Is that solid?

RyanRising, and being trimmed at the same time. One big grid operator, PJM, cut its near-term forecast after vetting data-center requests, though it still grows strongly later.

KateSo what exactly got approved in Tennessee?

RyanA construction permit for one reactor, issued September twenty-eighth. Building only: running it takes a separate operating licence. The earliest completion date it sets is October first, twenty thirty-one. That's a date on the permit, not a forecast of when power flows.

KateWas it a fight to get?

RyanNo. Nobody petitioned against it, and the hearing was the uncontested one every permit gets. The regulator's count is fourteen months from accepting the application, ahead of its schedule. Vogtle's two big reactors took about forty-four, for a licence that also covered running them, so it isn't like for like.

KateFast. But is it safe?

RyanThe NRC's own safety evaluation found reasonable assurance the final design will meet its design basis with, quote, adequate margin for safety. Its independent advisory committee supports the permit, and lists what it wants tested before operation: the water's natural circulation at startup, and how the passive cooling condensers perform.

KateAnd if something does go wrong, how far out do the emergency plans reach?

RyanNot decided yet. TVA kept two options, a zone that stops at the site boundary or one about two miles out, and chooses at the operating licence. Today's big plants plan for about ten miles.

KateAnd what will it cost?

RyanThat estimate is with the regulator, blacked out as proprietary. The regulator reviewed TVA's ability to pay in a separate evaluation, and its safety report prints no figure. And TVA's March report to Congress said a decision to build still needs its board's approval. Hold that.

RyanOntario's Darlington uses the same design as Tennessee's. When it was picked in twenty twenty-one, it could be completed as early as twenty twenty-eight. Now the target is grid power by the end of twenty thirty. Its basemat went in this April.

RyanWyoming's sodium-cooled plant was due in twenty twenty-eight. Then Russia's invasion removed what TerraPower called the only commercial source of its fuel, and completion moved to twenty thirty. KateWhy is that fuel so hard to get?

RyanThe new designs need uranium enriched well past today's five percent. America's one demonstration line made less than a tonne a year, and the energy department doesn't plan to order more from it. It says more than forty tonnes are needed by twenty thirty.

KateSo Tennessee's reactor has a fuel problem too?

RyanNo. It burns ordinary fuel, under five percent. The risk that does reach it is where ordinary fuel gets enriched: about a quarter of what American reactor owners bought last year came from Russia, and the waivers under the ban end by twenty twenty-eight.

KatePeople keep saying the Navy has run small reactors for decades. So what's the problem?

RyanAmerica's Navy counts more than seventy-six hundred reactor-years as of December twenty twenty-four, and says it has never had a reactor accident. But it's its own nuclear overseer. KateSo what does the Navy actually prove?

RyanThat small reactors can run safely for decades. On cost, the record is empty: nobody has published what a naval reactor costs, and the Navy weighs the price of a warship, not of a megawatt-hour.

The research · What it costs: the first one or the tenth

RyanOnly one owner in North America has published a budget for a small reactor. Ontario Power Generation: seven point seven billion Canadian dollars for its first unit and the infrastructure later units will share, including interest and contingency.

KateHas anyone in the US got that far?

RyanOne project had a full cost estimate: NuScale's power plant for a group of Utah towns. It rose to nine point three billion dollars, including financing, for under five hundred megawatts. It ended in twenty twenty-three because too few towns signed up: twenty-six percent of the power, against eighty needed.

KateSo it was too expensive?

RyanThe partners named lack of subscription, not price. Its target price had been raised to eighty-nine dollars a megawatt-hour, a threshold the project had to meet. That price drove the towns off is a fair guess, but it's not what they said.

KateAnd what does TVA assume for Tennessee?

RyanIts long-term plan assumes about seventeen thousand dollars a kilowatt for a first small reactor, in twenty twenty-six dollars, before tax credits. TVA calls that all-in costs plus risk contingencies. It's nearly twice the top of a generic range its own permit paperwork borrowed.

KateSo will they get cheaper?

RyanThat's the hinge. In the medians of the OECD's Nuclear Energy Agency, power from a first small reactor costs about one and a half times a gas plant's. From a mature series, a bit less than gas, but only if standard designs get built again and again.

KateHas nuclear ever actually gotten cheaper with practice?

RyanNot in America's record. Later US plants cost more than earlier ones, and nuclear's measured learning rate runs from negative to six percent; the higher rates you hear are model assumptions. A study of several hundred reactors found South Korea's costs fell, but that finding is disputed.

KatePeople say these will roll off assembly lines, like airliners. Is anyone building that line?

RyanNot yet. No reactor factory exists. Darlington's reactor vessel is a one-unit order at an existing shop, and its modules are steel sections from several shops, assembled on site. The one small-reactor factory under construction makes fuel.

KateAnd if they're all one design, couldn't one flaw hit all of them at once?

RyanIt's happened to big reactors. In twenty twenty-two, a single kind of crack took a big slice of France's reactors offline. EDF put its own cost at about twenty-nine billion euros, and France imported power for the first time since nineteen eighty. No small-reactor fleet exists yet, so that's an analogy.

KateThen why not just build more of the big ones we know?

RyanPer kilowatt, big is cheaper. MIT's projection puts the next two big Westinghouse reactors at about eighty-three hundred dollars a kilowatt, and a small reactor's mature-series cost still about forty-five percent higher. The government is backing both, and no new big reactor is ordered either. Hold that.

RyanIndiana is the mirror. A twenty twenty-five law lets a utility recover eighty percent of approved small-reactor development costs through rates, before it even holds a construction certificate. KateWhat happened the last time a reactor here ran over?

RyanGeorgia's two newest reactors went from about fourteen billion dollars planned to about thirty-five. Regulators let about seven and a half billion of Georgia Power's share into rates, financing on top, and left two point six billion with its shareholders. The company's own estimate: about nine dollars a month on a typical home.

KateAnd when it never gets finished?

RyanSouth Carolina abandoned two reactors of the same design in twenty seventeen, and the utility's customers are still paying for them, over twenty years. Overseas it's the same pattern: Finland's reactor came in thirteen years late, France's twelve, and Britain's first unit is now due in twenty thirty, about five years late.

KateSo who pays depends on what?

RyanThe contract. In Finland, mostly the French vendor. In Britain, the builder carries the overrun, and bill payers pay a guaranteed price for thirty-five years. Here, on our reading, the deals on record put the first small reactors' early costs on ratepayers. Amazon has publicly pledged to pay its data centers' full power costs.

KateAnd the waste?

RyanTennessee's spent fuel stays on site, in a pool and then dry casks, because America has no repository. Taxpayers have already paid eleven point one billion dollars for the energy department's failure to take utilities' spent fuel.

KateIs a small reactor at least cleaner than a big one?

RyanNobody has measured one. The few studies that exist put a small reactor about the same as a big one over its life, not cleaner. What a big one emits, we'll get to.

KatePeople say solar and batteries can't get through a calm winter week. True?

RyanHalf right. Today's batteries hold hours, not days, and the grid's watchdog says a storm lasting days can drain them. But in the two recent winter storms that cut power, most of what failed was gas plants. Wind froze too, and so did some nuclear units.
