Read the verdict as it aired

Nia No winner here. First what's settled, then which way the record tilts, then a dial for you to set.

Nia Settled: the permit is real, and it came fast. This reactor burns ordinary fuel. No small reactor in our record has been built on its first schedule. The first ones cost more, on the agencies' tables and on TVA's own planning figure. And Tennessee's estimate is filed and blacked out.

Nia On what has happened, the record leans Green: every small reactor that runs took over a decade, the deals are options, and rules already let early costs land on bills. On this reactor's licensing and fuel, it leans Gold. Whether a series gets cheap, and how badly the grid needs it, nobody has shown yet.

Nia Who carries the first one is a judgement. Here's the dial, then: what should have to be true before your bill helps pay for a small reactor? Show me one running, like the Utah towns that kept a way out and took it. But then nobody goes first, and their project ended.

Nia The buyers carry it: the companies that want the power fund the first plants, like Amazon in Indiana and Meta with TerraPower. But so far the deals are options and intentions. Amazon's own deal says it's not obligated to buy.

Nia Taxpayers share it, up to a limit: federal money takes part of the risk, like the four hundred million dollars TVA was selected to negotiate. But that help depends on next year's budget, and taxpayers have already paid billions for spent fuel the government was meant to take and hasn't.

Nia Bill-payers carry it, with a referee: a utility recovers costs and a regulator checks them, as Ontario and Indiana already allow. But Georgia's referee let about seven and a half billion dollars of Georgia Power's share of Vogtle into rates, and South Carolina's customers still pay for two reactors never finished.

Nia Order the fleet now: commit to a series so the price can fall, like Ontario's four units. But last time, about thirty American reactors applied and two got built. The teams nearly meet at the two middle settings, and Tennessee's TVA decides for itself. Your setting is your call.

The judge named no sides on this one, so there's no dial: we don't invent positions.
