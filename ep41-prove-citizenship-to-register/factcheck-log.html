Part 1 — What registering is today: the sworn box

1. The federal registration form makes you swear or affirm you are a citizen, under penalty of perjury, and warns you may be fined, imprisoned or deported

CONFIRMED. "I swear/affirm that: I am a United States citizen … under penalty of perjury. If I have provided false information, I may be fined, imprisoned, or (if not a U.S. citizen) deported from or refused entry to the United States." The form says "may", not "will".

  • https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf

2. Falsely claiming citizenship to register carries up to five years in prison; a noncitizen voting in a federal election up to one; and unlawful voting makes a noncitizen deportable

CONFIRMED. 18 U.S.C. 1015(f): "imprisoned not more than five years, or both". 18 U.S.C. 611(b): "imprisoned not more than one year". 8 U.S.C. 1227(a)(6)(A): "Any alien who has voted in violation of any Federal, State, or local … statute … is deportable."

  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1015
  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/611
  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1227

3. A third federal law punishes "knowingly submitting a materially false registration application" with up to five years

APPROXIMATE. 52 U.S.C. 20511 carries up to five years, but it punishes depriving or defrauding "the residents of a State of a fair and impartially conducted election process" by submitting applications "known … to be materially false". The engine's paraphrase drops that element. Not paraphrased on air.

  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/52/20511

4. California registers voters on a sworn statement, and its Secretary of State refused the Justice Department's request

CONFIRMED. Secretary of State's letter, 2026-10-15: "California requires all voter registration applicants to attest to U.S. citizenship under oath, under penalty of perjury and by signature" and "I will not disenfranchise thousands of Californians … based solely upon an unsupported and likely inaccurate 'belie[f]' that certain voters are 'likely' non-citizens." A party's letter: quote it as "California says".

  • https://admin.cdn.sos.ca.gov/pdf/other/casos-to-doj-101526.pdf
  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.1043513/gov.uscourts.cacd.1043513.22.7.pdf

5. California checks who you are against government records, but that check proves identity, not citizenship

CONFIRMED. The Justice Department's TRO memo: proof "is not required, so long as the applicant self-attests to American citizenship under penalty of perjury" and "Although California verifies identifying information against government records, such verification establishes identity, not citizenship." DOJ's wording, a party's brief.

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.1043513/gov.uscourts.cacd.1043513.22.2.pdf

6. In Ohio you give a licence number or the last four digits of your Social Security number and swear you are a citizen; the licence office keeps paper forms that need only that oath

CONFIRMED. Sixth Circuit stay order, p.2 (an image scan, read at the primary): applicants "must provide … a driver's license number or the last four digits of a social security number. They also must attest under penalty of perjury that they are U.S. citizens"; paper forms "require only an attestation of eligibility".

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891.23.0.pdf

7. Ohio's new law requires documents to register at the licence office

CORRECTED. Ohio HB 54 restricts only the licence office's electronic offer: the worker must offer to register you "by electronic means" only if you present, or earlier presented, proof of citizenship. A citizen without proof gets the paper oath form instead of the screen. Ohio is not a documents-for-all state. Say "Ohio's licence counter", and only for that narrow change.

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891.23.0.pdf

8. The Sixth Circuit let Ohio's law run for this election without deciding whether it breaks federal law

CONFIRMED. "Ohio appears primed to succeed in vacating the injunction on threshold grounds. Because those grounds are sufficient to warrant entry of a stay, we need not address the merits of the district court's NVRA analysis." Stay entered 2026-09-23.

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891/gov.uscourts.ca6.159891.23.0.pdf
  • https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74762684/red-wine-blue-v-frank-larose/

9. The dissent called Ohio's law "antithetical to the NVRA"

CORRECTED. Not in the order (the peg hunt read all 21 pages). The usable dissent line: the NVRA is "meant to facilitate, not hamper" registration.

Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both designs were held to the same test: every number re-opened at its primary, every comparator recomputed. The record is not even, and the brief does not pretend it is. Where it has been counted, noncitizen registration is rare (1 in 77,000 records in Utah, 1 in 410,000 registrants in Georgia, 1 in 360,000 ballots in Michigan), and the documents rule's measured citizen cost (Kansas, about 1,100 applicants blocked for each noncitizen it stopped) is far larger than the noncitizens it stopped. That is where the evidence leans, and the brief says so.
  • The proof side is built at full strength anyway. Its strongest points are all CONFIRMED and kept: the checkbox demonstrably failed in Utah (14 ticked "No" and were registered; 34 ticked "Yes"); counts are floors, because audits only find what they can match and Georgia's 20 surfaced by accident, through jury-duty statements; the oath's penalties are rarely used (Kansas: zero complaints; Ohio: a handful charged); and a state's own form may lawfully ask for more (Inter Tribal Council). The document survey's honest weak point ("by tomorrow") is stated, and so is the fact that an independent measure comes out higher, not lower.
  • The records side is not let off. Records checks fail in the other direction (SSA 2006, Texas 2025, Florida 2012, Missouri), the overhauled SAVE has no published error rate, and the California list has no accuracy figure at all. Utah, a records-first state, says its own checks cannot show who is not a citizen.
  • Advocacy-sourced figures are labelled. The 21.3 million survey's sponsors are named; the New Hampshire 244 is an advocacy tally (the court's at-least-146 is used instead); the New Hampshire expert ranges are a plaintiffs' expert's; DOJ's 175,000 and its charges are a party's claims.
  • No verdict on a live ballot measure or bill. Prop 39 and the SAVE America Act are places the question is being set, never verdicts.
  • Identity↔stance rotation is decided at build, when the cast is seated (the episode's cast list is still empty); this log cannot audit it yet.
