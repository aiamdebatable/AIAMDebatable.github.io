  • CONFIRMED — the source says what the episode says.
  • CORRECTED — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
  • UNRESOLVED — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.

The critics' case is held to the same standard: the 95% overturn rate is mostly one insurer reversing itself (log 98), the 13% is a 2019 sample (log 82), the ghost-network figures are mental health only (log 183, 186), and "traditional Medicare has no prior authorization" is false (log 153).
  • Where the sources stand. The record is lopsided in who says what, and the brief says so. Most pro-Advantage numbers come from the industry or from work it paid for: the 36% (ATI for Better Medicare Alliance), the selection rebuttals (AHIP, Humana's own subsidiary, Milliman for UnitedHealth, FTI, Paragon, HLC) (log 15–17, 413–428). Most of the critical numbers come from government referees (MedPAC, CBO, GAO, HHS's inspector general, a Senate majority staff) and from academics, some of them long-standing critics of MA payment (log 76). The peer-reviewed studies that favour MA are named as such (log 156, 207, 215, 220), and the one most favourable on low-value care was written mostly by Humana's researchers (log 156). No false balance: on the taxpayer question the record leans one way, and the brief says so.
  • Straw-manning / cherry-picking? Every cost figure carries its comparison group (Original Medicare with no supplement, with Medigap, or all FFS), because the answer flips with the comparator (log 19, 22, 15). Allegations stay allegations: UnitedHealth's Justice Department matters are investigations and an undecided case, with no charges (log 435–447).

Open questions this episode could not settle

  • Whether moving to MA saves an enrollee money against Original Medicare with Medigap: the best switcher study finds small differences with no consistent pattern (log 19, 80); nothing newer settles it.
  • How much of MedPAC's gap is favorable selection: MedPAC 11% against Humana's 4.9–5.8% (log 329, 420); CBO lands near MedPAC overall but does not split selection from coding (log 402).
  • Whether MA's lower use of care is efficiency or denial: both have peer-reviewed support (log 85, 87, 215, 218); no study since the 2024 coverage-criteria rule repeats OIG's test (log 133, 134).
  • How many people actually hit their plan's out-of-pocket cap (log 38).
  • How much the extras are used: MedPAC says the data are inadequate, especially for OTC cards, meals and transport (log 70, 71).
  • Realized dialysis access for MA enrollees with kidney failure (log 201).
  • What the 2027 exits do in rural counties: no rural split exists (log 14).
  • Whether the Fifth Circuit reinstates the 2023 audit-extrapolation rule (log 401), and how the court rules on the Poehling recommendation (log 446).
  • Duals: the Commonwealth Fund's satisfaction edge is mostly an age effect and its figures are unverified (log 314, 315); an age-adjusted study now finds D-SNP duals report somewhat better access and satisfaction than duals in Original Medicare (log 506), but nothing in this record measures their spending or health outcomes.
  • What a Medigap premium is as a share of a low income: no current named source (log 508).
