Medicare Advantage vs Original Medicare: Which Is the Better Deal for 2027?

Is Medicare Advantage a better deal for you than Original Medicare, and who pays for the extras?

Last changed 2026-11-08 · See every change

Four people at one kitchen table this October, one choice: a Medicare Advantage plan or Original Medicare. Two AI teams argue which is the better deal and who pays for the extras; you set the dial.

Settled on the record·Still argued

Why it was in the news

Medicare's open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs October 15 to December 7, 2026, and plans are leaving: in 193 counties no Advantage plan with drug coverage is open to the general public next year, up from 123.

Settled

What both sides accept

Plans can deliver Medicare's basic benefit for less than Original Medicare spends; the fight is over the rest of the money. Both routes ask permission for some care, though plans ask dozens of times as often per person. And a supplement keeps getting more expensive.

Still argued

The fight

Green arguesTake the Advantage

For most people, no premium beyond Part B, a yearly cap Original Medicare doesn't have, and extras like dental and hearing. Against having no supplement, people who picked a plan at 65 spent less at every level studied. Fix the denials; don't throw away the cap.

Gold arguesKeep the original

Against Original Medicare with a supplement, people who switched saw no clear saving. Plans say no most often after a hospital stay, and in most states the way back to a supplement shuts after 65. You pay more each month for the year you hope never comes.

▶ Hear Gold at 9:23 on YouTube

What the shouting gets wrong

It isn't which plan is cheaper. It's which year you're buying for: the year you're well, or the year you're not. A plan is cheapest while you're healthy and it stays in your county; its costs show up when you're sick, and in most states the way back to a supplement can close behind you. And the extras aren't free: Medicare pays about 14% more for people in plans, and everyone's Part B premium carries part of it.

The verdict

Where the record leans
Where the record leans: for a healthy person who'd otherwise have no supplement, the bundle is the better deal. For someone who can afford one and wants any doctor and a way back, no clear saving from switching, and real costs in a sick year. And the public pays about fourteen percent more per enrollee.
What didn't hold up
Advantage always saves you money: it depends on the comparison. Original Medicare never says no: it does. Half the doctors in your directory are fake: that was mental health, in ten counties. People in Advantage die sooner: the record doesn't show it. And almost every appeal wins: only among the few who appealed.

Still argued · your call

Your dial: A lower bill, or an open door later?

The judge named these positions and declared no winner. Open one to see what the record says it buys, what it costs, and what nobody has measured. Nothing is saved or counted.

  1. 1The open door
    What it buys
    Original Medicare, a supplement around $200 a month, and a drug plan, with any doctor who takes Medicare: 98% of doctors outside pediatrics take part. Against Original Medicare as most people hold it, with extra coverage, there's no clear saving either way. In a 2018 survey, people with a supplement had the fewest cost problems: 12%, against 19% in Advantage.
    What it costs
    A premium that keeps rising: Plan G went up 13% in a single year. And outside a window, a supplement can be priced on your health, or refused.

    On the record: row 135 of the fact-check log, row 19 of the fact-check log, row 26 of the fact-check log, row 49 of the fact-check log, row 283 of the fact-check log

  2. 2The bundle: an Advantage plan
    What it buys
    Often no extra premium: 75% of people in individual plans with drug coverage pay nothing beyond Part B. A cap on a bad year, at most $9,850 in your network in 2027 and about $5,421 on the average plan in 2026, plus extras like dental, vision and hearing. For a healthy person who'd otherwise have no supplement, the record leans to the bundle as the better deal.
    What it costs
    Permission, a network, and a door that may close. Plans ask permission far more often, most sharply after a hospital stay: in June 2024 they turned down more than half the requests for inpatient rehab. Networks need a provider of each type within a set distance, never a particular hospital. And 74% of people in these plans have a condition that could get them refused a supplement in most states. The public also pays about 14% more per enrollee, which is how the extras come with no extra premium.
    Not measured
    Whether the denials were wrong: the investigators relied on the insurers' own data without checking the records, and the insurers' answer that many denials are paperwork hasn't been checked either.

    On the record: row 47 of the fact-check log, row 34 of the fact-check log, row 104 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row 284 of the fact-check log, row 327 of the fact-check log

  3. 3Bare
    What it buys
    Only the Part B premium, $202.90 a month this year, with no supplement or plan premium on top. About 3.5 million people, 13% of Original Medicare, went without extra coverage in 2023.
    What it costs
    No yearly limit on what you pay. This is where the record most clearly favours the plans: against no extra coverage, people who picked a plan at 65 spent less at every level studied. And any protection beats none: a supplement cuts the highest costs too.

    On the record: row 31 of the fact-check log, row 30 of the fact-check log, row 22 of the fact-check log, row 24 of the fact-check log

  4. 4Both programs
    What it buys
    Medicaid or a Savings Program pays the costs, if you sign up. The fullest Savings Program pays the Part B premium and Medicare's deductibles and copays, on either route, and Medicare providers can't bill you for them. Extra Help means no drug-plan premium or deductible next year. People on both Medicare and Medicaid report somewhat better access and satisfaction in plans built for them than in Original Medicare, though not much better than in other Advantage plans.
    What it costs
    It only works if you sign up, and many people don't: about 2 in 5 people eligible for a Savings Program weren't enrolled in 2018 to 2020. It's for people with low income and savings, and people with Medicaid generally can't buy a supplement.
    Not measured
    How people on both programs fare on spending and health: no study in this record measures it.

    On the record: row 494 of the fact-check log, row 495 of the fact-check log, row 499 of the fact-check log, row 503 of the fact-check log, row 506 of the fact-check log

Whichever you pick: Check your network (call the office), your drugs, your supplement window, and whether you qualify for help, before open enrollment ends on December 7. Free help that isn't paid by any plan: your State Health Insurance Assistance Program, at shiphelp.org.

What our fact-check caught

36 of the 532 claims we checked came back wrong in our drafts. Every one is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidYou can switch back to Original Medicare, supplement and all, any January to March.

    The record saysYou can leave the plan then, but that window gives you no guaranteed right to a supplement, and in most states an insurer can turn you down.

  2. Draft said95% of appealed nursing-facility denials were overturned.

    The record saysFewer than one in five denials was appealed at all. One company got 42% of the appeals and reversed nearly all of them; insurers with few appeals reversed about 60%.

  3. Draft saidOriginal Medicare has no prior authorization. Period.

    The record saysIt does, for a short list of services, and turns down almost a quarter of those requests. A pilot in six states adds more from 2026.

  4. Draft saidThree in four people in Advantage would be refused a supplement.

    The record saysThree in four have a condition that could get them refused. That counts who could be turned down, not who was.

  5. Draft saidSigning up for a supplement in your first six months means no higher price.

    The record saysIt stops insurers refusing you or pricing you on your health. Prices still vary by age, where you live and more.
