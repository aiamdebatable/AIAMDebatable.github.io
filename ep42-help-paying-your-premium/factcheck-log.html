Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both sides were held to the same test: every number re-opened at its primary, every comparator recomputed. The record is not even, and the brief does not pretend it is. That the lapse raised what enrollees pay and cut paid enrollment is measured on CMS and KFF data (log 58, 71, 80). That is where the evidence leans, and the brief says so.
  • The cost-and-targeting side is built at full strength anyway. Its strongest points are CBO's own and all CONFIRMED: fewer than half of new enrollees are newly insured (log 151); job-based coverage falls (log 143, 152); about $350 billion over ten years for a permanent extension (log 133); the help was written as temporary (log 16); the $0 plans were the channel for documented broker fraud (log 219, 225–238). Its weakest numbers (the ~$35 billion a year and per-person divisions) are marked APPROXIMATE and labelled.
  • The fraud dispute is at equal weight, and neither side gets a measured prevalence it does not have. The integrity side's cases rest on GAO, DOJ and CMS primaries; its size estimates are models by HHS's own officials or an aligned think tank (log 189–201). The skeptics' critiques come from a think tank, an insurer-commissioned actuary, advocacy groups and news quotes, and offer no alternative count (log 203, 212–216). Paragon's own series cuts against the integrity side's reading of the drop (log 200); KFF, the actuary and the Urban economist each concede real fraud (log 203, 212, 213).
  • Advocacy-sourced figures are labelled. Paragon, CBPP and CRFB are marked advocacy; the actuary is marked insurer-commissioned; the House committee's 31% is corrected at CBO.
  • Dated official acts only. Vote counts, never party splits; officials by role; the releases written in party terms (CMS 10-14, Illinois) are used for their numbers only.
  • Identity↔stance rotation is decided at build, when the cast is seated; this log cannot audit it yet.

Open questions this episode could not settle

  • How much of the February-to-February drop was the lapse, how much the rules in force for 2026, how much the 2025 law, and how much improper enrollment falling away. No one has split it.
  • How common improper enrollment actually is. Every figure in circulation is a model; no audit of individual files exists.
  • What a real household will see for 2027: CMS's national landscape was not out on 2026-10-20.
  • Whether subsidies of this size raise premiums or hospital prices: no estimate for 2021–25 exists.
  • What coverage buys: the record holds no health or financial-harm evidence for people who lost Marketplace coverage, so the value side of the trade is argued, not measured.
  • Whether a minimum premium would cut fraud without cutting coverage: the only evidence is engine-only and unverified (log 239, 240).
  • What the Fourth Circuit decides after Oct 30, and what happens in the states' suit over the 2027 rule.
  • How many of the 760,000 have been reinstated.
