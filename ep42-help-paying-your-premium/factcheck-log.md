- **CONFIRMED** — the source says what the episode says.
- **CORRECTED** — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under

- **APPROXIMATE** — checked, roughly right, and **deliberately not cited on air**. Not the same as

  and the per-household tax shares (top-up T1 rows) DO air, each spoken as "roughly, by our own arithmetic". Row 308's
  arithmetic was re-computed by the main session 2026-10-21.
  UNRESOLVED: the claim was established, the *figure* just isn't precise enough to state. Use it when
  the direction is sound but a number would imply more precision than the source supports.

site prints, and since package `HFIX` a verdict it does not recognise **fails the build** instead of

**How this was checked.** A peg hunt first (2026-10-20, recorded in full at the end of this log): a
blind sweep with every topic word banned, the Federal Register's presidential documents, rules,
proposed rules and notices for 2026-09-22 to 10-20 read by title, and the CMS newsroom index read by
date; then targeted reads of what it surfaced. Then the deep-research-v3 engine (run wf_9f153147-e8d:
41 agents, 3,934,659 subagent tokens; 24 findings; 12 claims verified with the primary opened, all 12
held, 108 not adjudicated, 0 flagged; its critics named 18 gaps). Then a two-way hand pass on the
primary record, split by domain: **A, the outcomes and the cost** (what enrollees paid, plan choices,
sign-ups against paid enrollment, the states and their backfills, New Mexico's books, the uninsured
surveys, 2027 rates and insurer exits, every CBO and JCT number, the employer tax break, the 400% cliff,
and whether subsidies raise prices); and **B, the fraud, the rules and the peg** (each improper-enrollment
estimate and how it was built, GAO's covert tests, the DOJ cases, CMS's actions, the statutes, the IRS
proposed rule, HHS's 2025 and 2027 rules, the three courts, the dated acts in Congress and the White
House, and the peg's dates). Every primary relied on was downloaded and saved with its text extracted
(PDFs with pypdf, web pages as text), and is kept in the show's evidence archive (not part of the
published bundle). Where a site blocked direct download, the page was read through a text proxy or an
archive. **cbo.gov refused every direct route, so every CBO figure was read in CBO's own text through
Wayback Machine captures of CBO's own pages** (and two CBO letters through Senate offices' copies). The
main session then checked every relied-on quote against the saved copies: **132 of 134 for hand pass A
and 100 of 101 for hand pass B**. The misses were not source text: they were suggested on-air wording,
or the agent's own notes, set inside quote marks. The hand passes overrule the engine wherever they
differ. A claim only the engine made, which neither hand pass adjudicated, is in Part 16 and is **not on
air**. Where only a secondary source could be reached, the entry says so. The scout brief was used as a
checklist of claims to test, never as a source.

**Counting rule:** one entry per claim the brief relies on or a script could repeat, including the ones
deliberately kept off air. Parts 1–4, 14 and 15 are hand pass B's domain; parts 5–13 are hand pass A's;
part 16 holds engine claims no hand pass adjudicated. **Totals: 245 entries — CONFIRMED 177 · CORRECTED

**Reading the verdicts here.** "Secondary" or "advocacy" in a CONFIRMED entry means the claim was
checked against that source, which said it; the brief attributes those figures to their source and
labels advocacy. APPROXIMATE entries are used, if at all, only as a direction or an attributed report,
never as a precise figure. UNRESOLVED entries are in the brief's "Off air" list. A CORRECTED entry states
the claim as it reached us; what the episode says instead follows. "Our arithmetic" marks the show's own
computation on a quoted figure. Every rule, case and bill status is as of 2026-10-20.

## Fairness audit

- **Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly.** Both sides were held to the same test: every number
  re-opened at its primary, every comparator recomputed. The record is not even, and the brief does
  not pretend it is. That the lapse raised what enrollees pay and cut paid enrollment is measured on
  CMS and KFF data (log 58, 71, 80). That is where the evidence leans, and the brief says so.
- **The cost-and-targeting side is built at full strength anyway.** Its strongest points are CBO's own
  and all CONFIRMED: fewer than half of new enrollees are newly insured (log 151); job-based coverage
  falls (log 143, 152); about $350 billion over ten years for a permanent extension (log 133); the help
  was written as temporary (log 16); the $0 plans were the channel for documented broker fraud (log
  219, 225–238). Its weakest numbers (the ~$35 billion a year and per-person divisions) are marked
  APPROXIMATE and labelled.
- **The fraud dispute is at equal weight, and neither side gets a measured prevalence it does not
  have.** The integrity side's cases rest on GAO, DOJ and CMS primaries; its size estimates are models
  by HHS's own officials or an aligned think tank (log 189–201). The skeptics' critiques come from a
  think tank, an insurer-commissioned actuary, advocacy groups and news quotes, and offer no
  alternative count (log 203, 212–216). Paragon's own series cuts against the integrity side's reading
  of the drop (log 200); KFF, the actuary and the Urban economist each concede real fraud (log 203, 212,
  213).
- **Advocacy-sourced figures are labelled.** Paragon, CBPP and CRFB are marked advocacy; the actuary is
  marked insurer-commissioned; the House committee's 31% is corrected at CBO.
- **Dated official acts only.** Vote counts, never party splits; officials by role; the releases written
  in party terms (CMS 10-14, Illinois) are used for their numbers only.
- **Identity↔stance rotation** is decided at build, when the cast is seated; this log cannot audit it yet.

## Open questions this episode could not settle

- How much of the February-to-February drop was the lapse, how much the rules in force for 2026, how
  much the 2025 law, and how much improper enrollment falling away. No one has split it.
- How common improper enrollment actually is. Every figure in circulation is a model; no audit of
  individual files exists.
- What a real household will see for 2027: CMS's national landscape was not out on 2026-10-20.
- Whether subsidies of this size raise premiums or hospital prices: no estimate for 2021–25 exists.
- What coverage buys: the record holds no health or financial-harm evidence for people who lost
  Marketplace coverage, so the value side of the trade is argued, not measured.
- Whether a minimum premium would cut fraud without cutting coverage: the only evidence is engine-only
  and unverified (log 239, 240).
- What the Fourth Circuit decides after Oct 30, and what happens in the states' suit over the 2027 rule.
- How many of the 760,000 have been reinstated.
