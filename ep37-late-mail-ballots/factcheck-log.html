Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both designs got the same treatment: every claim re-opened at its primary. On paper the grace side has more numbers (the curve, the press tallies), but that partly reflects what exists: the receipt side's core evidence is the national comparison (5.7) and USPS's own words about the postmark, and the brief builds it at full strength from both.
  • No false balance. On "do grace periods rescue ballots", the record leans one way (yes, about 5 per 100 mailed in a Washington general). On "do they lower late rejections overall", the record is mixed and the brief says so, with both readings of the national data.
  • No party as subject. The map cuts across parties (Texas, Mississippi, West Virginia and Alaska count late ballots; so do California, New York and Illinois). The litigation and the 2023–26 rollbacks are described by what they did, not by who did it. The trust experiment's partisan split travels with its caveat (row 8.6).
  • Advocacy is attributed. The 83% poll airs only with its sponsor and that sponsor's stake in the case; the UW "800,000" line, Voting Rights Lab's "tenfold" and Brookings' fraud commentary are labelled as the arguments they are.
  • Absences are not facts. "No fraud case was cited by either side", never "it never happened"; "no audited case isolates the late arrivals", never "late ballots never changed a result".
  • Candidates out. Every race, flip and fraud case is described by role; no candidate is named.
  • The viewer's rule is theirs to check. No line states a deadline for "your state".
  • The integrity record, both halves (top-up D2). The proven cases are aired at full weight (a US House result voided; a mayoral primary thrown out) and so are the reviews that found no fraud at scale. Where a correction cut toward reassurance (Paterson had no fraud finding) and where one cut toward the concern (the 2005 commission's "potential" still names absentee ballots as the largest source), both are kept. Confidence splits are measured findings and travel with the winner-loser caveat (14.2), except the mail-specific residue (14.3). Hand counts are never offered as proof of legitimacy (14.19, 14.21).
  • Absences stay absences. "The largest public list of proven cases contains none" (14.16), never "it never happened"; no fraud rate is aired (14.37).
