Tariffs: They Raise What You Pay. Is What They Buy Worth It?

The question: Tariffs raise what you pay. Is what they buy worth it?

▶ Watch on YouTubeThe full record ↓

About a thousand dollars a year, mostly paid at home. Some real plants, some real deals, and a lot of promises.

Why anyone is asking now

When this episode came out in October 2026, the Supreme Court had ruled in February that the emergency-powers law doesn't authorize tariffs, and about $166 billion was going back to the companies that imported the goods. The washer and furniture tariffs, under a different law, still stood.

The fight

Green arguesNot at this price

About 90 cents of every tariff dollar is paid here, about $1,000 a year for the average household, and hardest on the poorest. The court's refunds go to importers, not shoppers. There are 21,000 fewer factory jobs than in January 2025, most deals are still on paper, and last time each washer job cost buyers over $800,000 a year.

Gold arguesWorth paying for

Europe, Britain, Switzerland and Cambodia cut their tariffs on American goods, in law, and of the partners checked, only China and Canada hit back. The 2018 washer tariff built plants that still run, and customs raised about $195 billion in a year. Use tariffs where they deliver a plant or a deal.

What both sides quietly agree on

Americans pay most of it: about 90 cents of each tariff dollar stayed here, about $1,000 a year for the average household, depending on the model. The court's refund goes to importers, not households. And factory output is up while factory jobs are not.

What the shouting gets wrong

On who pays, the record isn't close: mostly, you do. On what it bought, the record has real answers on both sides: some real plants, some real deals, and a lot of promises. The question left to you is what $1,000 a year is worth to you.

Mara That leaves one dial neither team can set for you. What would you pay about a thousand dollars a year for? For the revenue, which fails when its legal base falls. For the leverage, which fails when a deal stays on paper. For the factories, which fail when the jobs don't come. Or for nothing: keep the money, which fails when the plant and the deal never arrive.

Mara So the question we're handing you isn't tariffs, yes or no. It's this: what's a thousand dollars a year worth to you? That one's yours.
