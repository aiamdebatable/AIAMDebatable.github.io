Why it was in the news

When this episode came out in October 2026, the Supreme Court had ruled in February that the emergency-powers law doesn't authorize tariffs, and about $166 billion was going back to the companies that imported the goods. The washer and furniture tariffs, under a different law, still stood.

Settled

What both sides accept

Americans pay most of it: about 90 cents of each tariff dollar stayed here, about $1,000 a year for the average household, depending on the model. The court's refund goes to importers, not households. And factory output is up while factory jobs are not.

Still argued · your call

Your dial: What would you pay about a thousand dollars a year for?

The judge named these positions and declared no winner. Open one to see what the record says it buys, what it costs, and what nobody has measured. Nothing is saved or counted.

  1. 1The revenue
    What it buys
    Customs raised about $195 billion in fiscal 2025, two and a half times the year before and about four times what the federal fuel taxes for the Highway Trust Fund brought in. About three-quarters of the monthly take survived the court's ruling, because other tariffs replaced the ones it struck down.
    What it costs
    It fails when its legal base falls. When the court struck down the emergency tariffs, refunds turned net customs receipts negative for three months this year, and about $166 billion is going back to the importers, not to households. And most of the money comes from home: about ninety cents of each tariff dollar stayed here.

    On the record: row C1 of the fact-check log, row D4 of the fact-check log, row C5 of the fact-check log, row A6 of the fact-check log, row B19 of the fact-check log

  2. 2The leverage
    What it buys
    Europe, Britain, Switzerland and Cambodia cut tariffs on American goods, in law, and Japan now accepts American-built cars without re-testing them. Of the partners we checked, only China and Canada hit back, and Europe built a €93 billion package it never applied.
    What it costs
    It fails when a deal stays on paper, and most of the deals are still on paper: Indonesia's waits on its parliament, India's is a framework, and Thailand is still negotiating. Where retaliation did land, it landed on farms: farm exports to China are forecast at $12 billion, down from $16 billion. With Canada, it's still live.
    Not measured
    Whether any deal raised what America sells abroad: exports rose about 15% to almost everyone, deal or no deal, so the record shows correlation, not cause.

    On the record: row C8 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row D1-32 of the fact-check log, row D1-8 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row C16 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row D1-20 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected)

  3. 3The factories
    What it buys
    The 2018 washer plants are still running. Samsung's in South Carolina has made more than 8 million washers, and LG's in Tennessee employs about 740 people. Steel and drug-making are up, and factory output is up about 3% on 2024.
    What it costs
    It fails when the jobs don't come, and so far they haven't: there are 21,000 fewer factory jobs than in January 2025, and appliance and furniture makers have fewer workers now. Last time, each washer job cost buyers over $800,000 a year. The new washer line in Louisville doesn't start until 2027, and its company's chief executive says tariffs weren't the driving factor.
    Not measured
    What this round's plants will add: the one everyone points to is still being built.

    On the record: row D2-10 of the fact-check log, row D2-01 of the fact-check log, row C19 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row D2-08 of the fact-check log, row C28 of the fact-check log

  4. 4Keep the money
    What it buys
    You keep about $1,000 a year, the average household's bill, depending on the model. That matters most to the families with the least: as a share of income, the tariffs hit the poorest tenth about three times as hard as the richest.
    What it costs
    It fails when the plant and the deal never arrive. The plants and the deals you'd trade away came with the tariffs: the deals in force were struck under tariff pressure, and the 2018 washer plants came with a tariff.
    Not measured
    Which buys more, the money kept or what it pays for: nobody on this record can tell you.

    On the record: row B25 of the fact-check log, row B29 of the fact-check log, row D7 of the fact-check log, row C8 of the fact-check log (our draft, corrected), row C29 of the fact-check log
