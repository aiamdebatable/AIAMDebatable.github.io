# Fact-check log — Should Cities Abolish Single-Family-Only Zoning?

_Independent verification pass, run 2026-09-07 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). Each claim gets a
verdict: **CONFIRMED**, **CORRECTED**, **UNRESOLVED** or **APPROXIMATE**. This is a REQUIRED gate. The
show's differentiator is that this file is real._

- **CONFIRMED** — the source says what the episode says.
- **CORRECTED** — the source says otherwise; the brief was changed, and the change is listed under
  "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
- **UNRESOLVED** — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.
- **APPROXIMATE** — checked, roughly right, and deliberately not cited on air as a precise figure.

**Verification status:** adversarial-pass

_How this pass ran: a research fan-out of 65 agents (24 distinct finder queries across 6 angles) put
its 12 highest-relevance claims under three independent verifier votes — all 12 held 3–0, and all
12 were legal or procedural. The fan-out then reported, in its own words, that every production and
rent figure in the debate had fallen outside its verification budget. Those figures were checked by
hand against the primary documents the same day: the Auckland paper extracted in full, the
Minneapolis Fed article, Pew's article (from a legislature-hosted copy), the Terner Center report,
the WFAE article and the National Zoning Atlas brief. Three completeness critics named 18 gaps; the
ones that bear on what is said on air are recorded below as UNRESOLVED._

## THE PEG

## 1. "California SB 79 took effect 1 July 2026 and lets housing near transit be built regardless of local zoning."
**CONFIRMED, with a precision note.** Gov. Code §65912.157(n): the section "shall not apply to a local
agency until July 1, 2026" and not to unincorporated county land until the next housing cycle. It
sets *floors* a city may not go below (Tier 1: no height limit under 75 ft, no density cap under
120 units/acre within a quarter-mile of heavy rail), for projects of at least five units. It is not
"any housing" and not "no zoning".
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB79

## 2. "Los Angeles deferred SB 79 citywide until about 2030."
**CONFIRMED as the city's position; the date is an estimate.** Ordinance 188968 (urgency), effective
30 June 2026, asserts every parcel qualifies for the statute's temporary deferral under
§65912.161(b)(1)(A); the deferral runs to one year after adoption of the next housing element, which
the city estimates around 2030. No text names 2030. HCD's compliance review was pending at the time
of research. ⛔ The episode does not say HCD approved or rejected it, and does not call the deferral
"defiance" — the statute authorises deferral; the dispute is whether the city's capacity claim is true.
- https://cityclerk.lacity.org/lacityclerkconnect/index.cfm?fa=ccfi.viewrecord&cfnumber=25-1083-S4

## 3. "On 20 August 2026 three cities were sued over SB 79."
**CORRECTED.** The San Francisco petition (CPF-26-520062) carries a court stamp of **31 July 2026**.
20 August is the date of the plaintiffs' press release announcing all three actions. The brief now
says "announced 20 August" and gives the San Francisco filing date. The San Diego and Montebello
pleadings were not obtained; their contents are reported as the plaintiffs' allegations.
- https://drive.google.com/file/d/18etsAa5oJAcyYb97mS55EL_BjJVAKkKE/view
- https://www.car.org/aboutus/mediacenter/newsreleases/2026releases/CARgroupsues3cities

## 4. "San Francisco excluded 'industrial employment hubs' and the state approved it."
**CONFIRMED.** Ordinance 082-26, passed 9–0 on 5 May 2026, permanently excludes M, SALI, PDR, WMUO
and P parcels under §65912.160(e). HCD's letter of 1 July 2026 finds the ordinance substantially
compliant, contingent on the city not enforcing Planning Code §207.11(b)(5), which HCD said
"impermissibly prohibits the use of the State Density Bonus Law". Note HCD is a *respondent* in the
petition, which asks the court to compel it to revoke that approval.
- https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/o0082-26.pdf
- https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/planning-and-community/HAU/san-francisco-sb79-in-070126.pdf

## 5. "San Diego exempted sites with no sidewalk path to transit."
**CONFIRMED.** Ordinance O-22099, Recital K: sites where "no walking path of less than one mile from
a site to a TOD Stop using sidewalks exists". The statute the ordinance quotes says "walking path",
not "sidewalks". The Encanto example (a parcel 375 ft from a trolley station excluded for a dirt
alley) comes from the plaintiffs' release and press, not from a pleading read here.
- https://docs.sandiego.gov/council_reso_ordinance/rao2026/O-22099.pdf

## 6. "Texas now requires every city to allow manufactured homes — in force 1 September 2026."
**CONFIRMED, narrowed.** Enrolled SB 785 amends Occupations Code §1201.008: a zoning city "shall
permit the installation, by right, of a new HUD-code manufactured home" under **at least one**
residential or dedicated district actually applied to land in the city. One district, not every
district. Passed 2025; effective 1 September 2026.
- https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00785F.htm

## 7. "About 40 % of Texas cities — more than 1,200 — effectively banned manufactured homes."
**CORRECTED.** That is the industry association's figure, on an unstated denominator. The National
Zoning Atlas's May 2025 brief, read in full, finds **44 % of the 579 municipalities in its sample**
(255 cities, holding 79 % of the state's population) would have to change their zoning to comply:
33 % did not explicitly allow manufactured housing and 11 % allowed it only by special permit or
hearing. A quarter of the 255 could comply simply by mapping a district they had already written.
The Atlas figure and denominator are what the episode uses.
- https://www.zoningatlas.org/s/Texas-Manufactured-Housing.pdf

## 8. "Massachusetts votes on 3 November 2026 on Question 7, the starter-homes lot-size measure."
**CONFIRMED, via press reporting of the official announcement.** The Secretary of the Commonwealth
announced the ballot order on 21 July 2026; press lists Question 7 as "Single Family Homes" — the
initiative to allow single-family homes on lots of at least 5,000 sq ft with 50 ft of frontage and
public water and sewer. The Secretary's own pages block automated readers, so the number is confirmed
from two independent reports of the announcement, not the primary page.
- https://www.boston.com/news/politics/2026/07/21/there-9-ballot-questions-will-be-up-for-a-vote-in-november/
- https://ballotpedia.org/Massachusetts_Limit_on_Required_Lot_Size_for_Single-Family_Homes_Initiative_(2026)

## 9. "Montana's Supreme Court upheld the state's duplex and ADU mandate on 18 March 2026."
**CORRECTED (date) and NARROWED (holding).** The opinion in *MAID v. State*, 2026 MT 53, is dated
**17 March 2026**. The court affirmed that SB 323 and SB 528 survive an *equal-protection* challenge,
reversed an injunction against the Land Use Planning Act, and vacated as advisory the ruling on
private covenants. It did not rule on the merits of the mandates, and whether they override HOA
covenants is undecided.
- https://statecourtreport.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/montana_densification_5824b10a-9859-ceaf-8bf7-9cfdb4a00000.pdf

## 10. "The federal ROAD to Housing Act puts $200 million a year behind zoning reform."
**CONFIRMED, with the operative word.** Public Law 119-101, Sec. 208: "There is authorized to be
appropriated … $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031." Authorised, not appropriated
— no money exists until Congress appropriates it. Sec. 208(c)(1): nothing in the section lets HUD
"mandate, supersede, or preempt any local zoning or land use policy". The episode says "authorised".
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ101/pdf/PLAW-119publ101.pdf

## 11. "New Jersey's Supreme Court made it harder for developers to get variances."
**CONFIRMED, narrowed.** *Monarch v. Montville* (13 July 2026, unanimous) adds a threshold showing —
no substantial impairment of the zoning plan — before a board may grant a variance for an
*inherently beneficial* use. It is not a ruling on single-family zoning and the project was neither
approved nor denied.
- https://www.njcourts.gov/system/files/court-opinions/2026/a_70_24.pdf

## 12. "Governor Newsom has until 30 September to act on SB 1116, AB 956 and SB 677."
**CONFIRMED as to the deadline; UNRESOLVED as to content.** The session ended 31 August and the
30 September deadline is reported consistently. What each bill does was not verified from bill text
here; the episode names the deadline in the description and does not describe the bills.
- https://www.housingwire.com/articles/california-housing-bills-newsom/

## THE EVIDENCE

## 13. "Auckland upzoned three-quarters of its residential land in 2016 and permitted about 22,000 extra homes in five years."
**CONFIRMED.** Greenaway-McGrevy and Phillips, *Journal of Urban Economics* 136 (2023), read in full:
"approximately three-quarters (75.2%) of all residential land … is classified as upzoned"; the
spillover-adjusted estimate "implies that 21,808 additional dwellings were permitted over the five
years following the zoning reform, corresponding to approximately 4.11% of the dwelling stock". The
raw difference is 34,614 permits; 21,808 is the conservative figure and the one aired (rounded to
"about twenty-two thousand"). Permits, not completions.
- https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-02/p1863.pdf

## 14. "Auckland rents ended up roughly a quarter lower than they would have been."
**CONFIRMED, from the published abstract.** Greenaway-McGrevy and So, *Economic Inquiry*, published
11 July 2026: "Eight years after the reform, the synthetic control under our preferred empirical
specification implies that rents decreased by 23% due to the reform." The full text is paywalled, so
the range across specifications and the placebo p-values are not aired; the episode says "roughly a
quarter, in one published study". The scout brief's "23–28 %" range and "2025" date were from the
2023 working paper and are not used.
- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecin.70075

## 15. "Minneapolis: housing stock up 12 %, rents up 1 %, 2017–2022, while the rest of Minnesota saw +4 % and +14 %."
**CONFIRMED.** Pew, 4 January 2024, read from a Minnesota Senate committee-hosted copy of the
article (pew.org refuses automated readers). Pew also estimates that of ~21,000 units permitted,
87 % were in buildings of 20+ units and only 1 % in 2–4-unit buildings, calling the triplex policy's
impact "modest".
- https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2024/01/04/minneapolis-land-use-reforms-offer-a-blueprint-for-housing-affordability

## 16. "The Minneapolis Fed found the slow rent growth is better explained by a demand shock than by the zoning plan."
**CONFIRMED.** Hembre, Horowitz, Starling and Xu, 25 August 2025: on Census data "the median rent in
Minneapolis was $1,343, up 25.7 percent since 2019 … 3.2 percentage points less than the median rent
for the comparison group"; private indices differ widely (Apartment List −3.5 % vs +22.4 %; Zillow
+10.0 % vs +32.2 %). Their conclusion: slow rent growth "may result more from a negative housing
demand shock specific to the Minneapolis market in 2020 than the expected impact of changes in the
housing supply". Permits 2020–24: 87 duplex/triplex/fourplex buildings, 225 units.
- https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2025/unpacking-supply-and-demand-in-rent-trends-since-the-minneapolis-2040-plan

## 17. "In SB 9's first year, Los Angeles approved 38 units; San Diego approved none."
**CONFIRMED.** Terner Center, 18 January 2023, covering January–November 2022: Los Angeles 211 unit
applications, 38 approved; San Diego 7 applications, 0 approved; San Francisco 25 and 4. Barriers
named: costs and lack of building expertise, low maximum unit size, height limits, owner-occupancy,
impact fees, Subdivision Map Act requirements.
- https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/sb-9-turns-one-applications/

## 18. "SB 9 produced almost nothing."
**UNRESOLVED as a present-tense claim.** No statewide 2024 or 2025 SB 9 count was found; HCD's
annual-progress-report tables exist but no published aggregate. The episode says "in its first
year" and nothing about later years.

## 19. "Charlotte: 8,537 duplex, triplex and quad units since the 2023 UDO."
**CONFIRMED as applications.** WFAE, 13 August 2026, reporting the Charlotte Ledger's analysis:
8,537 "submitted or approved" since 1 June 2023; "hundreds" a year built before versus "thousands"
after, the latter the analyst's own estimate. The episode says "submitted or approved", never "built".
- https://www.wfae.org/business/2026-08-13/udo-three-years-later-how-many-duplexes-triplexes-and-quads-have-been-built

## 20. "Houston built nearly 80,000 homes on small lots after its minimum-lot reform."
**CORRECTED.** The 80,000 figure is a Mercatus tally of houses on lots under 5,000 sq ft, not a
peer-reviewed count. Gray and Millsap's peer-reviewed figure is **25,269** new residential parcels
under 5,000 sq ft created 1999–2016; other peer-reviewed counts reach ~39,000 for 2005–18. The
episode says "more than twenty-five thousand" and attributes it. The scout's "$340k vs $545k"
townhouse price comparison could not be traced to a primary source and is not aired.
- https://www.huduser.gov/portal/periodicals/cityscape/vol26num3/ch9.pdf

## 21. "Upzoning raised land prices in Chicago and built nothing."
**CONFIRMED as the author states it.** Freemark (2020): transit-area upzonings of 2013 and 2015
raised transaction prices on upzoned parcels and produced no increase in newly permitted dwellings
over five years. The 2013 ordinance covered about 6 % of the city's land — a narrow reform, which is
the point the episode makes with it.
- https://yonahfreemark.com/2021/04/13/upzoning-chicago-impacts-of-a-zoning-reform-on-property-values-and-housing-construction/

## 22. "Infill imposes unpriced infrastructure and school costs on existing residents."
**UNRESOLVED.** No municipal fiscal-impact study of Minneapolis 2040, Portland's infill project or
Charlotte's UDO was found by the fan-out or by hand; the only sourced cost material concerns fees
that suppress infill. GOLD is argued on air from the decider question and from the thinness of the
production and rent evidence; no cost figure is stated.

## 23. "Single-family-only zoning covers about 75 % of residential land in US cities."
**UNRESOLVED.** The figure traces to a 2019 newspaper analysis of about ten cities and is not a
national measurement. Not aired.

## 24. "Huntington Beach's challenge to California's housing mandates was denied review by the US Supreme Court in February 2026."
**UNRESOLVED.** Scout-level; not independently verified in this pass. Not aired.

## Corrections applied to source

1. **The lawsuit date.** The scout brief said the three cities were sued on 20 August 2026. The San
   Francisco petition was filed 31 July; 20 August is the plaintiffs' announcement. The brief and the
   description say "announced 20 August".
2. **The Texas figure.** "About 40 % of Texas cities" (industry association) replaced by the National
   Zoning Atlas's 44 % of a 579-city sample, with the denominator stated.
3. **The Montana date and holding.** 18 March corrected to 17 March; "upheld the mandate" narrowed to
   "survived an equal-protection challenge; the covenant question was vacated as advisory".
4. **The Houston count.** "Nearly 80,000" (Mercatus tally) replaced by the peer-reviewed 25,269
   (1999–2016), attributed; the townhouse price comparison dropped.
5. **The Auckland rent figure's provenance.** The scout's "23–28 %, Greenaway-McGrevy 2025" replaced
   by the published *Economic Inquiry* abstract (11 July 2026, Greenaway-McGrevy and So): 23 % under
   the preferred specification.
6. **The ROAD Act money.** "$200m/yr Innovation Fund" now reads "authorised, not appropriated".
7. **The framing.** The scout treated the pegs as instances of abolishing single-family-only zoning.
   None of them is; the brief's reframe now says so and the episode argues dose and decider.
