Single-Family Zoning: Should Cities Abolish It?

Should cities abolish single-family-only zoning?

Last changed 2026-11-07 · See every change

▶ Rewatch on YouTube

Nobody abolished it. The state answered over the city's head — and the cities are being sued for how they answered back.

Settled on the record·Still argued

Why it was in the news

When this episode came out in September 2026, three statehouses, one supreme court and Congress had all acted on this question that year. California's law on housing near transit took effect in July, and housing groups went to court against San Francisco, San Diego and Montebello over how they applied it.

Settled

What both sides accept

Nothing in the news abolished single-family-only zoning. Where the change was broad and by right, it built, and where it was narrow with strings, it didn't. Both teams also agreed that legalizing homes isn't the same as building them.

Still argued

The fight

Green arguesEnd it, make it usable

Where the change was broad and by right, it built: Auckland rezoned three-quarters of its residential land and got about 22,000 extra homes permitted. Los Angeles legalized duplexes with strings attached and approved 38 in the first year. The people a city keeps out can't vote there, so the state should hold the pen.

▶ Hear Green at 3:12 on YouTube
Gold arguesThe neighbourhood's say

Charlotte wrote its own rules to be usable and has had about 8,500 duplex, triplex and quad units submitted or approved. Los Angeles had the state's rule handed to it and got a deferral and no homes. People on a street have no vote in Sacramento, only at city hall, so keep the say and price the change honestly.

▶ Hear Gold at 7:52 on YouTube

What the shouting gets wrong

Both teams ended up arguing over two dials: how much a rule allows by right, and who turns it, the state or the street. On the first dial the record is clear, since broad, usable rules built and narrow ones didn't. Both sides even wanted the same tools, a floor, rules written to be usable and a court that can check the map, so what's left is who should hold the pen.

What our fact-check caught

4 of the 24 claims we checked came back wrong in our drafts. Every one is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidHousing groups sued three California cities on August 20.

    The record saysThe San Francisco case was filed on July 31. August 20 is when the groups announced all three cases.

  2. Draft saidAbout 40% of Texas cities, more than 1,200, effectively banned manufactured homes.

    The record saysThat was an industry group's figure. A zoning study of 579 Texas cities found 44% would have to change their rules to comply.

  3. Draft saidMontana's Supreme Court upheld the state's duplex mandate on March 18.

    The record saysThe ruling came on March 17, and it held only that the mandates survive an equal-protection challenge. The question of private covenants was left undecided.

  4. Draft saidHouston built nearly 80,000 homes on small lots after it cut its minimum lot size.

    The record saysThat's a think-tank tally. The peer-reviewed count is about 25,000 new small lots from 1999 to 2016.
