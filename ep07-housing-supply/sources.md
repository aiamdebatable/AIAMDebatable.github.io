# Sources — "Does building more housing actually lower rents?"

_Grouped by claim. Every on-screen number and named case traces here. Verdicts in `factcheck-log.md`._

## The Austin case
- ~120,000 units added, ~30% stock growth 2015–2024; asking rents −4% (2021→2025) city and suburbs;
  median rent $1,546 (Dec 2021) → $1,296 (Jan 2026) = −16.2%; Class C −11.4% vs Class A −2.6%;
  50+ unit buildings −7% (2023→2024) — Pew, Clifford/Rodnyansky/Su, 18 Mar 2026 (rents: Apartment List;
  supply: ACS): https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2026/03/18/austins-surge-of-new-housing-construction-drove-down-rents
- ⚠️ Causation NOT established by this source — no methodology, no regression, and demand softening
  (tech layoffs, migration reversal) is never addressed. See factcheck-log §5.
- A 10% ZIP-code supply increase ≈ 1.4pp slower rent growth (1,654 Zillow ZIP codes, 2017–2024) —
  Pew, Rodnyansky/Su/Horowitz, 31 Jul 2025 (**this** study, not the Austin article):
  https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2025/07/31/new-housing-slows-rent-growth-most-for-older-more-affordable-units

## Neighbourhood-level effects — the contested core
- Nearby rents −5–7% within ~250m of large new buildings in low-income areas; three DiD designs;
  1,483 buildings 50+ units; 11 strong-market cities; restricted to "pioneer" buildings, all-buildings
  extension "indistinguishable from zero" — Asquith, Mast & Reed, *REStat* 2023, 105(2):359–75:
  https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article-abstract/105/2/359/100977
- No average effect on nearby rents, but **+4.4% over five years** on the cheapest tercile of existing
  buildings; DiD 300m vs 300–800m, Minneapolis 2000–2018 — Damiano & Frenier, *Journal of Urban
  Affairs* 2026 (⚠️ the widely-circulated +6.7%/−1.7% pair is a pre-2020 draft):
  https://www.tonydamiano.com/project/new-con/bbb-wp.pdf
- Displacement (moves to poorer ZIPs) −17.14% within 500m, yet parcels within 100m 2.5pp (29.5%
  relative) more likely to gentrify — Pennington, "Rents, Movers, and Stayers," Nov 2025
  (⚠️ **unrefereed working paper**):
  https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c51b33a5417fc03ad2b3fdb/t/6914f5de8c66691ed5595365/1762981342282/SF_Housing_Spillovers.pdf

## Filtering — does it reach the poor, and how fast
- Per 100 new market-rate units, 45–70 people vacate below-median-income tracts and 17–39 vacate
  bottom-quintile tracts, almost all within five years (⚠️ address-history tracing + simulation, not a
  quasi-experiment) — Mast, *Journal of Urban Economics* 2023, 133:103383:
  https://research.upjohn.org/up_workingpapers/307/
- Real annual rental filtering ~2.5%/year vs 0.5% owner-occupied — Rosenthal, *AER* 2014, 104(2):687–706:
  https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.104.2.687
- Moving chains from central high-end units reach middle- and low-income neighbourhoods within rounds;
  Helsinki register data, 3,196 units — Bratu, Harjunen & Saarimaa, *JUE* 2023, 133:103528:
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094119022001048

## Does upzoning produce buildings?
- Chicago transit upzonings (2013/2015): allowed density +17–50%, parcel and condo values +15–23%,
  **no** effect on permitted dwellings over five years (⚠️ abstract limits findings to "short-term,
  local-level"; author objects to anti-upzoning readings) — Freemark, *Urban Affairs Review* 2020,
  56(3):758–789: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1078087418824672
- Auckland 2016 upzoning: rents ~28% below synthetic control at six years, range 14–35% by dwelling
  type; 2017–2024, 51 NZ commuting zones — Greenaway-McGrevy & So, *Economic Inquiry* 2026:
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ecin.70075
- Peer-reviewed rebuttal to the Auckland critiques — "Dispelling myths: Reviewing the evidence on
  zoning reforms in Auckland," *Land Use Policy* 2025, doi 10.1016/j.landusepol.2025.107497
- ⚠️ The prominent critique is an **unreviewed Substack post** targeting a different (construction)
  paper — Murray & Helm: https://www.fresheconomicthinking.com/p/the-auckland-myth-there-is-no-evidence
- Auckland consents 12.7 per 1,000 residents (Jul 2022) — Stats NZ (⚠️ **not** a national record;
  Canterbury was 13.2)

## Why approved housing does not get built
- Construction inputs +42.2%, new nonresidential building +42.5%, Feb 2020 → Jan 2026 — BLS PPI via AGC:
  https://www.agc.org/sites/default/files/users/user21902/PPI%20Tables%202026_1.pdf
- SF hard costs $360K/unit low-rise to $450K/unit high-rise, 50–75% of total development cost
  (⚠️ 2020 white paper on 2017–19 data, pricing **market-rate** rental) — SF Planning, *Housing
  Development Feasibility and Costs*:
  https://default.sfplanning.org/plans-and-programs/housing/affordability-strategy/HAS_Feasibility_and_Dev_Costs_FInal.pdf

## The politics of supply
- 63% of comments at zoning/planning board meetings were in opposition (22.8% neutral); 97 eastern
  Massachusetts municipalities, 2015–2017, matched to the state voter file. ⚠️ **The authors state
  homeownership "we cannot assess using the voter file"** — the disproportionately-homeowners claim is
  NOT supported — Einstein, Glick & Palmer, *Perspectives on Politics* 17(1), 2019:
  https://maxwellpalmer.com/docs/articles/Einstein_Glick_Palmer_Participation.pdf
- Owners' equity in household real estate **$34,148.5B (Q4 2025)** — Federal Reserve Z.1, Table B.101,
  line 51: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/20260319/html/b101.htm
- Median net worth: homeowners $396,200, renters $10,400 — Federal Reserve SCF 2022:
  https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/scf/dataviz/scf/table/

## Synthesis of the literature
- ⚠️ **No formal meta-analysis of new-supply effects on rents exists.** The best synthesis is a
  **narrative review** — Been, Ellen & O'Regan, "Supply Skepticism Revisited," *Housing Policy Debate*
  35(1):96–113, 2024: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10511482.2024.2418044
