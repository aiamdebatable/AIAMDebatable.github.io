# Fact-check log — Should You Be Able to Bet on Your Own Election?

_Independent verification pass, run 2026-10-03 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). Each claim gets a
verdict: **CONFIRMED**, **CORRECTED**, **UNRESOLVED** or **APPROXIMATE**. This is a REQUIRED gate, set out
under "Verification gate" in the episodes README. The show's differentiator is that this file is real._

- **CONFIRMED** — the source says what the episode says.
- **CORRECTED** — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under
  "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
- **UNRESOLVED** — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.
- **APPROXIMATE** — checked, roughly right, and **deliberately not cited on air** as a precise figure.

**Verification status:** adversarial-pass _(a deep-research fan-out with single-vote primary verification,
then a hand pass that opened each primary: CFTC orders and Federal Register documents, court opinions,
statutes, bill texts, the exchanges' own notices, Pew's tables)_

⛔ No live odds or market prices appear in this log or the episode. Enforcement subjects are named by role.

## Why now — the peg, and what was weighed against it
**Chosen:** the CFTC's 2026 reversal (withdrawal in February; the June proposed rule), with the states
moving the other way (Washington's order, Arizona's charges, Pew's map) and the courts unsettled (Ninth
Circuit remand; a Supreme Court response due after the vote), plus this summer's two CFTC settlements and
the September staff advisory.
**Weighed and rejected:** a final rule (not issued); a Supreme Court decision (impossible before the
vote); live odds shifts (partisan by construction); trading-volume growth (driven by sports); state suits
about sports only (New York, Missouri, a 44-attorneys-general letter).

## 1. 2012: the CFTC's one election-integrity reason
**CONFIRMED.** The contracts "can potentially be used in ways that would have an adverse effect on the
integrity of elections, for example by creating monetary incentives to vote for particular candidates even
when such a vote may be contrary to the voter's political views". Its other reasons were legal and economic.
- https://www.cftc.gov/stellent/groups/public/@rulesandproducts/documents/ifdocs/nadexorder040212.pdf

## 2. 2023: the CFTC's integrity worries about congressional-control contracts
**APPROXIMATE.** The order raised several ("integrity of elections, or the perception of integrity";
manipulation in both directions; misinformation; trading bans that miss people). It made no finding that
manipulation had happened — it recorded commenters who cited examples and others who disputed them. The
line "not equipped or well-suited" to police elections is the commenters', relayed by the order (the 2026
proposal paraphrases it as the Commission's own). The episode says "several worries", not "four findings".
- https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/filings/documents/2023/orgkexkalshiordersig230922.pdf

## 3. A federal judge ruled the CFTC had no legal hook, and did not decide the public interest
**CONFIRMED.** "Kalshi's contracts do not involve unlawful activity or gaming. They involve elections,
which are neither." And: "this Court has no occasion to consider that argument" (D.D.C., 12 Sept 2024).
- https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.261465/gov.uscourts.dcd.261465.51.0_2.pdf

## 4. The CFTC dropped its appeal (May 2025); no appeals court ruled
**CONFIRMED.** Unopposed voluntary dismissal, each side bearing its own costs. One news outlet later wrote
that the appeals court "ruled"; that is wrong.
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/06/12/2026-11854.txt (fn 149)

## 5. February 2026: the CFTC withdrew its 2024 proposed ban
**CONFIRMED.** "The Commission does not intend to issue final rules with respect to this proposal."
Withdrawn as of 4 Feb 2026, published 6 Feb.
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/02/06/2026-02454.txt

## 6. June 2026: the proposed rule calls the old orders' reasoning "incorrect"
**CORRECTED (scope).** An earlier note from this pass (peg hunt) said the proposal called the old
reasoning incorrect "on one point" only. Read in full, it does so on four: the 2012 order's "economic
purpose" test; both orders' reading of "involve"; the 2023 order's state-law prong; and both orders'
equating of gaming with gambling — "political elections are not gaming". It lists "results of political
elections" among contracts that "would generally fall outside" the rule. It restates the 2012 and 2023
integrity worries as history, with no "incorrect" label, and does not adopt them. Not final.
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/06/12/2026-11854.txt

## 7. 23 states make election betting entirely illegal; 9 in some cases
**CONFIRMED.** Pew's table. Pew's "no explicit law" category means no statute that names election betting,
not "legal" (Washington is in it, and a Washington court has acted under general gambling law).
- https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/06/23/more-than-half-of-states-restrict-betting-on-elections/

## 8. Wisconsin and New York bar a person who has bet on an election from voting in it; Delaware strips the vote too
**CORRECTED.** Wisconsin §6.03(2): "No person shall be allowed to vote in any election in which the person
has made or become interested … in any bet or wager depending upon the result of the election." New York
Const. art. II §3 says the same (wording to be re-read in a browser before air). **Delaware** (Const.
art. V §7) makes the bet a misdemeanor but says "the penalty of disfranchisement shall not apply" to
bettors — the opposite of Pew's summary. The episode does not say Delaware strips the vote.
- https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/6.03
- https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNS/A2S3
- https://delcode.delaware.gov/constitution/constitution.pdf

## 9. Oregon and Utah bar candidates from betting on their own races; in Utah it is a felony
**CONFIRMED.** Oregon ORS 260.635 (penalty not read). Utah 20A-1-606: a candidate's bet is a third-degree
felony; anyone else's is a class B misdemeanor.
- https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title20A/Chapter1/20A-1-S606.html

## 10. Washington's court ordered an exchange to stop election, politics and mention markets in the state
**CONFIRMED (with a correction).** King County Superior Court, 12 Aug 2026, under general gambling and
consumer-protection law. It is a preliminary injunction; the attorney general's release calls it "a final
order", which overstates it.
- https://agportal-s3bucket.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Consumer%20Protection/64_OrdGrantAmendMtnPI.pdf?VersionId=a9FvcaBwWpQETWxbKUdHL1jqjTOD6XAi
- https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/judge-orders-kalshi-cease-numerous-washington-operations

## 11. Arizona charged an exchange with 20 counts, four for election wagering
**CONFIRMED (counts); CORRECTED (the injunction).** A federal court blocked the prosecution at the request
of the United States and the CFTC (preliminary injunction, 5 May 2026); Arizona appealed on 6 July. A news
headline called the injunction "permanent"; it is preliminary.
- https://www.azag.gov/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-charges-kalshi-illegal-gambling-operation-election-wagering

## 12. Minnesota passed the first statewide ban on prediction markets; the federal government sued the next day
**CONFIRMED.** Signed 18 May 2026; CFTC/United States complaint filed 19 May. A July injunction against the
law is reported in the press only; not used as settled.
- https://www.cftc.gov/media/13941/OGC_MinnesotaComplaint051926/download

## 13. Kentucky banned election betting
**UNRESOLVED.** The enrolled text of HB 904 (2026) contains no election-betting ban; it bars licensed
operators from working with prediction markets. Pew's one-line statement could not be traced to a statute.
Not used on air.

## 14. Tennessee made it a felony to try to influence an event you hold a contract on
**CORRECTED (date).** Public Chapter 1109, signed 27 May 2026 (not 22 May), effective 1 July; a Class E
felony; covers any event, not only elections.
- https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/114/pub/pc1109.pdf

## 15. Ninth Circuit: sports preemption rejected; the election-contract question sent back
**CONFIRMED.** "We remand for the district court to consider Kalshi's election contracts." No ruling on
elections. The appeals-court split (Third v. Ninth) is about sports.
- https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/28/25-7516.pdf
- https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/251922p.pdf

## 16. The Supreme Court petition is about sports; the response is due 9 November, after the vote
**CONFIRMED.**
- https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-299.html

## 17. A former member of Congress paid about $35,000 for trading on whether he'd attend the State of the Union
**CONFIRMED.** Consent order: disgorgement $17,569.98 plus a $17,500 penalty; three-year trading ban; the
theory is manipulation via misleading posts while holding positions.
- https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9276-26

## 18. A White House teleprompter operator paid about $172,500 for trading on words the President would say
**CONFIRMED.** Disgorgement $107,539.02 plus a $65,000 penalty (about a 40% cooperation reduction);
three-year ban; misappropriation of confidential speech text. The CFTC thanked the exchange for its help.
- https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9289-26

## 19. CFTC staff: one-person contracts are "presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation"
**CONFIRMED, with a balance note.** The advisory lists "election results" once, among outcomes "outside the
control of any single person"; it does not assess election contracts' manipulability either way, and it
notes a "small or select group" acting together could create comparable risks.
- https://www.cftc.gov/csl/26-27/download

## 20. Kalshi suspended three congressional candidates in April, from both parties' primaries
**CONFIRMED.** Kalshi's own notice: two Democratic-primary and one Republican-primary candidate; five-year
suspensions; fines $539.85, $784.20 and $6,229.30. More suspensions followed in August; the parties of
those cases are not all known, so "both parties" is used for April only.
- https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-political-insider-trading-enforcement-update

## 21. The exchanges bar candidates, campaign staff and vote counters from election markets
**CORRECTED.** An earlier note said no exchange document named poll workers. Kalshi's market-integrity
page bars "anyone working in a vote-tallying capacity" and walks through a poll-worker example; its
rulebook's operative bar is Rule 5.17 (not 5.15). Polymarket US bars anyone with "authority or influence
sufficient to affect the outcome".
- https://kalshi.com/market-integrity/blocked-traders
- https://integrity.polymarket.us/

## 22. Whether federal law reaches a candidate trading on their own race is unclear
**APPROXIMATE.** CRS: the CFTC called such a trade a potential violation but has not brought a case, and
"at least one commentator has questioned whether it would be viable". Not "untested".
- https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/LSB/PDF/LSB11406/LSB11406.3.pdf

## 23. A Pennsylvania county added prediction markets to its poll workers' oath
**CORRECTED (wording).** Delaware County's election board voted unanimously on 24 March 2026 to add "a
prohibition on direct or indirect participation in bets, wagers or prediction markets". The exact new oath
sentence circulated online is not in the minutes and is not used. Pennsylvania's election code already
has poll workers swear they have no interest "in any bet or wager on the result".
- https://delawarecopa.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=5571,plainText=false)
- https://www.votebeat.org/pennsylvania/2026/04/15/delaware-county-poll-worker-prediction-market-election-betting-wager-ban/

## 24. Los Angeles County's election chief: returns that differed from market expectations led to suspicion of ballot counting
**CORRECTED (wording).** Not "a wrong market price": "early election returns differed from market
expectations, and that resulted in suspicion and questioning of normal ballot processing" (June 2026
mayoral primary). Reported by Votebeat from a webinar we could not access.
- https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/08/10/election-officials-worried-prediction-betting-markets-kalshi-polymarket/

## 25. Kalshi politics volume: $1.13 billion in Nov 2024; $169 million in July 2026; $53 billion total that month
**CONFIRMED.** Pew's table (data from The Block, notional volume).
- https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/23/prediction-markets-trading-volume-doubled-between-may-and-july-largely-driven-by-sports/

## 26. Politics is 4% of Kalshi's volume and 32% of Polymarket's since July 2024
**CORRECTED.** Not on Pew's page. Summing Pew's table gives about 2.4% and 24%. The episode says "a small
slice of Kalshi's, about a quarter of Polymarket's" if used at all.

## 27. 2012: one trader held a candidate's price up for hours on Election Day, and lost close to $7 million
**CORRECTED (loss).** Rothschild & Sethi: "Trader B lost close to $7 million" ($6,882,186), having spent
"over $375,000 to hold prices in place" in one 90-minute window; when he pulled out, the price "shot up
quickly to reach the Betfair level". An earlier note said "$4 million", which is a count of contracts.
- https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/local/Social/f25/wrap/readings/Rothschild+Sethi-prediction-markets-2016.pdf

## 28. A single trader moved a 2024 election price
**UNRESOLVED.** No peer-reviewed source found; a preprint finds money on both sides. Not used.

## 29. A university market beat 964 polls about three times in four (1988–2004)
**CONFIRMED.** Berg, Nelson & Rietz: "The market is closer to the eventual outcome 74% of the time"; mean
error 1.82 vs 3.37 points. Small-stakes academic market; one line only.
- https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/trietz/papers/long%20run%20accuracy.pdf

## 30. News outlets now show market prices through data deals
**CONFIRMED (CNBC, Dow Jones); APPROXIMATE (CNN, CBS).** CNBC–Kalshi (4 Dec 2025) and Dow Jones–Polymarket
(7 Jan 2026) from the companies' releases; CNN's partnership confirmed by CNN's own disclosure, dated from
Axios; CBS's midterm use of Kalshi data from trade press only. No study of the effect on viewers was found.
- https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/04/cnbc-and-kalshi-strike-exclusive-partnership.html

## 31. Congress: a Republican-led insider bill, Democratic-led bans, and one bipartisan ban
**CONFIRMED.** H.R. 9367 (Republican-led; Members, spouses, dependent children; ordered reported 5–4,
24 June 2026). STOP Corrupt Bets Act (H.R. 8123 / S. 4226; Democratic-led; bars election contracts for
everyone). H.R. 7840 (introduced by a Republican with a Democratic co-lead; bars contracts on "the result of
any vote in an election"). None is law.
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr9367ih/xml/BILLS-119hr9367ih.xml
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr8123ih/xml/BILLS-119hr8123ih.xml
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr7840ih/xml/BILLS-119hr7840ih.xml

## 32. "Eight senators" wrote to the CFTC in April
**CORRECTED.** Seven senators and one House member (all one party). The CFTC's own proposal also says
"eight U.S. Senators"; that is its error.
- https://www.merkley.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/26.04.30-CFTC-Event-Contract-Letter.pdf

## 33. Britain allows political betting but prosecutes betting on inside information as cheating
**CONFIRMED.** Gambling Act 2005 s.42; four guilty pleas so far (29 June and 10 Sept 2026) over bets on the
2024 election DATE; ten defendants await trial. A bet on timing, not on a result or a count.
- https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/two-admit-general-election-betting-offences
- https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/two-more-admit-general-election-betting-offences

## Fairness audit
- Both teams' strongest evidence is stated with its weakness (Green's forecasting record is old and
  small-stakes; Gold's vote-buying fear has no case behind it; "regulated beats offshore" is unmeasured).
- Party balance: the April suspensions span both parties' primaries; Congress has bills led from each side
  and one bipartisan. Both CFTC settlements and all UK pleas involve one side each — named by role only,
  never as a party pattern; never "both parties do it equally".
- No odds, prices or market screens.
- Cast rotation: everyone from the previous debate rests; no one holds the seat they held two debates
  ago. The restrictive rail (gold, "bar the bets") is argued by a woman this episode, after a man last
  time; the other rail by a man. The seating was also chosen so it does not repeat an earlier episode's
  seating exactly. The judge, the desk lead and the ringmaster are each in that seat for the first time
  in this run of episodes.
