# Research brief — Episode 2 · Should Nuclear Anchor Clean Energy?

_Reconstructed from the episode's spoken script + on-screen data (backfilled 2026-07-16). This is the
15-minute data-forward episode that proved the render pipeline before Episode 3._

## The question
**Should nuclear be the centerpiece of our clean-energy future?** Online it's one big yes/no fight;
the research shows most of it isn't a debate at all.

## Settled (not the debate)
1. **Nuclear is about as clean as wind.** Lifecycle emissions ~12 g CO₂/kWh — same class as wind, far
   below gas or coal.
2. **Nuclear is about as safe as wind.** ~0.07 deaths/TWh including Chernobyl & Fukushima, vs. ~25
   (coal) and ~3 (gas) — though 0.07 sits at one end of a genuinely disputed range (Chernobyl
   estimates run from <100 confirmed to WHO's ~4,000 projected to Greenpeace's tens of thousands;
   Fukushima: ~1 radiation death but ~2,200 from the evacuation itself).
3. **Western new-build has been a financial disaster.** Vogtle ~$14B→~$35B (7 yrs late); Hinkley
   Point C £18B→toward £50B; Flamanville €3B→>€13B (12 yrs late).
4. **But it's not the technology — it's who's building.** Korea's Barakah built its 4th unit at ~40%
   of the first's cost; China connects reactors in ~6 years vs. ~9 worldwide.
5. **Capacity factor is nuclear's real edge:** ~92% (2024) vs. wind ~34%, solar ~23% — it runs almost
   all the time, which is why AI data centers are suddenly contracting for it.
6. **The cheapest firm clean power is extending existing reactors** (~$30–50/MWh), cheaper than
   building anything new.

## Contested (the real fight)
Given nuclear's real cost/speed record, should it still be a **centerpiece** of clean-grid plans — or
can **storage + renewables + transmission** do the job cheaper and faster? Both sides accept the
settled facts and both agree renewables carry *most* of a clean grid.

## The reframe
The fight isn't "clean or safe" (settled) or "were Western builds expensive" (settled) — it's the
**hard last stretch**: the still, dark winter weeks when wind and sun underdeliver for days, and what
firms the grid then.

## Both sides quietly agree
**Keeping today's reactors running is the cheapest firm clean power there is.** When both opponents
reach for the same fact, notice it.

## Verdict framing
No winner. Green's weak link: its claim that CCS & long-duration storage are less proven is an
extrapolation. Gold's weak link: its warning that rebuilding the industry takes decades is a
projection. The real question is handed to the viewer.
