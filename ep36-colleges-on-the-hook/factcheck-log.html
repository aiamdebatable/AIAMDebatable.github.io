Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both teams got the same treatment: every claim re-opened at its primary. The record is not symmetric. On who carries the risk today, the record is clear: the borrower, the parent and the taxpayer, never the school (sections 18–19). On whether making colleges carry it would help, both sides argue from predictions: no study measures risk-sharing on degree-granting colleges; the only behavioural evidence is 1990s default sanctions on for-profits (row 18.38). The brief says so.
  • Both sides of "make colleges carry the risk" are steelmanned from named sources (section 18): its advocates (Looney and Yannelis, Cooper) and its critics, including its own designers' warning about access, the House formula's critics, and the needed low-pay fields ED's commenters raised.
  • Advocacy is attributed. AEI, Brookings, CAP, Urban, New America, FREOPP, EPI and the Burning Glass Institute appear as named analyses, never as the record itself. Sallie Mae's survey is attributed as the largest private student lender's.
  • No politics. The 2025 law, the House bill and the 2027 rule are described by what their text does. No line frames the episode for or against an administration or party.
  • No field is singled out by politics. The graduates-vs-jobs table applies one rule to all 73 NY Fed majors and reports where every field lands. The worst on underemployment are criminal justice and the performing arts, then fine arts; other fields are reported where they land, beside business management, biology and political science.
  • A needed job is not mocked or blamed. The teacher case is a needed degree at below-average pay; it is used as the hard case for both sides, and a generic teacher is "they", never "she".
  • Who the averages leave out is said. Scorecard earnings cover federal-aid recipients only; the 2027 test sees only graduates; ED publishes no Parent PLUS default rate.
