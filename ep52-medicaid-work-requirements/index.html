Read the verdict as it aired

Raj No winner here. What's settled: the rule alone hasn't been shown to raise work. In Nebraska, almost everyone who lost coverage didn't answer, rather than being found idle. The records see earnings, not hours. And in any single month of twenty twenty-two, roughly half the adults it reaches didn't work eighty hours.

Raj Still open: whether Nebraska's four hundred and ninety qualified, and whether a records-first design can shrink the paperwork losses. On the evidence about work, the record leans toward Green. On whether Medicaid should carry a duty at all, evidence can't decide, and majorities say it should.

Raj Green's best ground: the check mostly caught people who didn't answer, the agency's own model expects people who meet the rules to lose coverage anyway, and a cut leaves no marketplace help. Gold's best ground: most people pass or are excused, Georgia's paperwork share fell once it ran, and the improper money is real.

Raj Which leaves the dial: what should keeping Medicaid ask of an adult who can work? Nothing extra: Medicaid with no work condition, the way states ran expansion before this rule. But on a one-month test, about half didn't work eighty hours, the gap supporters point to.

Raj Let the data check it: keep a condition, but confirm it from records and ask only what they can't show, as the rule already requires first. But records see earnings, not hours, and Arkansas still closed eighteen thousand cases after excusing two-thirds by data match.

Raj Ask once, keep it light: the federal floor, one qualifying month per review, household income counts, thirty days to answer, the way Nebraska runs it. But its first two months lost four hundred and ninety people who didn't answer, with no plans to add staff. Settings two and three are where the teams nearly meet.

Raj Ask, and pay for the help: the work rule bundled with job search and training, like the nineteen-nineties welfare-to-work trials. But only three of eleven of those programs raised employment by more than three points over five years, and family income usually didn't rise.

Raj Check at the front door: prove it when you apply, as Indiana will ask applicants for three months running, then stop re-checking. But Nebraska's first applicants show the same pattern: more than eight in ten of their work-rule denials were for not answering. Where it belongs is your call.

The judge named no sides on this one, so there's no dial: we don't invent positions.
