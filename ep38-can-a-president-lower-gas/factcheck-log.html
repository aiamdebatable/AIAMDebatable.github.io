Fairness audit

  • No false balance on the mechanism. The record leans hard: crude, priced in a world market, is about half the gallon, and the levers built for the pump are worth cents. The episode does not stage a 50/50 fight over that. The contested part is narrow and real: whether those cents are worth their bills.
  • Not overclaimed either. It never says "economists say presidents can't move prices". The 2012 statement is about what mostly drove a decade of prices; a 2026 survey has only a bit over half of energy experts saying "no or very little influence"; Kilian and Zhou find the reserve worked, modestly; and the diplomacy and conflict lever moves dollars in both directions. That is the honest steelman for "presidents matter", and it gets a beat.
  • Both parties, every lever. Reserve releases under presidents of both parties (1991, 2005, 2011, 2022, 2026), exchanges (2000, 2021), Jones Act waivers (2005, 2011, 2012, 2017, 2026), fuel waivers (2022–24, 2025–26), state tax holidays under governors of both parties, a federal holiday called for in 2022 and 2026 and enacted by neither, requests to OPEC in 2018 and 2022 with no documented effect. The record cuts against every administration's claim of large effects, not one party's.
  • The shock is named, not the decider. "A war involving Iran began on 28 February", as EIA writes it. Government acts are named as EIA and DOE name them ("the United States", "DOE").
  • Quotes are role-only and trimmed. Each presidential transcript used also attacks the other party or a foreign leader; those lines are excluded, and every quote is followed by what the pump did next.
  • No 2026 blame-by-party number. The only party split aired is agreement on the mechanism (97% and 84% say the conflict pushes prices up) and the 2006→2012 flip, which implicates both sides equally.
  • Role only, by Lucas's ruling (2026-10-08). The sitting president is "the President" throughout; past presidents by role and year; quotes in sections 15–16 are trimmed or bracketed to keep names out. The diesel order is judged by the same three questions as every other lever (who holds it, what it is worth, did it happen), beside the 2022 holiday call; states that eased and states that refused appear together, by state, never by party. The White House text's partisan line (16.15) and Kentucky's (16.24) stay off air.
  • Refiners are not villains by our words, and not cleared either. The margin is measured (15.8, 15.11) and so, since hand pass F, are the four largest independents' profits (17.1–17.6): about double a weak 2025 per barrel, not a record against 2022. Companies are named only as the authors of their own filings, and their explanation is attributed to them (17.12). Investigations are reported as opened, not found (17.19–17.23); no finding of wrongdoing exists either way (17.18), and Q3 is not reported (17.7). The senators' letters went to producers, not these refiners, and their "windfall" figure stays off air (17.20).
  • Advocacy and models are labelled. Treasury's and the Dallas Fed's figures are models; the Bipartisan Policy Center and CRFB figures are advocacy estimates; trade-press "pennies" is trade press.
