Gas Prices: Why So High If America Makes Its Own Oil?

The question: If America makes its own oil, why is gas over $4 a gallon, and what can the government actually do about it?

▶ Watch on YouTubeThe full record ↓

America makes more oil than it uses, but sells it at the world price. Two AI teams argue which levers are worth pulling (the President's, Congress's or your state's) and what they cost; you set the dial.

Why anyone is asking now

When this episode came out in October 2026, regular gas averaged $4.35 a gallon, $1.23 more than a year earlier, after fighting effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz. On October 5 the President signed an order on dyed diesel, the untaxed fuel that farms and job sites use off the road.

The fight

Green arguesPull the levers

Doing nothing costs about $570 a year for a typical car, and households under $40,000 cut their gas use by about 7%. In a real shortage, release the reserve and pause the tax where the pause reaches drivers, as Georgia's reached them at 64% in 2026. And keep a light on the refinery's slice, which about doubled.

Gold arguesKeep the cushion

The levers built for the pump are worth cents, and each comes with a bill. The reserve is at its lowest since 1982, and a federal tax holiday is Congress's call and would come out of road money. Keep the cushion for a shortage that empties the pumps, and tell drivers the truth: the price is the world's.

What both sides quietly agree on

America makes more oil than it uses, but sells it, and the fuel, at the world price. About half your gallon is the oil, and the tax is a set number of cents. In 2026, the refinery's slice was about double a normal year.

What the shouting gets wrong

Two common claims fell: that it's our oil so it should be cheap, and that a president can't do anything. The levers are real, they're just measured in cents, and the biggest promise, a federal gas-tax holiday, was never the President's to keep. Judge any promise with four questions: which slice of the gallon, by when, paid for with what, and who actually holds that lever.
