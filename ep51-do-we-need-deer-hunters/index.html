Read the verdict as it aired

Mara Nobody wins this one outright. Here's what the record settled, what each side earned, and then the dial is yours.

Mara Settled: we still need hunters; nothing else removes deer across a whole state. They mostly leave by skipping years, and a generation isn't being replaced. Mentoring adults is the one fix with decent numbers. And nothing tried has turned Michigan's or Wisconsin's line around.

Mara Green earned this: the door revolves, so people can come back, mentoring keeps about four in ten new adults, and hunters given room took more antlerless deer. Gold earned this: the proven programs are small, the cheap fixes barely registered, and Illinois's hunters haven't grown their harvest in twenty-four years.

Mara What didn't hold up: that disease drove them off, that people turned against hunting, that licenses got too expensive, or that Michigan's baiting ban emptied the woods. Each explains little or nothing on the record. And who should pay is a choice the record can't make for you.

Mara So the dial: how hard should your state bet on hunters? Win them back: mentor adults, cheap first licenses, friends recruiting friends, like Wisconsin's Hunt for Food. But every Wisconsin program was running while its gun-deer sales fell about ten percent.

Mara Aim the hunters we have: give them room to take female deer where the herd is, like Michigan's public-land antlerless seasons. But Michigan's earlier loosening got no big response, and this year its commission voted to cut antlerless chances from twenty twenty-seven, over its biologists, says Michigan Farm Bureau, which opposed the vote.

Mara Hire help where hunters can't go: sharpshooters for parks, suburbs and disease hot spots, paid by the town or the taxpayer, about six hundred and ten dollars a deer in Solon. But Michigan's DNR says sterilization is no replacement for hunting, and Illinois suspended its sharpshooting for good.

Mara Make everyone pay: a broad conservation tax like Missouri's, about sixty-two percent of its department's money, paid by every shopper. But the federal version has never passed the Senate. Where your state sets that dial decides who takes the deer, and who pays. Where you'd set it is up to you.

The judge named no sides on this one, so there's no dial: we don't invent positions.
