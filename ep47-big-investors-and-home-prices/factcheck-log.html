Fairness audit

  • Neither side is written beyond its evidence. Green's best figures are primary and on the record: Gorback-Qian-Zhu's faster price and rent growth, Barbieri-Dobbels' +3.2% and measured concentration effect on rents, Hanson's rent markups, Coven's own top-decile +6.2%, and the Dutch shift to first-time buyers (rows 114, 135, 137, 139, 141, 145). So are its limits: "outbid with cash" is unmeasured, the price studies are local and mostly before 2015, no study shows a ban lowers prices, and the Dutch effect showed only in early-adopting cities (rows 123, 138, 142, 172, 234). Gold's best figures are primary too: the 0.59–0.7% national share, the net selling, GAO's 38–64% to owner-occupants, the Dutch ban's flat prices, Barbieri-Dobbels' net rent fall (rows 54, 64, 69, 73, 140, 142, 231). So are its limits: every rent backfire result is a model (Coven's forced exit, Barbieri-Dobbels' Atlanta model), the one measured Dutch rent rise is gone from the paper's latest version, and build-to-rent "adds homes" is untested (rows 100, 106, 140, 143, 233).
  • Top-up T2 cut against both sides. Against Green: the Dutch ban's effect faded and showed only where cities adopted early, and the landlords it shut out were small (rows 146, 234). Against Gold: the Dutch rent rise Gold relied on is not in the latest version (row 143). The government evaluation is logged as context only: it shares an author with the paper (row 234).
