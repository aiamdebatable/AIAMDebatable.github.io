## Fairness audit
- **Neither side is written beyond its evidence.** Green's best figures are primary and on the record:
  Gorback-Qian-Zhu's faster price and rent growth, Barbieri-Dobbels' +3.2% and measured concentration
  effect on rents, Hanson's rent markups, Coven's own top-decile +6.2%, and the Dutch shift to
  first-time buyers (rows 114, 135, 137, 139, 141, 145). So are its limits: "outbid with cash" is
  unmeasured, the price studies are local and mostly before 2015, no study shows a ban lowers prices,
  and the Dutch effect showed only in early-adopting cities (rows 123, 138, 142, 172, 234). Gold's best
  figures are primary too: the 0.59–0.7% national share, the net selling, GAO's 38–64% to
  owner-occupants, the Dutch ban's flat prices, Barbieri-Dobbels' net rent fall (rows 54, 64, 69, 73,
  140, 142, 231). So are its limits: every rent backfire result is a model (Coven's forced exit,
  Barbieri-Dobbels' Atlanta model), the one measured Dutch rent rise is gone from the paper's latest
  version, and build-to-rent "adds homes" is untested (rows 100, 106, 140, 143, 233).
- **Top-up T2 cut against both sides.** Against Green: the Dutch ban's effect faded and showed only
  where cities adopted early, and the landlords it shut out were small (rows 146, 234). Against Gold:
  the Dutch rent rise Gold relied on is not in the latest version (row 143). The government evaluation
  is logged as context only: it shares an author with the paper (row 234).
- **Corrections cut both ways and every one is kept.** Against the pro-ban side: Lambie-Hanson's
  "institutional" is any company and its rent result vanished in the later version; "outbid" is
  unmeasured (rows 116, 120, 172). Against the anti-ban side: Coven's "ban raises rents" is a forced exit
  in which prices fall; the build-to-rent slump is partly the bill's own doing; the Neighbors Bank 69/31
  that said first-time buyers are "holding their ground" is not a first-time-buyer measure (rows 106, 61,
  155). Against both: the "nobody measured who buys" line and the "investor-light" metro labels (rows 64,
  179, 180).
- **The pro-ban side was under-represented in the engine's dossier**, and hand pass C added three
  primaries it lacked (Gorback-Qian-Zhu, Barbieri-Dobbels, Hanson). The brief steelmans Green from them.
- **Vendor, advocacy and trade sources are marked where they speak:** Parcl, Cotality, Redfin,
  Realtor.com and John Burns (vendor; John Burns writes against the Senate text); AEI (advocacy, written
  against it); the NHC coalition, AFR/PESP and a senator's release (advocacy); NAHB, ResiClub, MPA and
  CRE Daily (trade). Each is used only for its own figure, attributed.
- **US first, the Dutch line paired (top-ups T3–T5, after the producer's 2026-10-27 rulings).** Most viewers are in
  the US, so every beat now rests on US data: wages and affordability (rows 251–277), rents and the big landlords'

  version reports no rent result (rows 106, 143, 233).
- Who buys the homes the big landlords would have bought, after 2027-01-07? Unmeasured (row 64).
- Does build-to-rent add homes, or turn homes that would have been sold into rentals? Untested (row 100).
