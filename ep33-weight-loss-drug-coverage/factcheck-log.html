Fairness audit

  • Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly. Both designs got the same treatment: every claim re-opened at its primary, every vendor claim marked as one. The record is not symmetric and the brief says so: the risk-tiered design has an eligibility rulebook, a price, trial outcomes in the people it targets (SELECT, SURMOUNT-4) and a decade-long NHS rollout plan, but no outcome record of its own yet (the Bridge started 2026-07-01; CMS has published no uptake). The program-required design has a rulebook (OPM's Call Letter) and the trial that pairs drug with therapy (STEP 3), but no study anywhere tests the requirement itself, and the nearest record (bariatric-surgery mandates) cuts against it. The brief leans where that leaves it and says so.
  • The money cuts both ways. Gold's premium argument is primary-sourced (OPM, BGH, EBRI, CBO); green's value argument is too (ICER). Both "it pays for itself" and "it's a waste" are refused: CBO and ICER agree it costs more than it saves; ICER says it is still good value per year of health gained.
  • Vendors are not evidence of outcomes. CVS, Lilly, Novo, GoodRx and Evernorth appear only for what they announced, never for what their programs achieved.
  • No politics. The White House figures (600,000 Bridge users; $245; TrumpRx) are attributed claims; no line frames the episode for or against an administration.
  • No moralising about weight. Person-first language; the episode is about the payer's decision, not about whether any viewer should take the drug. The "is it a shortcut?" question is asked on air because viewers ask it, and it is answered with the trial arms (both arms got the same lifestyle programme), never with a character's effort or willpower. The word "effort" is not used as a filter.
  • The misuse worry is stated as narrowly as the record allows. "No documented reason" (row 16.27), never "abuse"; the study cannot show who paid (16.29); the online-seller figures are APPROXIMATE and attributed; "people gain weight to qualify" has no evidence (16.33) and is not said.
  • Other uses are labelled by status. Approved (FDA label and letter), trial evidence (not approved), or early/observational. Heart failure is never called an approved use.
  • Employers named only on their own or reported actions (Starbucks, Cigna: "reported", with the month); no motive attributed.
