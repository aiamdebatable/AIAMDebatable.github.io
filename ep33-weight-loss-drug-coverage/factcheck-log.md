# Fact-check log — Weight-Loss Drugs: Should Your Health Plan Pay, for Whom, at What Price?

_Independent verification pass, run 2026-10-06 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). Each claim gets a
verdict: **CONFIRMED**, **CORRECTED**, **UNRESOLVED** or **APPROXIMATE**. This is a REQUIRED gate, set
out under "Verification gate" in the episodes README. The show's differentiator is that this file is
real._

- **CONFIRMED** — the source says what the episode says.
- **CORRECTED** — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under
  "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
- **UNRESOLVED** — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.
- **APPROXIMATE** — checked, roughly right, and **deliberately not cited on air**. Not the same as
  UNRESOLVED: the claim was established, the *figure* just isn't precise enough to state. Use it when
  the direction is sound but a number would imply more precision than the source supports.

⛔ These four are the whole vocabulary. The site builder reads them to count the claims the public
site prints, and since package `HFIX` a verdict it does not recognise **fails the build** instead of
being skipped silently. A fifth verdict means changing both this file and the builder.

**How this was checked.** A peg hunt (2026-10-06: a blind sweep with every topic word banned, plus the
Federal Register's presidential documents, rules, proposed rules and notices for 2026-09-08 to 10-06),
then the deep-research-v3 engine (41 agents; 110 claims found, 12 verified with the primary opened, all
12 held, 98 not adjudicated, 18 gaps named by its critics), then a three-way hand pass on the primary
record, split by domain: **A** public payers (Medicare, the federal employees' plan, ACA and state
rules, other countries), **B** the clinical record and the economics, **C** employers, commercial plans
and cash-price designs. Every primary relied on was downloaded and saved with its text extracted; the
"saved" path in each row names the copy kept in the show's evidence archive (it is not part of the
published bundle). Quotes are copied from that saved text. Where only a secondary source could be
reached, the row says so. Vendor announcements (CVS, Lilly, Novo, GoodRx, Evernorth) are the vendor's
own claims and are marked as such. After Lucas reviewed the pitch, a fourth, top-up hand pass (**D3**)
checked the debate viewers actually have ("is it a shortcut?", "are people abusing it?") and the
drug's approved and trial uses beyond weight; its rows are section 16.

**Counting rule:** one row per claim. A claim the brief does not use was left out; every claim the
brief relies on, and every claim it keeps off air, is here.

**Reading the verdicts here.** APPROXIMATE rows are used, if at all, only as a direction or an
attributed report ("a survey found about a quarter…", "Reuters reported…"), never as a precise figure.
UNRESOLVED rows are in the brief's "Off air" list.

## Fairness audit

- **Equal rigour, unequal record, said plainly.** Both designs got the same treatment: every claim
  re-opened at its primary, every vendor claim marked as one. The record is not symmetric and the brief
  says so: the risk-tiered design has an eligibility rulebook, a price, trial outcomes in the people it
  targets (SELECT, SURMOUNT-4) and a decade-long NHS rollout plan, but **no outcome record of its own
  yet** (the Bridge started 2026-07-01; CMS has published no uptake). The program-required design has a
  rulebook (OPM's Call Letter) and the trial that pairs drug with therapy (STEP 3), but **no study
  anywhere tests the requirement itself**, and the nearest record (bariatric-surgery mandates) cuts
  against it. The brief leans where that leaves it and says so.
- **The money cuts both ways.** Gold's premium argument is primary-sourced (OPM, BGH, EBRI, CBO); green's
  value argument is too (ICER). Both "it pays for itself" and "it's a waste" are refused: CBO and ICER
  agree it costs more than it saves; ICER says it is still good value per year of health gained.
- **Vendors are not evidence of outcomes.** CVS, Lilly, Novo, GoodRx and Evernorth appear only for what
  they announced, never for what their programs achieved.
- **No politics.** The White House figures (600,000 Bridge users; $245; TrumpRx) are attributed claims;
  no line frames the episode for or against an administration.
- **No moralising about weight.** Person-first language; the episode is about the payer's decision,
  not about whether any viewer should take the drug. The "is it a shortcut?" question is asked on air
  because viewers ask it, and it is answered with the trial arms (both arms got the same lifestyle
  programme), never with a character's effort or willpower. The word "effort" is not used as a filter.
- **The misuse worry is stated as narrowly as the record allows.** "No documented reason" (row 16.27),
  never "abuse"; the study cannot show who paid (16.29); the online-seller figures are APPROXIMATE and
  attributed; "people gain weight to qualify" has no evidence (16.33) and is not said.
- **Other uses are labelled by status.** Approved (FDA label and letter), trial evidence (not
  approved), or early/observational. Heart failure is never called an approved use.
- **Employers named only on their own or reported actions** (Starbucks, Cigna: "reported", with the
  month); no motive attributed.

## Open questions this episode could not settle

- Which 2027 Medicare price governs Part D-covered semaglutide claims: the $274 negotiated price or the $245 deal price.
- What happens to a Bridge user who switches Part D plan at open enrollment; what look-back CMS applies in 2027.
- Whether federal employees already on a drug on 2027-01-01 must enter a therapy program first; how each carrier will run the requirement (2027 brochures not out).
- Any study testing whether a coverage condition improves outcomes or saves money.
- Any 2026 per-drug net price; Caremark's negotiated Zepbound price; how many sponsors took the template.
- For the outside-the-benefit subsidy: deductible and out-of-pocket credit, tax treatment, fees, uptake.
- Any measured medical-cost rebound after a payer drops coverage.
- KFF's 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey (not out as of 2026-10-06) — a re-peg trigger.
- Any evidence of people gaining weight to qualify for coverage (none found).
- Whether employer or commercial plans pay for off-label uses (fibromyalgia, rheumatoid arthritis, alcohol use).
- A public poll on whether employer or private plans should cover the drugs.
