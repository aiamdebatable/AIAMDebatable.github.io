FTC vs IC3 for older adults: never add them (hand pass A)

A median ($900) and an average ($38,500) measure different things and must never sit side by side as if comparable.

Script traps

Merged from the three passes, duplicates removed.

The numbers

  • Always say REPORTED losses for FTC and IC3 figures. Sentinel and IC3 are self-reported, and the FTC says they are a fraction of real losses.
  • FTC and IC3 are never added, never placed in one sentence as if they were one total, and never set median against average. They overlap: Sentinel ingests part of IC3's data.
  • The FTC's "true size" figures are modeled ranges. Say "the FTC estimates somewhere between $38 billion and $262 billion in 2025, depending on assumptions" (60+: "$13 billion to $120 billion"). Never "$262 billion was lost".
  • 2025 is slightly undercounted: the FTC took no reports Oct 1 to Nov 13, 2025 (the shutdown). The modeled ranges fill the gap with an average; the reported $15.9B does not.
  • Texts are the most reported contact method, not where most money goes: text was 7% of 2025 loss reports. Social media led by dollars ($2.1B). For people 60+, phone-call scams have the highest median loss ($3,000), and for 80+ the phone is the biggest dollar channel.
  • 31/28/11/10/7 is a share of loss reports that name a contact method (website/app, social, phone, email, text). It is not a share of dollars.
  • Payment methods: say "bank transfers and crypto carry the most money; credit cards are used most but lose the least". Use the 2024 all-ages Data Book dollars or the 2025 60+ figure, labelled by year and group. Do not use the secondary "$4B bank + crypto" figure.
  • Gift cards: the vivid Target details are 2021 and nine months. Label them "in 2021". The current figure is $212M (2024, all ages) or $139M (2025, 60+).
  • Older adults: "less likely to lose money, but they lose more when they do" (frequency vs severity). Only 46% of FTC fraud reports carry an age. 6% of older adults' loss reports carry 70% of their dollars, so an "average" misleads; use medians.
  • Crypto ATMs: FTC figures (2023, H1 2024) and IC3 figures (2025) are different datasets and years. "Three times as likely" is a reporting rate. FinCEN's notice is guidance to banks, not a law. Iowa AG fees: "Bitcoin Depot takes 23%, CoinFlip claims 21%" (the AG's wording, from a lawsuit).
  • Industry surveys (Truecaller $25.4B, GASA $1.03T, McAfee's "1 in 4"): if used at all, say "industry survey estimates" and name the vendor. Never use one as a count.

The scams and the peg

  • The Treasury peg is about investment and romance scams laundered through the Prince Group. It never mentions texts or calls. The bridge to the viewer's phone must be the IC3 line: crypto investment scams often start with a text.
  • Do not say the FCC order "makes it easier to stop texts". It does the opposite at the margin. Also do not say it blocks scam texts: it governs legitimate callers that need consent. The useful line: more real bank fraud alerts may reach you after Nov 25, scammers already copy them, so call the number on your card.
  • Never attach $7.2B (or $470M) to toll or wrong-number texts. $470M is all text-started scams in 2024; 89% of text reports had no loss.
  • "$893M AI" is any complaint mentioning AI, not voice cloning. ">$5M distress" is the AI-tagged slice.
  • "Restrained", "frozen", "seized" and "forfeiture complaint" do not mean refunded. Level Up's $562.7M is "estimated savings": money people did not send, not money returned.
  • Caller ID authentication proves which carrier signed a call, not that the caller is honest. The non-IP gap is the FCC's own word, and a final fix was not confirmed.
  • "Can you hear me" and "hello" cloning: say "there is no documented case", not "hoax".
  • The SIM-farm bust was framed as a threat to officials, not consumer scams. SMS blasters: no US case in the record.
  • UNODC's $88–114B covers East Asia, SE Asia, Australia and NZ; it is not losses to Americans. Don't set it beside the FTC's $15.9B.

The options

  • Say "the FBI recommends", never "proven", for code words and call-backs. Say what IS measured: short scam lessons and warning calls reduce people's willingness to fall for a pitch in experiments, and the effect fades within weeks or months unless repeated. That supports a "refresh it with your family" line, honestly sourced.
  • Don't sell "awareness" in general: a bank's mass warning message showed no effect (G3). The things with measured effects are specific, timely and interrupting: a warning call before the pitch, or a cancel button at the moment of payment.
  • "Your bank has to refund you" is true only if you didn't make the payment. Teach the line: if a scammer got your login or one-time code and moved the money, that's unauthorized and you're covered under Reg E. If you sent it yourself, you have no legal right; you're asking for goodwill. Report fast either way: the 2-business-day / 60-day clocks are real.
  • "70% fewer spam calls" is not "blocks 70% of scams". Don't present call filtering as protection against texts in iMessage/RCS/WhatsApp or social media.
  • The Do Not Call Registry does nothing against scammers. Say so plainly; viewers believe it does.
  • Credit freeze: free, and the right move for identity theft. It would not have stopped a single payment a scam victim sent. Don't list it as a phone-scam protection without that line.
  • RAT's 58% is of attempted theft in 3,900 hand-picked, fast-reported incidents, not of all losses (IC3's 2025 total is about $20.9B). Say "the FBI sometimes freezes money still sitting in a bank, if you report within hours". The rate has FALLEN from 71% to 58%.
  • UK reimbursement is a policy comparison, not something a US viewer can use. Frame it as "here's what happened when one country made banks pay". Credit the 21% to an evaluation's judgement, not an experiment, and mention that out-of-scope losses rose.
  • FedNow and wires are final by design: "the bank can't just take it back" is accurate for wires once accepted, unless the receiving bank agrees.
  • USPIS can intercept a mailed cash package only if it hasn't been delivered, it must be a domestic shipment, and it costs a fee. Read the number from the FTC page on air: 1-877-876-2455.

The peg, as it may be stated (hand pass B)
