# Sources — ep11 "Who Pays for the Grid the AI Boom Needs?"

Every source the brief and the fact-check log rest on, with what each one actually supports and how
far it was read. ✅ = the primary document was fetched and read in full text. ⚠ = secondary, or the
primary was not obtainable. ⛔ = a figure that circulates in this debate and is NOT used, and why.

## The peg — three regulators, read from their own orders

✅ **Virginia State Corporation Commission — Final Order, Case No. PUR-2026-00056** (doc control
260750228, filed 31 Jul 2026). <https://www.scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch/DOCS/8dt001!.PDF>
Read verbatim by the deep-research pass. Supports: the mandatory contribution in aid of construction
for "direct connect" large loads and the substations and transmission lines serving them; the
direction to *"directly assign the cost of certain transmission infrastructure to the new large-load
facilities which made that infrastructure necessary"*; the 90-day filing window; the Rider T-1 total
revenue requirement of **$1,538,974,037**, uncontested and *"deemed reasonable and prudent"* under Va.
Code § 56-585.1 A 4; the typical residential increase **$2.90 → $0.94** per month (Dominion's
projection as filed vs. the post-order figure); the express **deferral** of higher-order / shared
transmission.
⚠ The order contradicts itself on the Rider T-1 component split ($998.5m/$540.4m on p.2 vs.
$940.4m/$598.6m in the Findings, both citing Ex. 2 at 6). **Only the total is used.**
⚠ The amended 12-CP allocation came from Dominion's own rebuttal with Staff and Consumer Counsel
support (fn. 28 shows the reduction is not attributable to the reallocation alone) — the episode says
**"reduced", never "overrode"**.

✅ **Kentucky Public Service Commission — Order, Case No. 2026-00115** (21 Aug 2026, 32pp).
<https://psc.ky.gov/pscscf/2026%20Cases/2026-00115/20260821_PSC_ORDER.pdf>
Downloaded and extracted with `pypdf`. Supports every Kentucky figure spoken or shown: Big Rivers
Electric / Kenergy / Justified DataPower LLC (TeraWulf); **482 MW** campus at the former Century
Aluminum smelter, Hancock County; **482 MW minimum take-or-pay**; credit support equal to **twice**
the estimated highest monthly bill; annual capacity cost **prepaid or fully collateralised**; the
express finding that the arrangement **does not shift the project's costs or risks to other
customers**.

✅ **New York — Executive Order No. 62**, *Establishing a Temporary Moratorium on Data Centers in New
York While the State Develops [Higher Standards]* (14 Jul 2026).
<https://www.governor.ny.gov/executive-order/no-62-establishing-temporary-moratorium-data-centers-new-york-while-state-develops>
Fetched and read 2026-09-02 during the build. Supports: the **≥ 50 MW** threshold; DEC to hold
pending permit applications in abeyance until the Department of Public Service completes a Generic
Environmental Impact Statement; a Data Center Interconnection Working Group within 60 days; DPS to
consider a "New York Grid Acceleration Fund" with **upfront capital contributions** from data centers
and an insurance pool; and the verbatim policy statement quoted on the debate floor: *"the cost of
electric system upgrades required to provide electric utility service to large loads should not be
paid for by every-day New Yorkers."*
⚠ The order states the principle and directs agencies to **explore** a mechanism; it does not itself
allocate shared-upgrade costs. The script says exactly that and no more.

⚠ **New York S10642 / A11560 — Responsible Data Center Development Act** (passed both houses
4 Jun 2026; unsigned; pocket-veto deadline 31 Dec 2026). **≥ 20 MW**, one-year DEC permitting
moratorium. Bill text not read; terms from concurring law-firm summaries (Greenberg Traurig, DLA Piper,
Mintz, Harris Beach Murtha, NatLawReview) and news10.com on the executive order. The **60-legislator
letter of 24 Aug 2026** is from
<https://ithacavoice.org/2026/08/pressure-mounts-for-kathy-hochul-to-sign-data-center-moratorium-bill/>.
Re-checked 2026-09-02: no signature and no veto reported.

⚠ Secondary accounts of the Virginia order, used only to corroborate dates and the ~$1.5bn figure:
Utility Dive, *Dominion ordered to directly assign some transmission costs to data centers*
<https://www.utilitydive.com/news/dominion-energy-scc-transmission-costs-data-center/827693/>;
Utility Dive, *Virginia SCC weighs Dominion data center transmission cost allocation*
<https://www.utilitydive.com/news/virginia-scc-dominion-data-center-transmission-cost-allocation/825300/>;
Piedmont Environmental Council press release
<https://www.pecva.org/resources/press/press-release-the-virginia-state-corporation-commission-takes-important-first-step-of-requiring-large-load-data-centers-to-pay-for-transmission-facilities-constructed-solely-to-serve-them/>.
⛔ `virginiamercury.com` and `pecva.org` return HTTP 403 to automated fetching; neither is
load-bearing.

## The statutes — read from the enacted text

✅ **Oklahoma HB 2992 — "Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act of 2026"**, ENROLLED bill
(5pp; passed 5 May 2026, signed 13 May 2026, effective 1 Jul 2026 with emergency clause).
<http://www.oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20ENR/hB/HB2992%20ENR.PDF>
Extracted with `pypdf`. Supports: **≥ 75 MW** per facility or in aggregate behind a single point of
interconnection; separate tariffs with credit requirements; measures covering costs that *"may remain
unrecovered if the customer departs the system or materially reduces load"*; **minimum ten-year term**;
applies to all retail suppliers. ⛔ The standard is **cost causation / "equitable share"** — the
episode never says "full cost of service". ⚠ The Act's name is "Customer", not "Consumer" — the
House's own press releases have it wrong.

✅ **Tennessee SB 2128 / HB 1847 — Public Chapter 961** (signed 7 May 2026; operative 1 Jul 2026),
read from the Fiscal Review Committee's **fiscal note** (7 Feb 2026) and **fiscal memorandum on the
bill as amended** (20 Apr 2026, amendments 015517 and 018292).
<https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Fiscal/FM3346.pdf>
Supports: the **but-for** definition of infrastructure cost (*"would not be incurred but for"*); the
amended act's **carve-out** allowing a utility to allocate part of the cost to general system
improvements that go beyond what the data center needs, under its contribution-in-aid-of-construction
policies; IPP authorisation.
⚠ The **≥ 50 MW threshold** is secondary (the fiscal documents do not state it) — **not spoken and not
shown.** ⚠ The enacted chapter text itself was not opened; the fiscal memorandum on the amended bill
is the closest primary record read.

## The convergence — the other jurisdictions

✅ **Pennsylvania PUC — large-load tariff framework, Docket M-2025-3054271** (Tentative Order 6 Nov
2025; Final Order voted 30 Apr 2026, entered 12 May 2026 — `https://www.puc.pa.gov/pcdocs/1929842.pdf`,
129 pp., **read in full on 2026-09-03**). Disposition on CIAC for network improvements at pp. 41–42:
but-for test, costs allocated to the large-load customer *"irrespective of whether other customers
would benefit"*, CIAC for all distribution and transmission interconnection costs, LTIIP-planned
upgrades the only exception. **Petition for Reconsideration or Clarification** by the Energy
Association of Pennsylvania, 27 May 2026 — `https://www.puc.pa.gov/pcdocs/1932400.pdf`, 22 pp.
⚠ An earlier version of this entry said the Final Order was not opened and that Pennsylvania called
the test *"uncertain, difficult to quantify"*; that phrase (p. 28) concerns the majority-beneficiary
criterion for collateral, not cost allocation. Corrected under ISS-111.

⚠ **Wisconsin PSC — We Energies "Very Large Customer" rate structure, Docket 6630-TE-113** (Final
Order 21 May 2026). Rests on a press release self-described as preliminary; the Final Order is
**unread**. The episode says only "ordered further revisions" / "sent back".

⛔ **"24 states have approved at least one large-load tariff"** — NOT used. Pennsylvania's was a
tentative order when counts were taken; a tentative order is not an approved tariff.

## Prices and siting

✅ **Kay, O. (Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas), Reaser, R. (UC Davis) & Taylor, R. (Dallas Fed) —
*Processing Power: The Effect of Data Centers on Wholesale Electricity Markets*, Dallas Fed Working
Paper 2606** (58pp). <https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2026/wp2606>
Extracted in full. Supports: existing data centers have **already raised wholesale prices 2–6% on
average nationwide**, substantially more in the corridors; extended to 2028, **~50%** under
high-utilisation build-out and **~20%** under moderate. ⚠ **An hourly, unit-level least-cost dispatch
MODEL with site-specific data — labelled as a model wherever it appears.** Authors' views, not the
Federal Reserve's.

⚠ **Gargano, A. & Giacoletti, M. (2025)** — cited in WP 2606: state incentives meaningfully shift
data-center siting and investment *"without commensurate increases in local tech employment."*
Read via the Dallas Fed paper's citation, not the paper itself.

⚠ **"Essentially no correlation between data-center count and electricity prices (Pearson
r = −0.053)"** — secondary-sourced and **not spoken or shown**. The episode says only that siting
responds to what states GIVE (sourced above) and that no documented case of relocation over rate
design was found.

⚠ **PJM 2026/27 Base Residual Auction — $329.17/MW-day.** Established by the deep-research pass as
the **administrative price cap** the auction bound against, not a market-clearing price. ⚠ The PJM
auction report itself was not re-opened in the build pass; the figure appears in the episode **once,
labelled as the cap it is** — the correction, not the claim. PJM's own documents say "Large Loads",
never "data centers"; the episode does not put the words in PJM's mouth.

⛔ **~$4.3bn "data-center infrastructure" figure and the "63% of the capacity-price increase"
attribution** — NOT established; NOT used.

## The "they'll just leave" claim

⚠ Searched for directly (deep-research pass + hand pass, 2026-09-01/02): **no documented case** of a
data-center project cancelled and relocated to another state because of a moratorium or a
cost-allocation tariff. Trade trackers describe the *conditions* for relocation, never an instance.
Spoken as **"zero found"**, with the caveat that absence of a case is not proof of absence.

⚠ **"500+ moratorium instruments across ~42 states by July 2026"** — trade-press aggregation, not a
register. Directional; **not spoken or shown**.

## Cost pressure noted, not argued

⚠ **Executive Order 14421**, *Declaring a National Emergency To Secure the United States Bulk-Power
System* (signed 26 Aug 2026, FR 2026-17843). Surfaced by the blind peg sweep and rejected as the peg:
a supply-chain-security instrument, not a cost-allocation one. Restricting foreign bulk-power
equipment raises the cost of the buildout being allocated. Not spoken.
