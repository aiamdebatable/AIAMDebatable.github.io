The economics — read directly

✅ **Watzinger, M. & Schnitzer, M. — The Breakup of the Bell System and its Impact on US Innovation (5 September 2022, 74pp). <https://www.monika-schnitzer.com/uploads/4/9/4/1/49415675/watzinger_schnitzer_breakup_of_bell.pdf> Full text downloaded and extracted; abstract quoted verbatim in the brief. Supports both halves of the episode's central historical claim: total US telecom-related patenting +19% after the 1984 breakup, driven by companies unrelated to the Bell System, while patenting by Bell's successor companies fell — though the number of top inventions did not. ⚠ Working-paper version from the authors' own site; peer-review/publication status not confirmed.**

⚠ **FTC — Merger Remedies 2006–2012: A Report of the Bureaus of Competition and Economics (issued February 2017). 89 merger orders · 79 divestitures · 121 buyers. Divestitures of ongoing businesses generally succeeded; buyers of limited asset packages more often struggled. ⛔ ftc.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching — the report itself was NOT read.** Figures come from a law-journal analysis naming the report and its numbers precisely: Skadden, A Moment of Repose: The FTC's Merger Remedy Study, New York Law Journal vol. 257 no. 48 (14 Mar 2017) — PDF extracted. <https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2017/03/a_moment_of_repose_the_ftcs_merger_remedy_study.pdf> Report landing page (403 to tooling, reachable in a browser): <https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/ftcs-merger-remedies-2006-2012-report-bureaus-competition-economics/p143100_ftc_merger_remedies_2006-2012.pdf>

⚠ **Journal of Antitrust Enforcement 13(2):237 — Breaking up Big Tech: scissor-line suggestions for smart cuts. <https://academic.oup.com/antitrust/article/13/2/237/7905789> Named for the "scissor line" framing the reframe uses. Abstract not yet read** — cited for the concept only. Read before quoting.
