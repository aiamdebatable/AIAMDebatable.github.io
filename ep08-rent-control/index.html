Rent Control Debate: Does It Protect Tenants, or Shrink Housing?

Does rent control protect tenants, or shrink the housing they need?

Last changed 2026-11-07 · See every change

One study found both halves. Each side quotes one.

Settled on the record·Still argued

Why it was in the news

When this episode came out in August 2026, New York had just frozen the rent on about a million apartments, at zero on both one-year and two-year leases, starting in October. A freeze on two-year leases was a first.

Settled

What both sides accept

Both teams accepted the same study: in San Francisco, about 20% fewer forced moves and 15% fewer rental units. Gold conceded that hard caps do shrink a rental stock. Green conceded his case is weakest against modern caps.

Still argued

The fight

Green arguesThe stock shrinks

In San Francisco, rent control left 15% fewer renters in the covered buildings. Berlin's cap halved listings, New York's building-improvement filings fell about 75%, and over eight decades Britain's rented sector fell from 90% of housing to under 10%. Four countries point one way, and the renter who needed the next apartment never learns it wasn't built.

Gold arguesThe tenant stays

Covered tenants in San Francisco were about 20% more likely to stay put. Almost every damage number describes a hard cap nobody is proposing, and San Francisco's law never touched new building. Forty years of moderate caps in New Jersey showed no significant effect on new construction, and repeal in Cambridge made owners $1.8 billion richer and nobody's rent cheaper.

What the shouting gets wrong

"Rent control" names two different policies: hard freezes like Berlin's, and modern caps that let the rent reset when a tenant moves out and exempt new buildings. The record leans against hard caps, while forty years of moderate caps showed no significant effect on new building. So the question is which law is on your ballot, and which half of the San Francisco finding you treat as the headline.

The verdict

The two cases, as the judge put them
The case that caps cost housing rests on four mechanisms that reproduce across four countries, and on a lender who says no before a rent exists.
The case that they protect people rests on forty years of moderate ordinances that show no construction effect, and on the fact that repeal made owners one point eight billion dollars richer and nobody's rent cheaper.

What our fact-check caught

12 of the 22 claims we checked came back wrong in our drafts. Every one is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidAfter Saint Paul capped rents, building permits fell 84% in six months while Minneapolis's tripled.

    The record saysThey fell 68% over three months, and Minneapolis was up, not tripled. Saint Paul then permitted about 1,200 homes in each of the next two years.

  2. Draft saidSan Francisco's law kept rents capped between tenants and covered new buildings.

    The record saysRents reset to market when a tenant moved out, and newer buildings were exempt.

  3. Draft saidBritain's rented sector bounced back after it deregulated rents in 1989.

    The record saysThe report we used dates the recovery to after 2000. Through the 1990s the sector stayed near its low point.

  4. Draft saidAbout 40,000 rent-stabilized apartments in New York are being held empty on purpose.

    The record saysThat is a 2021 count of stabilized units empty for any reason, including repairs and legal cases. It fell to about 26,000 by 2023, and the state says the count doesn't show why units are empty.

  5. Draft saidSan Francisco's rent control raised rents across the whole city by 5.1%.

    The record saysThat number is only in an early working paper. The published study says only that the law likely drove up market rents in the long run.
