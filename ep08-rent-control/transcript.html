The reframe

LucasI went looking for a fight about whether a policy works, and I came back with a fight about what the word means.

LucasHere's what I mean. The study that gets read out as the case against rent control describes a law with vacancy decontrol and a new-construction exemption. It never restricted new building.

LucasSo when somebody says the evidence is settled, ask which law. Berlin froze rents outright and lost half its listings in weeks. New Jersey ran moderate caps for forty years across more than a hundred and fifty towns, and the construction effect doesn't show up.

LucasThose aren't contradictory results. They're two different policies wearing the same name.

LucasAnd the thing neither side can wriggle out of is that one paper found both halves. Twenty percent fewer forced moves. Fifteen percent fewer rental units. Neither number is disputed by anyone.

LucasGreen argues the stock shrinks. Gold argues the tenant stays. Green goes first.

The verdict

KateThis one doesn't resolve, and I want to be exact about where it stops rather than call it a draw and move on.

KateBoth teams accepted the same table. Twenty percent fewer forced moves. Fifteen percent fewer rental units. Neither figure is contested by anyone, in either direction.

KateSo the disagreement isn't about what happened. It's about which half of one sentence you think is the headline — and that is a values question wearing an evidence question's clothes.

KateWhere the evidence genuinely divides is by generation of policy, and both sides conceded it. Green conceded his case is weakest against modern stabilization laws. Gold conceded that hard caps do shrink a stock.

KateThe case that caps cost housing rests on four mechanisms that reproduce across four countries, and on a lender who says no before a rent exists.

KateThe case that they protect people rests on forty years of moderate ordinances that show no construction effect, and on the fact that repeal made owners one point eight billion dollars richer and nobody's rent cheaper.

KateAnd the most repeated number in this argument turned out to be false as usually told. Saint Paul's collapse was sixty-eight percent over three months, and the rebound years get dropped.

KateThat is the same trick as last episode, pointed the other way. So the question we're handing you isn't whether rent control works. It's which one is on your ballot. That one is yours.
