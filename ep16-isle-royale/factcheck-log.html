1. "Since 28 August 2026, tents, group camping and hammocks are banned at Three Mile, Daisy Farm, Moskey Basin and Caribou Island through the season's end on 31 October — 13 tent sites, 8 group sites, the surrounding off-trail zones; 32 shelters stay open."

CONFIRMED. NPS Isle Royale release, 28 Aug 2026: tent and group camping and hammock use prohibited at the four campgrounds "beginning August 28 through October 31"; "13 tent sites, 8 group campsites"; off-trail zones closed; "32 shelters remain available." Reason, verbatim: "pawing and digging at tents and entering tents to drag packs and sleeping pads away."

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/isle-royale-closes-tent-and-group-campsites-at-four-campgrounds.htm

2. "Early July a wolf got inside a shelter, tore the screen and chewed a cooler; the park closed that campground twice and called the first round ineffective. From 9 July a wolf entered a tent at Three Mile and dragged packs; rangers hazed it with air horns and paintball guns."

CONFIRMED. Lansing State Journal via Yahoo, 14 Jul 2026: Duncan Narrows closed 6–8 July after a wolf accessed a shelter interior, damaged screens and chewed a cooler; closed again through 31 July; Three Mile tent/group sites closed from 9 July after a wolf pawed at and entered a tent and dragged backpacks; staff "employing paintball guns, air horns, and motion-sensing noise devices." The dossier's finder read the NPS Duncan Narrows and Three Mile releases directly (12 Jul wording: first closure "ineffective"). Spoken as "one campground" and "the first campground out of the harbour" — Duncan Narrows and Three Mile are not named on air.

  • https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/habituated-wolves-force-isle-royale-155418148.html
  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/isle-royale-closes-duncan-narrows-campground-until-end-of-july.htm
  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/isle-royale-closes-tent-and-group-campsites-at-three-mile-campground.htm

3. "In mid-August 2025 the park lethally removed one food-conditioned wolf after aversive conditioning, waste-management changes and food-storage rules failed; at least two wolves had shown the behaviour in June–July; it said a second remained."

CONFIRMED. NPS release, 15 Aug 2025: "at least two wolves" frequented campgrounds and developed areas in June and July, dragging away food storage bags and backpacks; deterrents tried were "aversive conditioning, changes in waste management practices, and implementation of food storage regulations"; the removal "became necessary due to a growing public safety risk" (Supt. Denice Swanke); consultation with "state, federal and tribal agencies." Second wolf: the release and Michigan Tech's 2026 report both say one of two was killed. Its later fate: not found — not spoken.

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/lethal-removal-of-food-conditioned-wolf-at-isle-royale-national-park.htm

4. "Summer 2024: wolves started taking food and garbage around the harbour; the park issued its first food-storage guidance. Spring 2025: hard-sided animal-resistant containers became mandatory."

CONFIRMED. NPS release 13 Jul 2024: guidelines "in response to wolves accessing human food and garbage in and around Rock Harbor and campgrounds located on the east end of the island." NPS release 15 Apr 2025: from 16 Apr 2025 overnight visitors must use IGBC-approved animal-resistant containers. The bench's earlier "April 2026" was wrong; corrected here.

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/isle-royale-national-park-implements-new-food-storage-guidelines-to-reduce-human-wildlife-interactions.htm
  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/news/isle-royale-national-park-implements-new-food-storage-regulations.htm

5. "37 wolves in three packs, the most since the late 1970s; about 500 moose (524), down 75% since 2019; predation rate 24% against a ~10% long-run average; no calves for the first time in 68 years."

CONFIRMED. Michigan Tech release 27 Apr 2026 and the Annual Report 2025–26 (read): "37 wolves, including three packs"; 524 moose, "a 75 percent decline in moose abundance since 2019"; predation "24 percent this year … well above the long-term average of 10 percent" (report text: 9.9%); "for the first time in 68 years, we observed no calves on survey plots." Spoken rounded: "about five hundred", "three quarters", "nearly seventy years"; 24% and 37 spoken exactly.

  • https://www.mtu.edu/news/2026/04/isle-royale-wolf-population-nears-historic-high-as-moose-numbers-plummet.html
  • https://www.isleroyalewolf.org/s/WolfMooseReport_2026_FINAL_508-compression-2.pdf

6. "By 2017 the island's own wolves had inbred down to two, a father and his daughter."

CONFIRMED. Journal of Heredity 108(2), 2017, "Genomic variation of inbreeding and ancestry in the remaining two Isle Royale wolves": M183 and F193; M183 is F193's father and both share the mother F160. Bone deformities are documented in the same literature. Not spoken: the IDs.

  • https://academic.oup.com/jhered/article/108/2/120/2667696

7. "In 2018 the Park Service decided to put wolves back: 19 released over a year from Minnesota, Ontario and Michigan's Upper Peninsula."

CONFIRMED. Wolves annual report 2018–2020, Table 1 (read): 19 wolves, Sep 2018–Sep 2019 — Grand Portage Reservation MN (2018), Jostle Lake ON and Michipicoten Island ON (Feb–Mar 2019), Upper Peninsula MI (Sep 2019). Five-year assessment abstract (Conservation Science & Practice 2025) says the same. Wisconsin was a cooperating state, not a source. Michipicoten is never spoken.

  • https://npshistory.com/publications/isro/wolf-ann-rpt/2018-2020.pdf
  • https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/csp2.70363

8. "The 2018 decision called this a one-time act: no more wolves after five years; it rejected a version that would have kept restocking for twenty."

CONFIRMED. Record of Decision, 7 Jun 2018 (read): Alternative B selected — supplement "as needed up to the third year," to year five only for an unforeseen event, and "no additional wolves will be brought to the island after 5 years from date of initial introduction." Alternative C ("maintain a population of wolves on the island for at least the next 20 years") not selected. Verified by the research run (claim [0], [1]) and by hand.

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/upload/ISRO_Web_Accessible_2018_06_07_Wolf_Record_of_Decision_FINAL_Signed.pdf

9. "The federal wildlife agency signed off with: potential human/wolf conflict on Isle Royale is very low and/or non-existent given the wilderness character of the island."

CONFIRMED. ROD 2018, USFWS Section 7 concurrence, verbatim (read from the PDF): "potential human/wolf conflict on Isle Royale is very low and/or non-existent given the wilderness character of the island."

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/upload/ISRO_Web_Accessible_2018_06_07_Wolf_Record_of_Decision_FINAL_Signed.pdf

10. "Before the Park Service may remove a native animal, its own policy says it must first try to fix the conflict by changing what the visitors do; only if that can't work may it relocate or destroy the animal."

CONFIRMED. NPS Management Policies 2006 §4.4.2.1: the Service "will determine whether or not it can eliminate or mitigate the conflicts by modifying or curtailing the conflicting visitor use or other human activities"; where that is not possible, populations may be reduced by relocation, hunting, habitat management, predator restoration, reproductive intervention or destruction.

  • https://www.nps.gov/subjects/policy/mp-4-natural.htm

11. "Feeding or disturbing wildlife is a federal offence in every park; on Isle Royale the ceiling is $5,000 and six months. A ranger said visitors who ignore the storage rules can be cited. No citation, fine or prosecution was found here."

CONFIRMED (the ceiling and the quote) / UNRESOLVED (enforcement). 36 CFR §2.2(a)(2) bars "feeding, touching, teasing, frightening or intentional disturbing of wildlife." Superintendent's Compendium, updated 25 Jun 2026: violations up to $5,000 (individuals) and six months (18 U.S.C. §3571, §3559). Ranger Liz Valencia to Bridge Michigan, Aug 2025: "a citation and possibly fine." Seven search passes found no citation at Isle Royale; the script says "found none … a search, not a records check" and never "none issued."

  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/2.2
  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/superintendents-compendium.htm
  • https://bridgemi.com/outdoors-life/isle-royale-kills-wolf-reliant-on-human-food-sources/

12. "Every park statement blames food left out and packs left unattended, not feeding. No park record of a visitor feeding a wolf was found. What exists is testimony in the island's own online groups."

CONFIRMED (the record) / TESTIMONY (the groups). All four NPS releases (2024, 2025, 2026 ×2) attribute the behaviour to unsecured food and gear. The research found no locatable post and no park record of deliberate feeding. Lucas's ruling 2026-09-13: what he has seen is first-person testimony in the Isle Royale Facebook group — spoken as "campers … describe," never as a documented case.

  • the four NPS releases above

13. "Isle Royale is 45 miles long, in Lake Superior, closer to Canada than to Michigan; no roads; ferry or seaplane; under 30,000 visits a year (29,091 in 2025), the least visited national park in the lower 48."

CONFIRMED. Britannica / NPS: 45 mi × 9 mi; 56 mi from the Upper Peninsula, 15 mi from the Canadian shore; largest island in Lake Superior. NPS 2025 visitation statistics: 29,091 recreation visits, least visited in the contiguous US. Access by ferry (Houghton, Copper Harbor, Grand Portage) or seaplane; no roads. Distances are secondary-sourced (Britannica); the visit count is primary.

  • https://www.britannica.com/place/Isle-Royale
  • https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1207/03-13-26-2025-visitation-statsitics.htm

14. "It closes completely from November to April, the only national park that shuts for winter; almost the whole island is designated wilderness (1976), 'a visitor who does not remain'."

CONFIRMED. NPS hours page: closed 1 Nov–15 Apr, the only national park to close entirely; NPS: 132,018 acres designated wilderness 20 Oct 1976 (~99% of the land); Wilderness Act §2(c): "where man himself is a visitor who does not remain." International Biosphere Reserve 1980 (NPS).

  • https://home.nps.gov/isro/planyourvisit/hours.htm
  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/nature/index.htm

15. "A closed system: no bears, no deer, no hunting, no roads. Moose swam out early last century; wolves crossed the ice from Canada in the late 1940s. Counted every winter since 1958 — the longest predator–prey study in the world."

CONFIRMED. Wolf–Moose Project "The Study": research began 1958 under Durward Allen; wolves arrived over an ice bridge in the late 1940s; moose colonised in the early 1900s; wolves have ranged 12–50, moose 385–2,422. FEIS 2018: no deer or bears on the island; hunting barred. "Late nineteen forties" is spoken; the 1948–49 winter date is secondary (NBC/NPS) and not spoken.

  • https://www.isleroyalewolf.org/the-study
  • https://npshistory.com/publications/isro/wolves-feis-2018.pdf

16. "The 1931 act says 'a public park for the benefit and enjoyment of the people' — nothing about science or wolves."

CONFIRMED. 16 U.S.C. §408: "established, dedicated, and set apart as a public park for the benefit and enjoyment of the people." No wildlife or scientific purpose in the section. (The dossier notes a separate provision barring hunting; the FEIS says public hunting is "not allowed by federal statutory law at the park.")

  • https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/408

17. "The park's foundation document calls the wolf–moose relationship the essence of the island's wilderness character."

CONFIRMED (finder-read). NPS Isle Royale Foundation Document (2016), quoted by the research run's finder as framing wolf–moose dynamics as "the essence of Isle Royale's wilderness character." Not re-opened by hand; the quote is short and the phrasing on air matches it.

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/upload/ISRO_Web_NotAccessible_Foundation_Document_2016.pdf

18. "The 2018 decision says nothing about removing a wolf. Public hunting is barred by law. A moose cull was considered and dismissed. Managing the moose at all would need a new plan."

CONFIRMED. FEIS 2018 (read): "Managed Culling / Public Hunting … dismissed from further consideration"; "public hunting is not allowed by federal statutory law at the park"; direct moose management (translocation, culling, fertility control, biocontrol) "outside the scope of this plan … may require a separate planning effort." The ROD's only removal-related language concerns not accepting habituated wolves as founders; it never addresses removing an island wolf.

  • https://npshistory.com/publications/isro/wolves-feis-2018.pdf

19. "The Wilderness Stewardship Plan's final study (2024) has never been signed. Its preferred version allows larger groups and lets most campsites be booked in advance; the version that cuts campsites, adds reservations and removes shelters is the one the agency did not prefer."

CONFIRMED. WSP Final EIS, Aug 2024 (read, 144 pp): Alternative B (preferred) "increasing the group size limit for camping to 12"; advance permits; "booked up to 85% of capacity." Alternative C: "decreasing day use group size, reducing the number of campsites … implementing a camping reservation system … remove shelters" (20 shelters). Record of Decision: none found on parkplanning.nps.gov as of 2026-09-13; the NPS release says a ROD follows ≥30 days after the FR notice.

  • https://parkplanning.nps.gov/document.cfm?documentID=138446&parkID=140&projectID=109183

20. "The closures came from the superintendent's ordinary closure power, a standing rule any park has."

CONFIRMED. Superintendent's Compendium (36 CFR §1.5(a)(1)), updated 25 Jun 2026; the campground closures are issued under that authority, not under any plan.

  • https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/superintendents-compendium.htm

21. "In June the Interior Department opened a review of the Park Service's whole wilderness policy; comments closed in August; no outcome yet."

CONFIRMED. Federal Register 91 FR 35998, 15 Jun 2026 (2026-11958), NPS Level 2 review of Director's Order #41; comments due 14 Aug 2026; the notice itself says the order creates no enforceable right. No outcome published as of 2026-09-13.

  • https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/15/2026-11958/national-park-service-level-2-wilderness-stewardship-policy-review-directors-order-41

22. "The wolves were federally endangered in Michigan the day one was shot; whether the park held a permit to kill one could not be found."

CONFIRMED (status) / UNRESOLVED (permit). The park's own 2024 WSP FEIS: gray wolves "federally endangered in the remaining contiguous 48 states" after the 10 Feb 2022 vacatur; USFWS pages confirm the 44-state entity remained listed through 2025. No take authorisation for the 15 Aug 2025 removal was located. Spoken as an open question.

  • https://parkplanning.nps.gov/document.cfm?documentID=138446&parkID=140&projectID=109183

23. "Yellowstone, twice: hazed with cracker shells, bean bags and rubber bullets, then killed. Around seventy hazing episodes in five years and two animals killed."

CONFIRMED (secondary) / APPROXIMATE (72). National Parks Traveler 2009 (Gibbon Meadow wolf, hazed then killed) and Oct 2011 (a second wolf euthanised after repeated approaches). 24. "Banff: two wolves shot in one summer (2016) after aversive conditioning failed. In September 2025 the park charged campers whose garbage a wolf reached; a court fined them a few hundred dollars ($350); the wolf was watched, not shot."

CONFIRMED (secondary). CBC, 2016: Parks Canada killed two habituated Bow Valley wolves, one unresponsive to aversive conditioning. TheTravel / Western Wheel, Sep 2025: campers charged under the Canada National Parks Act after a wolf accessed unattended garbage at Two Jack Lakeside on 3 Sep 2025; $350 fine; wolf warning in effect, animal monitored. Spoken as "a few hundred dollars."

25. "A provincial park in Ontario: every wolf that bit a person was shot afterward."

CONFIRMED (secondary/advocacy). International Wolf Center summary of Algonquin's record: ~12 habituated wolves since the 1970s, 4–6 biting incidents 1963–98, the wolves involved shot; McNay 2002 (ADF&G Technical Bulletin 13) carries the case histories. Algonquin is not named on air.

  • https://wolf.org/wow/wolf-human-interactions1/

26. "Yosemite: over a thousand bear incidents a year in the late 1990s; with canisters, lockers, education and enforcement, incidents were down more than 90% by 2015."

CONFIRMED. NPS Yosemite release: ~1,600 incidents and ~$660,000 damage in 1998; 76 incidents and $4,909 in 2015; NPS credits the combination, not canisters alone. Research-run verification [10], [11] opened the release. Spoken as "over a thousand" and "more than ninety percent."

  • https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/news/human-bear-incidents-reach-record-low-in-yosemite-national-park.htm

27. "The island on the Canadian side: wolves crossed the ice in 2014 and ate its caribou from several hundred to almost none; Ontario airlifted the last 15 out, then let the Park Service take the starving wolves; by 2022 it had no wolves and no caribou."

CONFIRMED (secondary). CBC / Global News 2018–19: wolves reached Michipicoten Island over ice in 2014; caribou fell from several hundred (sources range 680–900+) to under 10 by 2018; 15 airlifted to the Slate Islands and Caribou Island; the remaining wolves trapped for Isle Royale in March 2019; a 2022 survey found no wolves. Spoken as "several hundred"; the island is not named on air.

  • https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/sudbury/wolves-re-locate-isle-royale-michipicoten-parks-caribou-1.5072935
  • https://globalnews.ca/news/5094395/wolves-michipicoten-island/

28. "The Grand Portage Band's biologist: 'they eat all the food, they become overpopulated, and then they either kill each other or they die of starvation'; the Band did not want the wolves brought in."

CONFIRMED (secondary — quote via search excerpt of a paywalled article). Detroit News, 27 Apr 2026, Seth Moore (Grand Portage Band director of natural resources), quoted as above; the Band's own survey: beaver −70%, moose −50%. The Band's 2017 Draft EIS comment opposed the translocation (tribal members harvest moose) and it then cooperated. ⚠ Verify the quote against the article before publish (paywall blocked the fetch); the wording on air is the excerpt's.

  • https://www.detroitnews.com/story/news/local/michigan/2026/04/27/isle-royale-wolf-wolves-moose-numbers-michigan-tech-survey-2026/89817946007/

29. "The wolves crashed to fourteen in the 1980s; the moose crashed to about five hundred in the 1990s and doubled back by themselves."

CONFIRMED. Wolf–Moose Project: wolves 50 (1980) → 14 (1982), canine parvovirus; moose ~2,400 (1996) → ~500 (1997) after a severe winter and starvation, back to ~900 by 2001 unaided. "Doubled back" is spoken for ~500 → ~900.

  • https://www.isleroyalewolf.org/the-study

30. "The first time anyone put wolves on Isle Royale was 1952, four wolves from the Detroit Zoo; the experiment failed primarily because the wolves became habituated to humans; two or three were removed."

CONFIRMED. FEIS 2018 (read), citing Mech 1966: "the introduction of four wolves from the Detroit Zoo in 1952 … The experiment was not successful, primarily due to human habituation by the wolves … two or three were subsequently removed, and one or two remained in the wild."

  • https://npshistory.com/publications/isro/wolves-feis-2018.pdf

31. "Mean annual survival 0.74; 27 wolves by April 2023; more than 80% modelled chance of persisting 20 years despite high inbreeding." (on screen only)

CONFIRMED (abstract). Five-year assessment, Conservation Science & Practice 2025, abstract: annual survival 0.74 (95% CI 0.58–0.94), 27 wolves April 2023, >80% persistence over 20 years, intraspecific aggression the leading mortality cause. Paywalled body not read.

  • https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/csp2.70363

Hand pass — primary documents opened 2026-09-13 (before the dossier landed)

The 1952 precedent. FEIS 2018 p.5 (citing Mech 1966): four Detroit Zoo wolves were released on the island in 1952; "the experiment was not successful, primarily due to human habituation by the wolves"; two or three were removed, one or two stayed. The island's first wolf intervention failed for the reason the 2026 closures exist.

The 2018 prediction that failed. ROD 7 Jun 2018, USFWS concurrence: "potential human/wolf conflict on Isle Royale is very low and/or non-existent given the wilderness character of the island." FEIS Alternatives Considered but Dismissed: "The National Park Service will not accept wolves that display human habituation or other problem behaviors" (NPS 2006 §4.4.2.2) — the source wolves were screened for it; the island then produced it.

What the plan said the park WOULD do about people. FEIS Alt A/B common elements (p.17–18): "Periodic, temporary closures to visitors in some areas of the park would continue to avoid wolf-human interactions, if necessary. The park would continue to intervene in human-to-wolf interactions to ensure visitor safety and resource protection." Closures were the designed response.

What the plan ruled OUT. Public hunting "not allowed by federal statutory law at the park"; managed culling dismissed (logistics, staffing, carcasses, wilderness character). Direct management of moose (translocation, culling, fertility control, biocontrol) "outside the scope of this plan … may require a separate planning effort"; no approved moose contraceptive; biocontrol "against NPS management policy." Supplementation of wolves capped: as needed to year 3, emergency only to year 5, "no additional wolves … after 5 years from date of initial introduction" (the window closed Sep 2023).

The source wolves. Annual report 2018–2020, Table 1: 19 wolves — Grand Portage Reservation MN (2018), Jostle Lake ON and Michipicoten Island ON (Feb–Mar 2019), Upper Peninsula MI (Sep 2019). "Eight wolves died with the most common cause of mortality intraspecific aggression." Lucas's recall (Canada / Minnesota / Michigan) is right; Wisconsin was a cooperating state, not a source.

The 2026 report on the campground wolves (p.8–9): "Over summer 2025, there continued to be issues with at least two wolves spending a substantial amount of time near campgrounds and developed areas attempting to access food brought by park visitors and staff … one of the two wolves being killed by the National Park Service in August." Note "and staff".

The legal instrument for the closures and the fines. Superintendent's Compendium, updated 25 Jun 2026: food storage under 36 CFR §2.10(d) (lockers or approved hard-sided containers; hanging in campgrounds prohibited; backcountry 200 ft / 12 ft up 6 ft out); wildlife feeding prohibited under 36 CFR §2.2; closures under 36 CFR §1.5(a)(1); violations up to $5,000 / six months (18 U.S.C. §3571, §3559). Ranger Liz Valencia to Bridge Michigan, Aug 2025: visitors face "a citation and possibly fine for not following the guidance." ⚠ No record found yet of a citation actually issued at Isle Royale for feeding or food storage — UNRESOLVED, ask the dossier.

The Band's position. Grand Portage did not support the translocation (tribal members harvest moose) but cooperated to build the relationship; runs its own survey since 2019 — beaver density −70%, moose −50%; Seth Moore: the wolves are "running out of food" (Detroit News 27 Apr 2026, secondary — paywalled, quote via search summary; verify).

Comparators found. Banff: Parks Canada euthanised habituated wolves in 2016 (two) and 2020; in Sep 2025 it CHARGED campers under the Canada National Parks Act for leaving garbage that a wolf accessed (fines from $4,000, max $225,000) — the punish-the-human precedent. Yellowstone 11 Jul 2025: first food-conditioned black bear killed in five years after it defeated a storage pole; no visitor citation mentioned. Algonquin: ~12 habituated wolves since the 1970s, 4 biting incidents, a boy bitten in his tent 1996.

Timeline corrections carried into the peg: first food-storage guidance 13 Jul 2024; mandatory containers 16 Apr 2025; lethal removal 15 Aug 2025; Duncan Narrows closed 6–8 Jul 2026; Three Mile from 9 Jul 2026; four-campground tent ban 28 Aug–31 Oct 2026.

Five-year assessment (Conservation Science & Practice, 2025, doi 10.1111/csp2.70363 — abstract via search, PDF paywalled): 19 released Sep 2018–Sep 2019; mean annual survival 0.74 (95% CI 0.58–0.94); 27 wolves April 2023; reproduction every year incl. the first; 5 of 11 confirmed deaths were other wolves; PVA >80% persistence over 20 years "despite high levels of inbreeding"; one family group's dominance "may complicate the long-term genetic health".

Moore, the axis in one quote (Detroit News 27 Apr 2026, via search excerpt — verify against the article): rising wolves + falling beaver/moose "absolutely" cue more conflict, because this is "a population of wolves that are not hunted or trapped", with "no fear of humans whatsoever, and natural prey abundance is becoming less and less." He expects the wolves to "start to drop off" and moose to recover — the hands-off forecast, from the partner who did not want the translocation.

Founding purpose. Authorised 3 Mar 1931 "to conserve a prime example of North Woods Wilderness"; established 3 Apr 1940; 132,018 acres (~99% of the land) designated wilderness 20 Oct 1976 (secondary — NPS history pages; pull the statute text for the script).

Wilderness Stewardship Plan FEIS (Aug 2024), opened by hand (144 pp, downloaded from parkplanning.nps.gov and read with pypdf): Alternative B, the PREFERRED alternative, "would provide more wilderness opportunities for groups by increasing the group size limit for camping to 12"; advance backcountry/wilderness permits; "wilderness campgrounds would be booked up to 85% of capacity." Alternative C "would emphasize solitude by decreasing day use group size, reducing the number of campsites within campgrounds, eliminating some trails, implementing a camping reservation system … eliminating commercial use … remove shelters" — 20 shelters removed from wilderness under C. "No actions being considered under this plan/EIS would directly impact wolves at the park." The Backpacker account the dossier relied on is CONFIRMED. ROD to be signed by the Regional Director ≥30 days after FR publication; none found on parkplanning.nps.gov as of 2026-09-13.

ESA status on the day of the kill. The same FEIS (2024): gray wolves "listed as federally … endangered in the remaining contiguous 48 states" after the Feb 2022 vacatur; USFWS pages confirm the 44-state entity remained listed through 2025 (a Montana district court on 5 Aug 2025 vacated in part the 2024 "not warranted" finding). So the wolf removed on 15 Aug 2025 was an ESA-endangered animal on federal land. Whether NPS held a take authorisation is UNRESOLVED.

NPS Management Policies 2006 §4.4.2.1 (read via WebFetch): before reducing a native population the Service must first determine "whether or not it can eliminate or mitigate the conflicts by modifying or curtailing the conflicting visitor use or other human activities"; only where that is impossible may it relocate, hunt, manage habitat, restore predators, intervene in reproduction, or destroy. This is the ladder the park climbed.
