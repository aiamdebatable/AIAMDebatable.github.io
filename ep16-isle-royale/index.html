Isle Royale Wolves and Moose: Who Is the Island For?

The Park Service put 19 wolves back on Isle Royale in 2018-19; the moose are down 75%, one food-conditioned wolf has been shot, and tent camping is banned at four campgrounds through October. Who is the island for — and when the wilderness stops working, who intervenes, on which animal?

Settled on the record·Still argued

Why it was in the news

When this episode came out in September 2026, Isle Royale had just banned tents at four campgrounds for the rest of the season, because wolves kept entering tents to drag packs away. The Park Service had put wolves back on the island itself in 2018, and that winter's count found 37 wolves, about 500 moose and no calves.

Settled

What both sides accept

Both teams accepted the ladder, humans first and the animal last, and agreed the park followed it. Neither wants to touch the wolf and moose numbers, and both admitted neither has a cure: no park has hazed or fined its way out of a wolf that learned tents mean food. And both called the island's order backwards: one wolf shot, no camper cited.

Still argued

The fight

Green arguesManage the wilderness

The park restocked this island with 19 wolves, so it owns what they do. It climbed the ladder in the right order, from food guidance to containers to hazing to shooting one wolf, and the next summer was worse. Every wolf anywhere that learned tents mean food was eventually shot, so act on the first one early.

Gold arguesManage the visitor

The humans were never really managed: guidance with no penalty, containers a year later, and no visitor ever cited, though the fine can reach $5,000. Yosemite cut bear incidents by more than 90% with all four tools, enforcement included. Enforce the rules, close the campgrounds the wolves are working, and leave the wolves and moose to the study.

What the shouting gets wrong

The real dial is when a wilderness that people restocked may touch the animal: the first summer, or only after the visitor rules are actually enforced. Under that sits what the island is for: the 1931 act says the people, and the wilderness law says a visitor who does not remain. The design that survived both teams: cite the person who left the food, close what the wolves are working, leave the numbers alone, and sign or withdraw the visitor plan that has sat unsigned.

The verdict

What survives both teams
Enforce the visitor rules before the next removal, and cite the person whose pack the wolf took. Draw the line where the park drew it: a wolf that takes from an occupied site is past hazing. Close what the wolves are working. Leave the numbers to the study. And sign or withdraw the visitor plan, so the closures stop being a rule nobody wrote.
What didn't hold up
Green's predictor: the people who built this experiment said the conflict would be very low or non-existent, and he said so himself. Gold's cure: no park anywhere has fined or closed its way out of a habituated wolf, and she said so herself.

When on the ladder a wilderness touches the animal

  1. 1The first summer
    What it buys
    Acts on the animal early. The park put 19 wolves on the island itself in 2018, owns this experiment, and has already acted on an animal once at the top of the ladder. A ladder climbed in the right order that ends in the same place is not an argument for climbing it slower, and no park in the record has hazed a habituated wolf back. Green draws the line the park drew in 2025, a wolf that takes a pack from an occupied site, in the first summer, not the second.
    What it costs
    What fell from Green: the people who built this experiment said the conflict would be very low or non-existent, and its own debater said so himself. And the park already shot one wolf, in mid-August 2025, and the next summer was worse.
    Not measured
    Whether the wolves in the tents are hungry: nothing in the park's own record connects this winter's count to the tents, and the one person who connects them, the tribe's biologist, says it as a prediction.

What the island is for

  1. 1The act that says the people
    What it buys
    The 1931 act that created the park says a public park for the benefit and enjoyment of the people. That's the whole purpose clause, nothing about science, nothing about wolves. Green's reading of the wilderness law: it says a visitor who does not remain, not a visitor who is not allowed. And when the agency wrote down what it preferred in its visitor plan, it chose larger groups and more advance booking, not caps.
    What it costs
    It sets aside the other law on the same ground: almost the whole island has been wilderness since 1976, and the park's own foundation document calls the wolf-moose relationship the essence of the island's wilderness character.

    On the record: row 16 of the fact-check log, row 19 of the fact-check log, row 14 of the fact-check log, row 17 of the fact-check log

  2. 2The act that says a visitor who does not remain
    What it buys
    Almost the whole island has been wilderness by law since 1976: where the earth and its community of life are untrammeled by man, where man himself is a visitor who does not remain. The park's own foundation document calls the wolf-moose relationship the essence of the island's wilderness character. Gold's reading: a closed tent site is exactly what that act means.
    What it costs
    It closes ground to the people the 1931 act names: since August 28, no tents at four campgrounds through the end of the season. And the version of the visitor plan that cuts campsites, adds reservations and removes shelters is the one the agency did not prefer.

    On the record: row 14 of the fact-check log, row 17 of the fact-check log, row 1 of the fact-check log, row 19 of the fact-check log

What our fact-check caught

Our drafts got these wrong before they aired. Every correction is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidThe park shot the food-conditioned wolf in 2026.

    The record saysIt was shot in mid-August 2025, the summer before.

  2. Draft saidAnimal-resistant food containers became mandatory in April 2026.

    The record saysThey became mandatory in spring 2025, after the first food-storage guidance in summer 2024.

  3. Draft saidVisitors are feeding the wolves.

    The record saysNo park record shows a visitor feeding a wolf. The park blames food and packs left out. What exists is campers' own accounts, which are testimony, not a record.

  4. Draft saidThe park's long-delayed plan would restrict visitors.

    The record saysThe version the park preferred allows larger groups and advance booking. The version that cuts campsites and adds reservations is the one it did not prefer. Neither has been signed.
