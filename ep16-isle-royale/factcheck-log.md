## Hand pass — primary documents opened 2026-09-13 (before the dossier landed)

**The 1952 precedent.** FEIS 2018 p.5 (citing Mech 1966): four Detroit Zoo wolves were released on the
island in 1952; "the experiment was not successful, primarily due to human habituation by the wolves";
two or three were removed, one or two stayed. The island's first wolf intervention failed for the
reason the 2026 closures exist.

**The 2018 prediction that failed.** ROD 7 Jun 2018, USFWS concurrence: "potential human/wolf conflict
on Isle Royale is very low and/or non-existent given the wilderness character of the island." FEIS
Alternatives Considered but Dismissed: "The National Park Service will not accept wolves that display
human habituation or other problem behaviors" (NPS 2006 §4.4.2.2) — the source wolves were screened
for it; the island then produced it.

**What the plan said the park WOULD do about people.** FEIS Alt A/B common elements (p.17–18):
"Periodic, temporary closures to visitors in some areas of the park would continue to avoid wolf-human
interactions, if necessary. The park would continue to intervene in human-to-wolf interactions to
ensure visitor safety and resource protection." Closures were the designed response.

**What the plan ruled OUT.** Public hunting "not allowed by federal statutory law at the park"; managed
culling dismissed (logistics, staffing, carcasses, wilderness character). Direct management of moose
(translocation, culling, fertility control, biocontrol) "outside the scope of this plan … may require
