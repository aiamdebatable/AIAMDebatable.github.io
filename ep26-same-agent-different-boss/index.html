AI Agents at Work: Does the Same Agent Change When Your Boss Is the Customer?

How does the same AI agent change when your employer, not you, is the customer?

An explainer: what changes when your employer, not you, is the customer

Settled on the record

Why it was in the news

When this episode came out at the start of October 2026, four of the biggest AI companies had just spent four weeks of September starting to sell their personal agents to employers: xAI's Grok Bot, Microsoft's Copilot Autopilot, Meta's Muse and OpenAI's dots.

Settled

What the record has settled

At work it's largely the same agent, wrapped in company controls, and what changes is who's in charge of it. OpenAI, Microsoft and Anthropic all say they don't train on business customers' data by default, while on personal plans Microsoft's and Anthropic's terms make you responsible for what the agent does. Only OpenAI writes down what to do with an agent's memory when someone leaves: reset it.

What people get wrong

People assume the split is simple: at home they train on you, at work you're safe and your boss reads everything. The record splits by company and by setting: Meta's Muse starts with training switched on, Microsoft's new Copilot app says it doesn't train on your prompts and files, and at work it's IT that can read the log if it's set up to. The part people miss is the day you leave: the agent that learned how you work stays behind, and most companies haven't said what happens to it.

This one's an explainer: no sides were argued, so there's no dial.

What our fact-check caught

Our drafts got these wrong before they aired. Every correction is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidMicrosoft's personal Copilot trains on what you type by default.

    The record saysNot the new Copilot app: Microsoft says its prompts, answers and files aren't used to train its models.

  2. Draft saidAgent makers cap what they owe at 12 months of fees, with no special clause for what the agent does.

    The record saysThat's only the business terms. On personal plans, Microsoft's and Anthropic's terms make you responsible for the agent's actions, and Anthropic owes at most six months of fees or $100, whichever is more.

  3. Draft saidCalifornia's No Robo Bosses bill would ban predicting workers' behavior.

    The record saysIts final text has no such ban. It covers systems employers use to discipline and fire people, not workers' own agents, and would start July 1, 2027 if signed.

  4. Draft saidGrok Bot starts with every door closed.

    The record saysAccounts start with no access, but its web access is open unless a company admin sets a policy.
