How Your Ballot Gets Counted: Can You Watch Every Step?

The question: What actually happens to your ballot after you let go of it, and how much of it can you watch?

▶ Watch on YouTubeThe full record ↓

An explainer: one Michigan ballot followed from the box to the hand audit, and every door you can walk through (follows ep27–ep29)

Why anyone is asking now

When this episode came out in October 2026, more than two million absentee ballots had already gone out to Michigan voters for the midterms. That summer, Pew had found the two parties almost equally confident the midterms would be run fairly: 55% of Republicans and 58% of Democrats.

What the record has settled

In Michigan, every counting machine is tested in public before use, early processing produces no totals until 8 p.m. on Election Day, and the county and state boards must certify based on the returns. In the 2024 hand audit of nearly 120,000 ballots in the race for president, the gap between the top two candidates moved by one vote. Of the six steps, you can watch five; the one closed door is the signature check itself.

What people get wrong

The episode checked the claims people repeat from both sides, four mostly heard on the right, three on the left and three on both, and none holds up the way it's usually said. Signature mismatch is the top reason mail ballots get rejected, yet nationally in 2024 only about one returned mail ballot in a hundred was rejected for any reason. And trust follows who wins more than what people see: seeing how it works helps most as a shield before the result.

What our fact-check caught

18 of the 156 claims we checked came back wrong in our drafts. Every one is in the published log.

  1. Draft saidBad-faith poll-watcher stories "lower confidence in the election"

    The record saysThe study measured views of the watchers, not of the election

  2. Draft saidArizona's counting-room livestream is "required by statute"

    The record saysRequired, but contingent on the legislature funding it, and live only while ballots are tabulated

  3. Draft saidMedia may record "if authorized"

    The record saysWide shots only, no ballot face visible, with the permission of the person in charge

  4. Draft said"119,567 ballots in 166 precincts" audited

    The record saysNot in the report, which says "nearly 120,000" ballots

  5. Draft saidAbsentee challengers "1 per 8 inspectors"

    The record saysThat ratio applies only before Election Day; on Election Day, one per counting board per organization
