## 1. Why now — what was hunted, and what was rejected

The scout brief's hook (2026-09-30) was the calendar: ballots already out, plus a poll and a study. The
re-hunt on 2026-10-04 found two newer events, both about the process rather than about any party:

- **Chosen:** a misprint in Wayne County, Michigan put a library board candidate in the wrong race on
  absentee ballots in Belleville, Sumpter Township and Van Buren Township. At least 8,500 voters are being
  mailed new ballots, and faulty ballots already returned will be copied by hand onto correct ones
  (township notices of 2026-09-29; Votebeat, 2026-10-02).
- **Companion:** cities and townships are posting the public tests of their counting machines this month
  (examples: Eagle Township 10-05, Farmington Hills 10-12, Cass County's early-voting equipment 10-13,
  Dexter Township 10-19).
- **Rejected:** an advocacy group's claim of 668 duplicate absentee ballots mailed in Michigan (unverified
  and disputed by a local clerk); the federal-versus-state disputes of 2026 (context only, by the show's
  rule); the Federal Register returned nothing on elections for the window.

## 2. The engine's own work

deep-research-v3, explainer mode, 2026-10-04: 24 finders, 118 unique claims, 12 verified with the primary

## Fairness audit

- **Process, never who wins.** No candidate is named anywhere in the record the episode will draw on. The
  winner effect is shown both ways with dated evidence (2004: the party holding the White House was the
  confident one, 87 to 59; 2024, with the other party in the White House, 84 to 28), so it can air without
  a name.
- **The 2020 rule.** The Detroit staffing figures from 2020 and 2022 were deliberately not checked and are
  dropped; the misprint sits in suburban townships and a nonpartisan library race.
- ⚠ **The myths list leans one way.** Of the popular claims checked, more are claims most often made on the
  right (machines flip votes, observers are banned, duplicate ballots are fakes) than on the left (a
  challenger can stop you voting). The most common left-leaning claim, that signature matching
  disenfranchises voters in large numbers, was not checked in this pass and will be checked with the same
  rigour before scripting. If the record still leans after both sides' claims are checked, the episode says
  so; nothing is trimmed or added to even the count. Balance here means looking both ways, not forcing an
  even result. **Update at scripting (2026-10-04):** checked, § 4a; the list still leans 4 right / 3 left / 3 both,
  and the script says so.
- ⚠ **Other-state examples:** which party runs each office used as an example (Arizona, Nevada, Georgia,
  Colorado, Pennsylvania) was not verified. Check before claiming the examples are balanced.
- **Federal-versus-state 2026 disputes** are kept to context, at most one neutral line.

## Open questions this episode could not settle

- Which legal authority the Wayne County hand-copying rests on (officials cite the state's standing
  guidance; the duplication statute speaks of ballots the machine "cannot properly count"). Ask the Bureau
  of Elections or the county clerk; until then the script never says the statute requires it here.
- Whether the public may watch the signature check itself (no text read grants or forbids it).
- Whether the recording ban in absentee counting places applies when ballots cured after Election Day are
  tabulated at a county canvass meeting, where the Open Meetings Act gives a right to record.
- The full papers behind three trust figures (10 to 12.5 points, the audit-results effect, and the "both
  agree" survey shares) could not be opened. Until they are, they air only as "a university release
  reports", or not at all.
- The Maine poll-worker study has no published paper, sample size or effect size; it airs without a figure.
- Live absentee numbers (2,015,074 sent and 123,963 returned as of 2026-10-04) change daily and must be
  re-read on the day the script is locked.

### 4a. The left-leaning myth, checked: "signature matching throws out huge numbers of votes"

Checked 2026-10-04 at scripting, to the standard of the others. Sources: the EAC's 2024 Election
Administration and Voting Survey report (https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/2024_EAVS_Report_508.pdf)
and its Michigan state profile; Michigan's statute (https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-168-766a);
and two press accounts, Michigan Public
(https://www.michiganpublic.org/politics-government/2024-11-07/did-your-ballot-get-rejected-because-of-a-signature-problem-heres-how-to-fix-it)
and Bridge Michigan (https://bridgemi.com/michigan-government/signature-errors-ruin-thousands-michigan-ballots-dont-be-voter/).

- **National, 2024 (EAC, the federal survey of every state):** "Of the mail ballots that were returned by
  voters, 47,629,437 were reported as being counted and 584,463 were reported as being rejected, for a
  count rate of 98.8% … and a rejection rate of 1.2%". Reasons (Table 1): "Ballot envelope had a
  non-matching or incomplete signature 40.7%"; "Ballot envelope did not have a voter signature 10%".
  "By far the most common reason states reported for rejecting a mail ballot was that the voter's
  signature did not match what was on file for the voter or was incomplete." On air: "about one returned
  mail ballot in a hundred was rejected, for any reason; about two in five of those over a signature that
  didn't match, or wasn't complete". ⚠ No product is aired: the 1.2% and the 40.7% are computed over
  different sets of states (casewise deletion), so multiplying them would be false precision.
- **Cure, nationally:** 585,457 set aside for curing, 317,191 cured, 270,753 rejected (EAC). Not aired.
- **Michigan, 2024:** 0.8% of returned mail ballots entered the cure process (EAC Michigan brief, C7a/C1b).
  Not aired. ⚠ The EAC's Michigan "returned but not counted" share (3.1%) is not a rejection rate (it
  covers ballots not counted for any reason) and conflicts on its face with a press report of "less than 1%"
  rejected for any reason (Michigan Public). The conflict was not resolved, so **no Michigan rejection rate
  is aired.**
- **Michigan, variation by clerk (the grain of truth):** Bridge Michigan's analysis of a Secretary of State
  spreadsheet, August 2020 primary: "More than 800 people … didn't have their vote counted because their
  signature on the ballot envelope didn't match"; "in 85 percent of Michigan cities and townships, not a
  single ballot was rejected for a mismatch"; Sherman Township rejected 11 of 702. On air: "One check found
  most Michigan towns rejected none for a mismatch, and a few rejected far more" (the date is on screen
  only; it is a primary, not the 2020 general).
- **The law since then:** MCL 168.766a, "Add. 2023, Act 82, Eff. Feb. 13, 2024": "significant and obvious",
  slight dissimilarities resolved for the voter, notice and a cure. On air: "the law since twenty
  twenty-four sets the bar".

**Verdict:** the claim as usually said ("huge numbers") is not supported: about 1 in 100 returned mail
ballots was rejected nationally in 2024, for any reason. But it carries the most substantial grain of truth
on the list: signature problems are the single most common reason, and Michigan's own record showed
standards that varied sharply by clerk before the 2024 law. Both halves air.

**Lean:** with this checked, the list still leans (4 right, 3 left, 3 both, as classified above). The
script says so plainly, and nothing was added or dropped to even it (Lucas, 2026-10-04: no false balance).
