# Research brief — How Your Ballot Gets Counted: Every Step, and Where You Can Watch

_The full research behind the episode. This is the "full research" we link below the video. It is an
EXPLAINER: one Michigan ballot followed from the moment the voter lets go of it to certification, with
every checkpoint and every place the public may watch. Every figure here is in the fact-check log._

## The question

What actually happens to your ballot after you let go of it, and how much of it can you watch? Distrust
of the count is now close to even between the parties: in July 2026, 55% of Republicans and 58% of
Democrats said they were confident the midterms will be run fairly and accurately (Pew). So the ballot's
journey is a question for every viewer, whoever they vote for.

## Why now

Ballots for the 2026-11-03 midterms are already in Michigan voters' hands, and one batch has already gone
wrong and been caught. On 2026-09-28 Wayne County told Belleville, Sumpter Township and Van Buren Township
that the absentee ballots they had mailed listed a library board candidate in the wrong race. At least
8,500 voters are being sent new ones, and officials say any faulty ballot that comes back will be copied
by hand onto a correct one, under the state's standing guidance for these errors
([Van Buren Township notice, 2026-09-29](https://www.vanburen-mi.org/_T6_R1334.php);
[Belleville notice, 2026-09-29](https://bellevilleonthelake.com/news-and-notices/absentee-ballot-notice);
[Votebeat, 2026-10-02](https://www.votebeat.org/michigan/2026/10/02/wayne-county-ballot-misprint-belleville-library-van-buren-sumpter/);
county statement as quoted by [FOX 2 Detroit](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/absentee-ballot-michigan-election-error-belleville-sumpter-van-buren-township)).
Michigan's general rule for a damaged or defective absentee ballot is that election inspectors from
different parties copy it onto a duplicate, serial-numbered back to the original
([MCL 168.765c](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-168-765c)); which provision
officials are applying to this misprint has not been stated.

The "where can I watch" half is live too. Cities and townships are posting the public tests of their
counting machines this month: Eagle Township on 10-05, Farmington Hills on 10-12, Cass County's
early-voting equipment on 10-13 ([notice](https://www.casscountymi.org/m/newsflash/home/detail/712)),
Dexter Township on 10-19. The state rule is titled "Public accuracy test", and the time and place must be
published at least 48 hours ahead ([MCL 168.798](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-168-798)).

**Split:** the description carries the Wayne County misprint (hot, re-peggable after publish). The script
carries the structural material: the duplication rule, the public test, the ballots-out calendar. It names
places and rules, never a person or a party. Never "Detroit", never 2020.

## Settled: the ballot's journey (Michigan, 2026)

Each step as the record supports saying it.

1. **The envelope arrives.** Your city or township clerk compares your signature with the ones on file,
   and can reject it only if it differs "in significant and obvious respects". If it is rejected or
   unsigned, the clerk has to contact you, by phone, email or text if they have them, and you have until
   **5 p.m. on Friday, November 6** to fix it. (The deadline to fix a signature on your ballot
   *application* is earlier: 4 p.m., Friday, October 30.)
2. **Before any ballot is counted, the machines are tested in public.** Every tabulator is run on a test
   deck that gives each candidate a different number of votes; it must come out perfect and be
   certified. The test is announced at least 48 hours ahead, and must be done by **October 19** for
   early-voting machines and **October 29** for Election Day machines.
3. **Early processing is not early results.** Towns of 5,000 or more can choose to start opening and
   tabulating absentee ballots up to eight days early, from **October 26**. No totals can be produced
   until **8 p.m. on Election Day**, and each day's ballots are sealed. Such towns must post where and when
   they will count by **October 16**.
4. **Counting.** Wherever absentee ballots are counted, at least one inspector from each major party has
   to be in the room the whole time.
5. **A ballot the machine can't read is copied, not guessed.** Two inspectors from different parties copy
   it onto a fresh ballot marked "duplicate", with a serial number that ties it to the original, which is
   kept.
6. **The county canvass.** After the polls close, each county's bipartisan board of canvassers checks that
   the numbers add up, and must finish by **November 17**. It is arithmetic and paperwork, not a recount;
   the law calls the job "ministerial". Ballots cured after Election Day are tabulated at a county
   canvassers' meeting.
7. **State certification.** The four-member Board of State Canvassers, two from each major party, must
   certify by **November 23**, in a meeting open to the public and streamed online. (In 2024 it certified
   on day 17; in 2022, on day 20.)
8. **Audits.** After certification, precincts are picked at random for audits, including a hand count,
   and the state constitution says audits are "conducted in public". In 2024 the hand count covered nearly
   120,000 ballots, and the largest change for any candidate was **11 votes**.

## Where you can watch

| step | who may watch | the rule |
|---|---|---|
| Signature check | No stated public right to watch the comparison; challengers may watch ballots being issued and received | Bureau of Elections challenger manual (July 2026) |
| Public accuracy test | Anyone; the clerk may cap numbers by room size; 48 hours' notice | MCL 168.798; the state's "Public accuracy test" rule |
| Early processing | Party and group challengers (capped); public poll watchers from a viewing area | MCL 168.765a; Bureau manual |
| Election Day absentee counting | Challengers; public poll watchers from a public viewing area; authorized media, wide shots only; nobody else records | MCL 168.730, 733, 765a; Bureau manual |
| Precinct count and canvass | Public ("shall be public") | MCL 168.801 |
| County canvass meetings | Anyone, with a right to record; 18 hours' notice | Open Meetings Act, MCL 15.263 |
| State certification | Anyone in person; livestream | MCL 168.22d; Open Meetings Act |
| Post-election audits | Public | Michigan Constitution, Art. II §4 |

## The reframe: does seeing the process restore trust?

Who wins moves trust several times more than anything an election office can show you. After the 2024
result, the winning side's confidence in the mail count rose 53 points and the losing side's fell 17
(Pew). The swing runs both ways over time: in 2004 the party holding the White House was the confident
one, 87 to 59; in 2024, with the other party in the White House, it was 84 to 28 (Gallup).

Seeing the process is a smaller lever, but it is the one that works the same for both sides. In survey
experiments, one short official video raised trust in the count by roughly 2 to 13 points, measured soon
after viewing. The team's own real-world test a month later, on skeptical conservatives, moved one of five
measures, by 1.3 points. The one study that measured both on the same scale found a short audit video,
watched four weeks earlier, protected about as much trust as losing took away. It shielded trust; it did
not close the gap between winners and losers.

**The honest line:** seeing how the count works is most useful *before* the result, as a shield, not
after it, as a cure.

## Both sides quietly agree

Winning moves trust more than any official can, and people who actually see the work trust it more. The
open question is scale: 772,433 people worked the polls nationally in 2024, against 158 million ballots.

## Verdict framing: the dial you set

No winner is declared, because this is an explainer. The viewer gets one dial: **what would you need to
see to trust the count?** A livestream, a public machine test, an audit result, a seat in the viewing
area, or your own shift as a poll worker. Each setting has a real place you can go in Michigan this fall,
and each has a documented limit.

## Myths the record does not support

| claim | what the record shows |
|---|---|
| Tabulators flip votes | The 2024 hand audit of nearly 120,000 ballots moved the top two candidates by 11 and 10 votes; the margin between them moved by one |
| Machines are online during the count | Not connected while tabulating; connected after counting to send unofficial results; the paper is kept |
| Mail ballots are only counted if the race is close | Counting boards process and tabulate every valid absentee ballot |
| Counts that change after election night mean fraud | Late shifts are a few tenths of a point, in either party's direction, driven by county size and ballot type |
| Observers are banned | The law requires space for challengers; their numbers are capped |
| Early processing means early results | Ballots are tabulated early; no results exist before 8 p.m. on Election Day |
| A challenger can stop you voting | A voter who answers the oath questions "shall be entitled to receive a ballot and vote"; indiscriminate challenges are a misdemeanor |
| Canvassers can refuse to certify and overturn a result | Certification is "ministerial, clerical, and nondiscretionary" |

⚠ **The list leans, and may keep leaning.** More of these claims are most often made on the right than on
the left. The most common left-leaning claim (that signature matching disenfranchises voters in large
numbers) has not been checked yet; check it with the same rigour before scripting. If the record still
leans one way after both sides' claims are checked, the list leans, and the script says so plainly. No
item is trimmed or added to even it (the show's rule, 2026-10-04: no false balance).

## Script traps

- Never say Michigan law **requires** the Wayne County hand-copying. Say officials will copy the choices by
  hand; state the two-party rule only as Michigan's general rule.
- Say **"at least 8,500"** voters, never 10,300. "Nearly 10,000" only as the three communities'
  permanent mail-vote list (9,965, CBS).
- Cass County's 10-13 test covers its **early-voting** equipment only. Present the public tests as
  "cities and townships are posting their tests now".
- "Processed early" never means "counted early" in the sense of results. Ballots are tabulated; results
  are withheld until 8 p.m.
- The 10 to 12.5-point trust figure is trust in **another state's** elections, from a university release
  (the paper could not be opened). Air it only as "a university release reports", or not at all.
- The Maine poll-worker study has no published figure; describe it without one.
- Don't air the 64%/68% and 30%-vs-7% "both agree" survey shares or the 5.5 to 6-point audit-results
  effect until the papers are read.
- 2024 turnout: about 78% of active registered voters. The Bureau's own "74.6% of active registered
  voters" is a wording error (74.6% is turnout of the citizen voting-age population).
- Arizona's counting-room camera law depends on the legislature funding it, and streams only during
  tabulation.
- No candidate is named, the Detroit counts are dropped, and the federal-versus-state disputes of 2026 get
  at most one neutral line.
