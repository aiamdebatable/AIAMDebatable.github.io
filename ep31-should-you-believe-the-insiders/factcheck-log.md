- **CONFIRMED** — the source says what the episode says.
- **CORRECTED** — the source says otherwise; the wording was changed, and the change is listed under
  "Corrections applied to source" below.
- **UNRESOLVED** — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.

**Verification status:** adversarial-pass — a peg hunt (2026-10-04, blind sweep with every lab, person and

## 1. Why now — what was hunted, and what was rejected

The scout brief's hook (an Anthropic researcher's public resignation on 2026-09-08) was almost four weeks
old. The re-hunt found a fresher one with a clean, vocabulary-free query:

- **Chosen:** on 2026-10-03 the man who led the writing of OpenAI's launch safety reports went public in
  The Atlantic ("I Quit OpenAI Because Its Culture Is Broken"), calling himself part of "a parade of former
  colleagues … who have decided that the current path is unacceptable". It came two days after OpenAI said
  it had parted ways with three staff, at least two of them safety and alignment researchers. The essay
  states no probability, discloses the PR firm he hired after quitting, and cites only public incidents.
- **Rejected:** a Senate subcommittee hearing on rogue AI (an incidents question, covered elsewhere), a
  White House industry accord (a who-checks question, covered elsewhere), OpenAI notifying organisations
  about its agents' activity (a guardrail question, covered elsewhere), and an executive order renaming AI
  in federal documents (at most an aside).

## 2. The engine's own work

deep-research-v3, 2026-10-04: 24 finders, 118 unique claims, 12 verified with the primary opened (12 held,
0 flagged), 106 not adjudicated by the engine, 18 gaps named by its critics. The engine itself reported that

Anthropic researcher's resignation week a quiet one for the company, when its CEO had published a slow-down
