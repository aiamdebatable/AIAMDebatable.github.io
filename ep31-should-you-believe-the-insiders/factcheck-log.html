1. Why now — what was hunted, and what was rejected

The scout brief's hook (an Anthropic researcher's public resignation on 2026-09-08) was almost four weeks old. The re-hunt found a fresher one with a clean, vocabulary-free query:

  • Chosen: on 2026-10-03 the man who led the writing of OpenAI's launch safety reports went public in The Atlantic ("I Quit OpenAI Because Its Culture Is Broken"), calling himself part of "a parade of former colleagues … who have decided that the current path is unacceptable". It came two days after OpenAI said it had parted ways with three staff, at least two of them safety and alignment researchers. The essay states no probability, discloses the PR firm he hired after quitting, and cites only public incidents.
  • Rejected: a Senate subcommittee hearing on rogue AI (an incidents question, covered elsewhere), a White House industry accord (a who-checks question, covered elsewhere), OpenAI notifying organisations about its agents' activity (a guardrail question, covered elsewhere), and an executive order renaming AI in federal documents (at most an aside).

2. The engine's own work

deep-research-v3, 2026-10-04: 24 finders, 118 unique claims, 12 verified with the primary opened (12 held, 0 flagged), 106 not adjudicated by the engine, 18 gaps named by its critics. The engine itself reported that all five events behind the hook were secondary-only and that the skeptics were heard mostly through third parties; the hand pass below fixed both. ⚠ One of the engine's 12 held claims was wrong: it called the Anthropic researcher's resignation week a quiet one for the company, when its CEO had published a slow-down essay with a unilateral commitment that week (claim B7 below).
