AI Extinction Risk: Should You Believe the People Building It?

The question: When the people building AI say it might end us, how much of that is knowledge, how much is fear, and how much is marketing — and how would you tell?

▶ Watch on YouTubeThe full record ↓

You can't check their number. You can check what it cost them, who backs them, and what their company did that week.

Why anyone is asking now

When this episode came out in October 2026, an Anthropic researcher had quit in public in September, and the company's alignment science lead put the chance AI kills all humans above ten percent within a decade. On October 3, the man who had led the writing of OpenAI's safety reports went public that he had quit too.

The fight

Green arguesBelieve them

A researcher walked away from shares that hadn't vested, and within hours a lead at his own company backed him by name. Their number sits at the field's typical answer, one in ten, and on how fast AI moves, the more worried side has come closer. When insiders pay to warn you, the burden moves to whoever says they're wrong.

Gold arguesWeigh them

A cost proves he means it, not that he's right. The inside runs about two months ahead of the public, the incidents they cite were already public, and a chance of extinction has no method or track record. So weigh them: what it cost, who backs them by name, what the company did that week, and where the number sits.

What both sides quietly agree on

Nobody can check a chance of extinction. The insiders' number sits in the middle of what published researchers say, some who spoke paid for it while some show no cost at all, and nothing they've cited so far was a secret.

What the shouting gets wrong

The record leans, on both counts: these warnings look sincere, and they don't look like secret knowledge. Neither "they know what you can't see" nor "it's just marketing" survived. What's left is whose number you start from, and what the next alarm has to show you before you move it.

The verdict

Raj This one doesn't resolve, and neither team wins it. Three things from me: what's settled, what survived, and what's yours.

Raj Both teams accepted the same record. Nobody can check a chance of extinction. The insiders' number sits in the middle of what published researchers say. Some who spoke paid for it, and some show no cost at all. And nothing they've cited so far was a secret.

Raj What survived from green. Real costs for some, and colleagues still inside who backed them by name, within hours. A lab's own authors calling their scariest test a scenario they built. And on the pace of AI, the more worried forecasters came closer.

Raj What survived from gold. On capability, the inside runs about two months ahead of the public. The number has no method, and nobody can show who forecasts this well. And the money runs in every direction, including toward fear.

Raj What fell. Green's they know what you can't see: the record doesn't show it. Gold's it's marketing: nobody has shown a campaign, and the labs often talk their own results down.

Raj So here's where the record leans, and it does lean. These warnings look sincere. They don't look secret. That isn't a split decision. It's what the evidence says, on both counts.

Raj That leaves one dial neither team can set for you. Whose number do you start from? The people inside, as their own letter asks, which fails when they can't see more than you. The field's survey, which fails on who answers it. Or the forecasters, who can't yet show they're any good at this either.

Raj So the question we're handing you isn't whether it's ten percent. It's whose number you start from, and what the next alarm has to show you before you move it. That one's yours.
