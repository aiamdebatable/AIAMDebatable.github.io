Fact-check log — Deepfakes and Elections: Label It, Ban It, or Answer It?

Independent verification pass, run 2026-10-03 (web-sourced, not trusting the draft). Each claim gets a verdict: CONFIRMED, CORRECTED, UNRESOLVED or APPROXIMATE. This is a REQUIRED gate, set out under "Verification gate" in the episodes README. The show's differentiator is that this file is real.

  • CONFIRMED — the source says what the episode says.
  • CORRECTED — the source says otherwise; the script was changed, and the change is listed under "Corrections applied to source" below. These stay in the file: they are the point of it.
  • UNRESOLVED — could not be established from a source worth trusting, so it is not used on air.
  • APPROXIMATE — checked, roughly right, and deliberately not cited on air as a precise figure.

Verification statusadversarial-pass _(a deep-research fan-out with single-vote primary verification,

then hand passes that opened each primary: court orders and dockets, statutes, FCC and FEC records, the studies, the platforms' own pages)_

Why now — the peg, and what was weighed against it

ChosenAccountability in State Government v. Knudsen (D. Mont., order of 16 Sept 2026) and the

Weighed and rejectedHawaii's law struck in January (too old for the peg; used as research); an AP

1. A federal judge blocked Montana's law, for the people who sued, as likely viewpoint discrimination

CONFIRMED. "the Act treats identically deceptive media differently based solely on whether its message is weaponized to damage or deployed to elevate a candidate" — likely "viewpoint-based discrimination". Relief runs to the named plaintiffs only; Montana's law still applies to others.

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819.31.0.pdf

2. The court said the label didn't save it, and named the alternatives

CONFIRMED. A government-mandated label "functions as an outright alteration of the speech itself"; the court listed "tort law, criminal liability for election interference, educational campaigns, and counterspeech" as less restrictive alternatives the state had not shown to be inadequate.

  • https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819.31.0.pdf

3. At least 164 AI political ads, just under $80 million; 69% of ads (78% of spending) unlabelled

CONFIRMED. Wesleyan Media Project, 4 Sept 2026; described by Wesleyan as an undercount.

  • https://mediaproject.wesleyan.edu/releases-090426/

4. Labelling was no better in states that require it (29%) than in states that don't (32%)

CORRECTED (attribution). An earlier note called it an AP analysis. It is a cross-check by a Wesleyan co-director against NCSL's list, quoted by the AP; the sample size is not stated. Used only with that attribution and "small sample". A different "64% vs 27%" figure seen in a search summary has no source text and is not used.

  • https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/politics/2026/09/25/ai-ads-are-popular-in-the-2026-midterms-but-state-laws-havent-stopped-a-lack-of-disclosure/

5. NCSL counts 31 states: 28 by disclosure, Minnesota and Texas with windowed bans, Maryland year-round

CONFIRMED (NCSL's own count, dated 23 June 2026). A "33 enacted, 31 live" figure attributed to Public Citizen is our own count of its table, not its claim; not used.

  • https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/artificial-intelligence-ai-in-elections-and-campaigns

6. Several "label" laws are bans a label can cure: Michigan 90 days; California 30 days (cut from 120); Montana 60 days

CONFIRMED. Michigan MCL 168.932f; California Elections Code 20012 as amended by AB 502 (Stats. 2026, ch. 333, effective 19 Sept 2026); Montana MCA 13-35-802.

  • https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-168-932f
  • https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=ELEC&sectionNum=20012
  • https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0130/chapter_0350/part_0080/section_0020/0130-0350-0080-0020.html

7. Texas: video only, 30 days before an election, intent to injure a candidate or influence the result

CONFIRMED. Election Code 255.004(d); a Class A misdemeanor (subsection (c)). Current text read from a legal publisher's copy; the official site would not load.

  • https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/pdf/SB00751F.pdf
  • https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/election-code/elec-sect-255-004/

8. Maryland's 2026 law: intent, harm, a satire exemption, no window, up to $5,000 and/or 5 years, and a duty to correct

CONFIRMED. Chapter 444 (SB 141). The State Administrator of Elections "SHALL" publish a correction. It covers images, audio and video.

  • https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_444_sb0141E.pdf

9. Louisiana's 2026 law: label-only, 60 days, state and local races only

CORRECTED. An earlier note read an earlier version of the bill. The enacted Act 806 (signed 9 June, effective 1 Aug 2026) has no 60-day window, does have a deception test, and carries a criminal penalty (up to $2,000 and 2 years) for intent to injure or deceive. Whether it excludes federal candidates depends on an existing definition we did not read. Not used for the "state and local only" point.

  • https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1481244

10. California's law was blocked for the named plaintiffs; a second law fell on federal platform immunity

CONFIRMED. AB 2839: permanent injunction "against the named Plaintiffs" (29 Aug 2025). AB 2655: preempted by Section 230 as to two platforms (20 Aug 2025). California's own appeal brief says AB 2839 "is not at issue on this appeal"; the appeal (AB 2655) is briefed, with no ruling found.

  • https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-26.pdf
  • https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-24.pdf
  • https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71503636/the-babylon-bee-llc-et-al-v-bonta-et-al/

11. California's court said a narrow law about voting logistics "could survive"

APPROXIMATE. The order lists narrower laws California "could" adopt — including one limited to "the time, date, place, or manner of voting" — and says they "might" fit recognised exceptions. It rules on none. The episode says the courts have pointed to that target, not that it is safe.

  • https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-26.pdf

12. Hawaii's law was blocked in full; Hawaii did not appeal

CORRECTED (fees). Permanent injunction against enforcing Act 191, the whole statute (30 Jan 2026); the disclaimer safe harbour did not save it; no notice of appeal on the docket. An earlier note said Hawaii "paid" $118,000 in fees: the state agreed to pay $118,237.47 pending an appropriation by its legislature. Not aired as paid.

  • https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/babylon-bee-lopez-opinion.pdf
  • https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70456324/the-babylon-bee-llc-v-lopez/

13. Minnesota's deepfake law was not "upheld"

CONFIRMED. The 8th Circuit held "only that the district court did not abuse its discretion in denying extraordinary preliminary relief in light of the delay"; one challenger's labelled parody fell outside the law. Rehearing denied 31 March 2026 (an earlier note said about 2 April — corrected).

  • https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/02/251300P.pdf

14. The 8th Circuit blocked part of a Minnesota law against lies meant to stop people voting

CONFIRMED. Minnesota Voters Alliance v. Ellison, No. 24-3094 (20 Aug 2026), Minn. Stat. § 211B.075, as applied to one group's statements about felon voting: "there is no 'historic and traditional categor[y]' of unprotected speech covering false statements about voting"; the statute lacks the reliance element that makes fraud punishable; counter-speech is the less restrictive means. It did not decide a pure time-place-manner lie.

  • https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/08/243094P.pdf

15. The Supreme Court said states "may prohibit messages intended to mislead voters about voting requirements and procedures"

CONFIRMED, as a footnote. Minnesota Voters Alliance v. Mansky (2018), n.4 — dicta; the law in that case was struck. The 8th Circuit reads it as "when doing so is consistent with the First Amendment".

  • https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-1435_2co3.pdf

16. Counter-speech is the courts' standard alternative for false political speech

CONFIRMED. 281 Care Committee v. Arneson (8th Cir. 2014): "counterspeech is the tried and true buffer and elixir"; United States v. Alvarez (2012, plurality): "The remedy for speech that is false is speech that is true."

  • https://media.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/14/09/131229P.pdf

17. No conviction under a state election-deepfake law has been found

APPROXIMATE (a search finding). Found: an Oregon investigation (no outcome), a Texas complaint to a county sheriff (receipt confirmed only), civil suits, cease-and-desist letters. Prosecutors' and election agencies' records were not searched. Said as "we found no conviction", never "there has never been one".

  • https://www.opb.org/article/2026/07/09/artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-political-advertisements-oregon/

18. New Hampshire, January 2024: 9,581 calls in a cloned presidential voice, two days before the primary

CONFIRMED. FCC forfeiture order: 9,581 calls initiated on 21 Jan 2024. The message's exact text is in the order; the episode paraphrases it. Spoken as "nearly ten thousand" (the exact 9,581 is on screen) — the show rounds what is said and keeps the precise figure in the picture.

  • https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-104A1.pdf

19. The FCC fined the consultant $6 million — under the caller-ID spoofing law

CONFIRMED. Truth in Caller ID Act; $2,000 × 3,000 verified calls. Whether it was collected is unknown; the episode never says it was paid.

  • https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-104A1.pdf

20. The carrier settled for $1 million

CONFIRMED. Consent decree, 21 Aug 2024 (proposed: $2 million), over caller-ID authentication — not the fake voice.

  • https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-24-790A1.pdf

21. AI voices count as "artificial" under the robocall law

CONFIRMED. FCC declaratory ruling, adopted 2 Feb, released 8 Feb 2024.

  • https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf

22. A jury acquitted him of all 22 criminal charges (June 2025)

CONFIRMED (secondary). 11 felony voter-suppression and 11 misdemeanor impersonation counts, 13 June

  1. The state's 2024 indictment release listed 13 of each; why two of each fell away is unresolved.
  • https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/06/16/biden-ai-robocall-new-hampshire-steven-kramer-not-guilty
  • https://www.doj.nh.gov/news-and-media/steven-kramer-charged-voter-suppression-over-ai-generated-president-biden-robocalls

23. Three voters won $7,500 each in federal court

CONFIRMED. A federal judge approved the recommended default judgment on 20 Nov 2025 ($500 under the federal robocall law plus $2,000 under state law, trebled), with a nationwide injunction.

  • https://www.lwv.org/sites/default/files/2025-11/LWVNH%20v.%20Kramer%20-%20Default%20Judgment%20+%20Report%20and%20Recommendation.pdf

24. New Hampshire passed an election-deepfake law afterwards

CORRECTED (which law). An earlier note named the general fraudulent-deepfake felony. The election law is RSA 664:14-c (effective 1 Aug 2024): 90 days before an election, a disclosure safe harbour, and a civil suit by the person depicted.

  • https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/664/664-14-c.htm

25. The FEC has had no quorum since 1 May 2025; its 2024 action was an interpretive rule

CONFIRMED. FEC notice of 6 April 2026 (two commissioners); no confirmation found since. Interpretive rule published 26 Sept 2024. Re-checked before air.

  • https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/fec-notice-of-lack-of-quorum-26-336-04-06-2026.pdf
  • https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2024-21983.pdf

26. The FCC's 2024 proposal for AI disclosure in broadcast political ads was never adopted

CONFIRMED. Proposed July 2024; the docket shows no order and no closure; it is not on the FCC's December 2025 list of terminated dockets.

  • https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-74A1.pdf

27. A plain "AI-generated" label cut belief by 0.14 SD and had no significant effect on sharing

CONFIRMED. Wittenberg et al., PNAS Nexus 2025, n = 7,579; "Manipulated" −0.27 SD and "False" −0.23 SD on belief. Stated intentions about images, not votes.

  • https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/6/pgaf170/8151894

28. Warning people about deepfakes made them doubt real videos

CONFIRMED. Ternovski, Kalla & Aronow (2022), Journal of Online Trust and Safety — about informing people deepfakes exist, not about a label on a specific video.

  • https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/28

29. Of 78 election deepfakes, half had no deceptive intent

CORRECTED (attribution). Kapoor & Narayanan's analysis, on the Knight First Amendment Institute's blog, of WIRED's worldwide tracker: 39 of 78 with no deceptive intent; the authors note the database may be incomplete. Not a joint WIRED/Knight review, and not US-only.

  • https://knightcolumbia.org/blog/we-looked-at-78-election-deepfakes-political-misinformation-is-not-an-ai-problem

30. "No conclusive evidence" deepfakes changed an election result

CORRECTED (wording). The Turing Institute's reports say they found "no evidence" that AI-enabled content "measurably altered an election result", and attribute the gap to "insufficient data on the impact … on real-world voter behaviour". The episode uses their words.

  • https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-enabled-influence-operations-safeguarding-future-elections
  • https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-enabled-influence-operations-threat-analysis-2024-uk-and-european-elections

31. Platforms: AI disclosure on political ads; Meta blocks new political ads in the final week; TikTok bans paid political ads

CONFIRMED, without dates. Meta's own page confirms the disclosure rule and the final-week block but gives no dates (27 Oct–3 Nov is not on it; not used). Google requires an "altered or synthetic content" disclosure on election ads; YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered content; TikTok bans paid political ads and requires AI labels. X's policy page is generic and dated April 2025.

  • https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/
  • https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6014595?hl=en
  • https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en
  • https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity
  • https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity

32. "Every federal court that has weighed one of these laws against free speech has blocked it"

CORRECTED (wording). The first draft said every federal court that "reached" these laws blocked them. Minnesota's federal courts reached its law and declined to block it — on standing and delay, without weighing it against the First Amendment (entry 13). The claim holds only for the courts that weighed the laws on free-speech grounds: California (#10), Hawaii (#12) and Montana (#1). The script and brief now say so.

Corrections applied to source

  • "Every federal court that reached one blocked it" → "every federal court that has weighed one against free speech" (Minnesota's procedural rulings) → script, brief.
  • Montana scope: "blocked Montana's law" → blocked for the plaintiffs only → peg, brief.
  • Minnesota (8th Cir., Feb 2026): "upheld" → no merits ruling; rehearing date → 31 March → brief.
  • The "29% vs 32%" comparison: an AP analysis → a Wesleyan researcher's cross-check quoted by AP → brief.
  • Hawaii's fees: "paid" → agreed, pending appropriation → brief.
  • Louisiana: earlier bill version → enacted Act 806 (no window; criminal penalty) → brief.
  • New Hampshire's later law: general deepfake felony → RSA 664:14-c → brief.
  • The 78-deepfakes analysis: attribution → Kapoor & Narayanan on WIRED's tracker → brief.
  • Turing reports: "no conclusive evidence" → their exact wording → brief.
  • The "lies about voting can be banned" consensus → narrowed by the 8th Circuit's August ruling → reframe.

Fairness audit

  • Both teams carry their strongest evidence and its weakness (Green: labels ignored, late fakes can't be answered — but bans keep losing and no conviction exists; Gold: courts and satire — but the criminal case in the one clear harm collapsed).
  • No party split of AI ads on air (ruled 2026-10-01); nothing implies both parties use AI ads equally.
  • Named-race complaints are described by process only; no candidate is the subject.
  • Cast rotation: everyone from the previous debate rests; no one holds the seat they held two debates ago. The restrictive rail (gold, "draw a line") is argued by a woman this episode, after a man last time; the other rail by a man. The judge and the desk lead are each in that seat for the first time in this run of episodes.

Open questions this episode could not settle

  • Would a fraud-shaped law aimed only at voting logistics survive? No court has tested one in this setting.
  • Has California's 30-day rewrite been challenged?
  • Did the FCC ever collect the $6 million?
