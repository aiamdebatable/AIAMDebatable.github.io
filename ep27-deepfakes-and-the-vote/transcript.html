The verdict

AndreThis one doesn't resolve, and neither team wins it. Three things from me: what's settled, what survived, and what's yours.

AndreBoth teams accepted the same record. Thirty-one states have a law. Most ads skip the label. Every federal court that has weighed one of these laws against free speech has blocked it, the label offered as a way out never saved one, and no one has been convicted under them. The one clear harm was caught by old laws.

AndreWhat survived from green. The label meant to save these laws was itself held to be compelled speech. Satirists are who keep getting swept in. Warnings make people doubt real video. And the worst case on record was answered by laws that weren't about AI at all.

AndreWhat survived from gold. A fake two days out can't be answered before the polls close. Labels are skipped even where they're required. The national referees are out of action. And the laws that lost were broad ones. A narrow, fraud-shaped design hasn't been struck.

AndreWhat fell. Green's comfort: the old laws caught New Hampshire after the damage, and the criminal case collapsed. Gold's fear: the narrowest line she drew, a law against voting lies, was partly blocked in August, and none of these bans has ever produced a conviction.

AndreSo here's the design that survives both teams. A narrow law against lies about when, where and how to vote, built like fraud: intent, reliance, harm. Disclosure on paid AI ads, with the duty on whoever pays. A duty on election offices to correct, fast, to the same people. And everything else, the mockery, the attacks, left to an answer.

AndreThat leaves one dial neither team can set for you. When a fake of a candidate shows up, how hard should the law lean? Ban it outright, like Texas and Maryland. Ban it unless it's labelled, like Michigan and California. Require a label only, like most of the thirty-one states and the platforms. Or answer it with the laws we already have, the way New Hampshire was handled.

AndreSo the question we're handing you isn't AI or not AI. It's this: when a fake lands in your feed, how hard should the law lean? That one's yours.
