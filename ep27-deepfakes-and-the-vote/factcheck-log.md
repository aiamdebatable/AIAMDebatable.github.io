## Why now — the peg, and what was weighed against it
**Chosen:** *Accountability in State Government v. Knudsen* (D. Mont., order of 16 Sept 2026) and the
Wesleyan Media Project's count (4 Sept 2026).
**Weighed and rejected:** Hawaii's law struck in January (too old for the peg; used as research); an AP
story's labelling comparison (secondary; used with its real attribution); California's pending appeal (no

## 1. A federal judge blocked Montana's law, for the people who sued, as likely viewpoint discrimination
**CONFIRMED.** "the Act treats identically deceptive media differently based solely on whether its message
is weaponized to damage or deployed to elevate a candidate" — likely "viewpoint-based discrimination".
Relief runs to the named plaintiffs only; Montana's law still applies to others.
- https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819.31.0.pdf

## 2. The court said the label didn't save it, and named the alternatives
**CONFIRMED.** A government-mandated label "functions as an outright alteration of the speech itself"; the
court listed "tort law, criminal liability for election interference, educational campaigns, and
counterspeech" as less restrictive alternatives the state had not shown to be inadequate.
- https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819/gov.uscourts.mtd.84819.31.0.pdf

## 3. At least 164 AI political ads, just under $80 million; 69% of ads (78% of spending) unlabelled
**CONFIRMED.** Wesleyan Media Project, 4 Sept 2026; described by Wesleyan as an undercount.
- https://mediaproject.wesleyan.edu/releases-090426/

## 4. Labelling was no better in states that require it (29%) than in states that don't (32%)
**CORRECTED (attribution).** An earlier note called it an AP analysis. It is a cross-check by a Wesleyan
co-director against NCSL's list, quoted by the AP; the sample size is not stated. Used only with that

- https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/politics/2026/09/25/ai-ads-are-popular-in-the-2026-midterms-but-state-laws-havent-stopped-a-lack-of-disclosure/

## 7. Texas: video only, 30 days before an election, intent to injure a candidate or influence the result
**CONFIRMED.** Election Code 255.004(d); a Class A misdemeanor (subsection (c)). Current text read from a
legal publisher's copy; the official site would not load.
- https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/pdf/SB00751F.pdf
- https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/election-code/elec-sect-255-004/

## 8. Maryland's 2026 law: intent, harm, a satire exemption, no window, up to $5,000 and/or 5 years, and a duty to correct
**CONFIRMED.** Chapter 444 (SB 141). The State Administrator of Elections "SHALL" publish a correction. It
covers images, audio and video.
- https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_444_sb0141E.pdf

## 9. Louisiana's 2026 law: label-only, 60 days, state and local races only
**CORRECTED.** An earlier note read an earlier version of the bill. The enacted Act 806 (signed 9 June,
effective 1 Aug 2026) has no 60-day window, does have a deception test, and carries a criminal penalty
(up to $2,000 and 2 years) for intent to injure or deceive. Whether it excludes federal candidates depends
on an existing definition we did not read. Not used for the "state and local only" point.
- https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1481244

## 10. California's law was blocked for the named plaintiffs; a second law fell on federal platform immunity
**CONFIRMED.** AB 2839: permanent injunction "against the named Plaintiffs" (29 Aug 2025). AB 2655:
preempted by Section 230 as to two platforms (20 Aug 2025). California's own appeal brief says AB 2839 "is
not at issue on this appeal"; the appeal (AB 2655) is briefed, with no ruling found.
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-26.pdf
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-24.pdf
- https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71503636/the-babylon-bee-llc-et-al-v-bonta-et-al/

## 11. California's court said a narrow law about voting logistics "could survive"
**APPROXIMATE.** The order lists narrower laws California "could" adopt — including one limited to "the
time, date, place, or manner of voting" — and says they "might" fit recognised exceptions. It rules on none.
The episode says the courts have pointed to that target, not that it is safe.
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_24-cv-02527-26.pdf

## 12. Hawaii's law was blocked in full; Hawaii did not appeal
**CORRECTED (fees).** Permanent injunction against enforcing Act 191, the whole statute (30 Jan 2026); the
disclaimer safe harbour did not save it; no notice of appeal on the docket. An earlier note said Hawaii
"paid" $118,000 in fees: the state agreed to pay $118,237.47 pending an appropriation by its legislature.
Not aired as paid.
- https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/babylon-bee-lopez-opinion.pdf
- https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70456324/the-babylon-bee-llc-v-lopez/

## 13. Minnesota's deepfake law was not "upheld"
**CONFIRMED.** The 8th Circuit held "only that the district court did not abuse its discretion in denying
extraordinary preliminary relief in light of the delay"; one challenger's labelled parody fell outside the
law. Rehearing denied 31 March 2026 (an earlier note said about 2 April — corrected).
- https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/02/251300P.pdf

## 14. The 8th Circuit blocked part of a Minnesota law against lies meant to stop people voting
**CONFIRMED.** *Minnesota Voters Alliance v. Ellison*, No. 24-3094 (20 Aug 2026), Minn. Stat. § 211B.075, as
applied to one group's statements about felon voting: "there is no 'historic and traditional categor[y]' of
unprotected speech covering false statements about voting"; the statute lacks the reliance element that
makes fraud punishable; counter-speech is the less restrictive means. It did not decide a pure
time-place-manner lie.
- https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/08/243094P.pdf

## 15. The Supreme Court said states "may prohibit messages intended to mislead voters about voting requirements and procedures"
**CONFIRMED, as a footnote.** *Minnesota Voters Alliance v. Mansky* (2018), n.4 — dicta; the law in that case
was struck. The 8th Circuit reads it as "when doing so is consistent with the First Amendment".
- https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-1435_2co3.pdf

## 16. Counter-speech is the courts' standard alternative for false political speech
**CONFIRMED.** *281 Care Committee v. Arneson* (8th Cir. 2014): "counterspeech is the tried and true buffer
and elixir"; *United States v. Alvarez* (2012, plurality): "The remedy for speech that is false is speech
that is true."
- https://media.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/14/09/131229P.pdf

## 17. No conviction under a state election-deepfake law has been found
**APPROXIMATE (a search finding).** Found: an Oregon investigation (no outcome), a Texas complaint to a
county sheriff (receipt confirmed only), civil suits, cease-and-desist letters. Prosecutors' and election
agencies' records were not searched. Said as "we found no conviction", never "there has never been one".
- https://www.opb.org/article/2026/07/09/artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-political-advertisements-oregon/

## 26. The FCC's 2024 proposal for AI disclosure in broadcast political ads was never adopted
**CONFIRMED.** Proposed July 2024; the docket shows no order and no closure; it is not on the FCC's December

## 27. A plain "AI-generated" label cut belief by 0.14 SD and had no significant effect on sharing
**CONFIRMED.** Wittenberg et al., *PNAS Nexus* 2025, n = 7,579; "Manipulated" −0.27 SD and "False" −0.23 SD on
belief. Stated intentions about images, not votes.
- https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/6/pgaf170/8151894

## 28. Warning people about deepfakes made them doubt real videos
**CONFIRMED.** Ternovski, Kalla & Aronow (2022), *Journal of Online Trust and Safety* — about informing people
deepfakes exist, not about a label on a specific video.
- https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/28

## 29. Of 78 election deepfakes, half had no deceptive intent
**CORRECTED (attribution).** Kapoor & Narayanan's analysis, on the Knight First Amendment Institute's blog,
of WIRED's worldwide tracker: 39 of 78 with no deceptive intent; the authors note the database may be
incomplete. Not a joint WIRED/Knight review, and not US-only.
- https://knightcolumbia.org/blog/we-looked-at-78-election-deepfakes-political-misinformation-is-not-an-ai-problem

## 30. "No conclusive evidence" deepfakes changed an election result
**CORRECTED (wording).** The Turing Institute's reports say they found "no evidence" that AI-enabled content
"measurably altered an election result", and attribute the gap to "insufficient data on the impact … on
real-world voter behaviour". The episode uses their words.
- https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-enabled-influence-operations-safeguarding-future-elections
- https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-enabled-influence-operations-threat-analysis-2024-uk-and-european-elections

## 31. Platforms: AI disclosure on political ads; Meta blocks new political ads in the final week; TikTok bans paid political ads
**CONFIRMED, without dates.** Meta's own page confirms the disclosure rule and the final-week block but gives
no dates (27 Oct–3 Nov is not on it; not used). Google requires an "altered or synthetic content" disclosure
on election ads; YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered content; TikTok bans paid political
ads and requires AI labels. X's policy page is generic and dated April 2025.
- https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/
- https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6014595?hl=en
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en
- https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity
- https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity

## 32. "Every federal court that has weighed one of these laws against free speech has blocked it"
**CORRECTED (wording).** The first draft said every federal court that "reached" these laws blocked them.
Minnesota's federal courts reached its law and declined to block it — on standing and delay, without
weighing it against the First Amendment (entry 13). The claim holds only for the courts that weighed the
laws on free-speech grounds: California (#10), Hawaii (#12) and Montana (#1). The script and brief now say so.
